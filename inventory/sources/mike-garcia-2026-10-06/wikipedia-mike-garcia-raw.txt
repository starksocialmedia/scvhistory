'''Michael Joseph Garcia<ref>{{cite web |title=Mike Garcia |url=https://www.legistorm.com/search/search.html?textsearch=Mike+Garcia |access-date=January 13, 2021}}</ref>''' (born April 24, 1976)<ref>{{cite web |title=GARCIA, Mike 1976 – |url=https://bioguide.congress.gov/search/bio/G000061 |publisher=Biographical Directory of the United States Congress |access-date=8 October 2024}}</ref> is an American politician and former [[United States Navy]] pilot who served as the [[United States House of Representatives|U.S. representative]] for [[California's 27th congressional district]] from 2020 to 2025. A [[Republican Party (United States)|Republican]], he was first elected in a [[2020 California's 25th congressional district special election|May 2020 special election]] and went on to win a full term in the [[2020 United States House of Representatives elections in California|general election]].

Garcia was educated at the [[United States Naval Academy]], and [[Georgetown University]]. He served in the [[United States Navy|U.S. Navy]] from 1998 to 2012, participating in multiple combat missions during the [[Iraq War]]. Following his active duty in the Navy he worked at [[Raytheon Intelligence & Space]].

Garcia has faced and defeated [[Democratic Party (United States)|Democratic]] nominee [[Christy Smith (politician)|Christy Smith]] in all three of his successful elections: the 2020 special election held to fill the vacancy caused by Representative [[Katie Hill]]'s resignation; the [[2020 United States House of Representatives elections in California#District 25|2020 general election]], when he won his first full term; and the [[2022 United States House of Representatives elections in California#District 27|2022 general election]], when he won his second term. Garcia had initially represented [[California's 25th congressional district]], but the district was renumbered as the [[California's 27th congressional district|27th district]] following the [[2020 United States redistricting cycle|2020 redistricting cycle]].

Garcia ran for re-election to a third full term in 2024 and was defeated in the general election by Democratic challenger [[George T. Whitesides]].

==Early life and education==
Mike Garcia was born on April 24, 1976, in [[Granada Hills, California]], a suburb of Los Angeles, to parents who had immigrated from [[Mexico]] in 1959.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.electmikegarcia.com/who-is-mike-garcia-chapter-1/ | title=Who is Mike Garcia? ==Military and civilian career==
Garcia was commissioned an ensign in the [[United States Navy]] in May 1998 following his graduation from the U.S. Naval Academy and was sent to flight school at [[Naval Air Station Pensacola]]. He was deployed as an [[McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet|F/A-18]] aviator from the [[USS Nimitz|USS ''Nimitz'']]. During the [[2003 invasion of Iraq]], he participated in over 30 combat missions.<ref>{{Cite news |url=https://www.electmikegarcia.com/meet-mike/ |title=Mike Garcia's Biography |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200520010204/https://www.electmikegarcia.com/meet-mike/ |archive-date=May 20, 2020 |url-status=live}}</ref> Garcia worked as a [[business development manager]] at [[Raytheon Intelligence, Information and Services]], one of four business segments of [[Raytheon]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.ainonline.com/aviation-news/defense/2014-02-12/raytheon-stealth-means-more-low-rcs|title=Raytheon: Stealth Means More Than Low RCS|first=Chris|last=Pocock|website=Aviation International News|access-date=2020-05-20|archive-date=2020-07-11|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200711001004/https://www.ainonline.com/aviation-news/defense/2014-02-12/raytheon-stealth-means-more-low-rcs|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Tenure===
Garcia was sworn into office on May 19, 2020.<ref>{{cite news|last=Khalil|first=Joe|date=May 19, 2020|url=https://whnt.com/news/washington-dc-bureau/republican-mike-garcia-sworn-in-as-californias-newest-member-of-congress/amp/|title=Republican Mike Garcia sworn in as California's newest member of Congress|work=[[WHNT-TV|WHNT Channel 19]]|location=[[Huntsville, Alabama]]|access-date=May 20, 2020}}</ref>

In January 2021, he [[Attempts to overturn the 2020 United States presidential election|voted to object]] to Congress's [[2021 United States Electoral College vote count|certification of the states' Electoral College votes]].<ref>{{cite news|url= https://www.washingtonpost.com/graphics/2021/politics/congress-electoral-college-count-tracker/|title= How members of Congress voted on counting the electoral college vote|newspaper= [[The Washington Post]]|access-date= 2024-11-13|archive-date= 2021-01-08|archive-url= https://archive.today/20210108070551/https://www.washingtonpost.com/graphics/2021/politics/congress-electoral-college-count-tracker/|url-status= live}}</ref>

On October 5, 2023, Garcia signed a letter to the [[United States House Committee on Agriculture|House Agriculture Committee]] along with 15 House Republicans opposing the inclusion of the Ending Agricultural Trade Suppression (EATS) Act in the 2023 [[United States farm bill|farm bill]]. The EATS Act, introduced in response to the California farm [[animal welfare]] law [[2018 California Proposition 12|Proposition 12]], would have overturned state and local [[animal welfare]] laws restricting the sale of agricultural goods from animals raised in [[battery cage]]s, [[gestation crate]]s, and [[Veal#Veal crates|veal crates]]. The letter argued that the legislation would undermine [[states' rights]] and cede control over U.S. agricultural policy to the Chinese-owned pork producer [[WH Group]] and its subsidiary [[Smithfield Foods]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=2025-07-18 |title=GOP Conservatives Oppose EATS Act Over Chinese Influence in US Pork Industry |url=https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/news/article/2024/03/08/gop-conservatives-oppose-eats-act-us |access-date=2025-07-18 |website=DTN Progressive Farmer |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=More lawmakers push to kill EATS Act |url=https://www.farmprogress.com/farm-policy/more-lawmakers-push-to-kill-eats-act- |access-date=2025-07-18 |website=www.farmprogress.com |language=en}}</ref>

In March 2024, Garcia was one of 10 House Republicans who signed a letter to the [[United States House Committee on Agriculture|House Agriculture Committee]] opposing the inclusion of the Ending Agriculture Trade Suppression (EATS) Act in the 2024 [[United States farm bill|farm bill]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=ICYMI: Rep. Anna Paulina Luna Leads in Letter Fighting Against Foreign Control of the U.S. Pork Industry - Anna Paulina Luna |url=https://luna.house.gov/posts/icymi-rep-anna-paulina-luna-leads-in-letter-fighting-against-foreign-control-of-the-u-s-pork-industry |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240414004611/https://luna.house.gov/posts/icymi-rep-anna-paulina-luna-leads-in-letter-fighting-against-foreign-control-of-the-u-s-pork-industry |archive-date=2024-04-14 |access-date=2025-07-18 |website=Anna Paulina Luna |language=en}}</ref>

=== Certification of the 2020 election and Trump's second impeachment ===
On January{{nbsp}}4, 2021, Garcia announced his intention to reject some states' electoral votes based on claims of fraud.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Jalonick|first=Lisa Mascaro and Mary Clare|date=4 January 2021|title=Rep. Garcia joins challenge to Electoral College as other Republicans condemn 'scheme'|url=https://www.vcstar.com/story/news/2021/01/04/representative-mike-garcia-challenge-electoral-college-others-gop-condemn-effort/4128616001/|access-date=2021-11-23|website=[[Ventura County Star]]|language=en-US}}</ref> On January 6, he objected to Congress's [[2021 United States Electoral College vote count|certification of the states' Electoral College votes]].<ref>{{Cite news|last1=Yourish|first1=Karen|last2=Buchanan|first2=Larry|last3=Lu|first3=Denise|date=2021-01-07|title=The 147 Republicans Who Voted to Overturn Election Results|language=en-US|work=The New York Times|url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2021/01/07/us/elections/electoral-college-biden-objectors.html|access-date=2021-11-23|issn=0362-4331|archive-date=2021-01-07|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210107065429/https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2021/01/07/us/elections/electoral-college-biden-objectors.html|url-status=live}}</ref> He later acknowledged [[Joe Biden]]'s win and said the "election process has run its course".<ref name=":0" />

Garcia voted against [[Second impeachment of Donald Trump|Donald Trump's second impeachment]] after the [[2021 storming of the United States Capitol|2021 attack on the United States Capitol]].<ref>{{Cite web|last=Brown|first=Michael|date=2021-01-13|title=Congressman Garcia Votes Against Impeachment Of President Trump|url=https://www.hometownstation.com/santa-clarita-news/politics/congressman-garcia-votes-against-impeachment-of-president-trump-362574|access-date=2021-03-11|website=Hometown Station {{!}} KHTS FM 98.1 & AM 1220|language=en-US}}</ref>
