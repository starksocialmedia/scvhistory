Subject Matter of Conference/Seminar:
Review of State initiatives/programs of relevance to the Santa Clarita Valley

Speakers and Persons of Interest in Attendance:
State Senator Scott Wilk, Assemblypersons Valladares and Lackey and
various individuals involved with state government. Audience attendees
included approximately 30 persons representing various local non-profits
and businesses.

Points of Interest:
Although a few of the speakers who originally planned to take part in were
sidelined by COVID, the speakers who replaced them were engaging. One
presenter would couldn't attend was Wade Crowfoot, the Secretary of
Natural Resources. A sample of the speakers and a summary of their
comments are listed below.

H.D. Palmer is the current Deputy Director for Governmental External
Affairs at the Department of Finance and serves as the spokesperson for
Governor Gavin Newsom's administration on budget and fiscal issues. He
noted that the first waves of the COVID-19 pandemic led to exponential
growth in unemployment.

California has created a million jobs in the last 12 months Palmer said. That
represents approximately 90% of the jobs lost and the hope is that we will
have regained all of those remaining lost jobs by the fall of this year. He
noted that this year's budget will have a surplus of nearly $100 billion. There
is a requirement for lawmakers and the Governor to reach a budget
agreement before the June 15 constitutional deadline to pass a balanced
budget.

The next speaker, Mike Jacob, Vice President of the General Counsel of
the Pacific Merchants Shipping Association, discussed the ongoing supply
chain crisis clogging up California industrial ports. As the twin ports of Los
Angeles and Long Beach carry 40 percent of the entire national import
supply of non/ liquid goods, Californians are on the front line of the supply
issue, with piles of shipping containers languishing on cargo ships or in the
ports themselves. There have been a reduced number of ships parked
offshore since the height of the pandemic, but there are still more than there
would be if the supply chain issues were resolved.

Jennifer Pierre, General Manager of the California State Water Contractors,
began by noting the impacts to water supply due to the current drought and
potential impacts from climatic change. She said that water suppliers are
bracing for increased aridity in the west and that the Delta Conveyance
Project is a climate change adaptation that will allow us to take surplus
water in wet years. It would serve to protect the State Water Project (SWP)
from sea level rise and seismic failure in the Sacramento/San Joaquin
Delta and that the SWP "can't go on without it". She urged everyone to tell
the story of water leaders and what needs to be done, namely, conveying
why investments are required to do all supply enhancements together.

Jason Elliot, Senior Counselor to the Governor on Homelessness gave a
presentation on the state's efforts to combat homelessness. The issue of
mental health is closely tied to homelessness, with approximately one~
quarter of California's approximately 161,000 homeless persons
experiencing severe mental illness, according to a 2020 report by the U.S.
Department of Housing and Urban Development. Elliot noted that the true
success in terms of combating homelessness comes through collaboration
with local efforts, such as the City of Santa Clarita and LA. County's
support of Bridge to Home, the homeless shelter in the Santa Clarita Valley.

Sean Wallentine, Vice President of Government Affairs for the California
Independent Petroleum Association gave a presentation regarding the status
of oil and gas production in the state. He stated that current California
demand is approximately 1.8 million barrels of oil per day of which
producers in the state supply 400,000 barrels though they are capable of
supplying significantly more but restrictions from the state prevent them
from doing so. The major of the remainder comes from Ecuador, and results
in significant loss of rainforest habitat and increased carbon emissions due
to the need to ship it to California ports. He noted that both Los Angeles
and Ventura counties ( and others) have current efforts to ban oil
production all together within their jurisdictions.

Deborah Gonzalez, Vice President of Government Affairs and Chief Data
Officer for the Public Policy Institute of California, a nonprofit, nonpartisan
think tank offered insight into the numbers behind policymaking, as well as
trends in representative voting records and voter's priorities. She passed out
the latest PPIC survey on state and national issues. The survey results
showed that the top issues for Californians (in order) were: 1)

Jobs/economy/inflation; 2) Housing costs/availability; 3) Homelessness; 4)
Gasoline/oil prices; and, 5) Water/water availability/drought.

Santa Clarita's representatives, Assemblywoman Suzette Martinez;
Valladares, R;Santa Clarita, and Assemblyman Tom Lackey, R;Palmdale
made the last presentation. Valladares shared that her focus on making
bipartisan policies allowed her to be an effective representative for our
Santa Clarita Valley at the state level. Assemblyman Lackey echoed
Valladares' sentiment about the importance of promoting local
understanding of state government, as well believing in being a part of an
active government.

Background
The California Special Districts Association (CSDA) is a non-profit association that
promotes good governance and services through professional development and
advocacy for independent special districts. SCV Water is a special district that is a
member of CSDA.
CSDA has 24 chapters throughout California, the newest of which is the Special
Districts Association of North Los Angeles County (SDANLAC) with two representatives
from SCV Water serving on the Executive Board.
Purpose of meeting
The purpose of this meeting was to get updates from the CSDA Senior Public Affairs
Field Coordinator, Chris Palmer, and to finalize the plans for the upcoming general
membership meeting of the SDANLAC which will take place at 11 :30 on June 22, 2022
at the Chimbole Cultural Center in Palmdale.
