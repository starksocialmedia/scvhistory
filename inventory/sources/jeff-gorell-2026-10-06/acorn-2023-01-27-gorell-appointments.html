Gorell makes new appointments for 2nd District

Camarillo resident joins planning board
By wpeditor
on January 28, 2023

By Victoria Talbot
vtalbot@theacorn.com

ON THE DAIS—Ventura County Supervisor Jeff Gorell speaks during his first Board of Supervisors meeting at the Ventura County Government Center earlier this month. MICHAEL COONS/Acorn Newspapers

Jeff Gorell wasted little time making his first appointments to various county seats in a meeting of the Ventura County Board of Supervisors on Jan. 24.

The new District 2 supervisor had a number of vacancies to fill, including three seats on the Casa Conejo Municipal Advisory Council.

Two of the open seats were left by Richard Gaz and Wendy Zimmerman, who had been appointed in 2019 by Gorell’s predecessor, Linda Parks. Their terms expired Jan. 1. The other open seat, which had been vacant, was for an alternate.

Unlike in Oak Park, members of the Casa Conejo MAC voted to have the District 2 Supervisor appoint them rather than be elected. The Casa Conejo MAC was formed by Parks.

To fill the seats, Gorell chose three longtime Casa Conejo residents whose volunteer activities revolve around family and schools.

“These individuals are excited and interested in playing a bigger role in their community through public service,” Gorell said, “and we are excited to have them.”

Daiva McBride is a lifelong Ventura County resident who, with her husband, raised two children during her 28-year Casa Conejo residency. She has been involved in her children’s schools, their sports activities, Girl Scouts and the local school board.

Debra Hanna, who has lived in Casa Conejo for 33 years, has two daughters who were raised in the community. She has been active in Girl Scouts, Newbury Park Steelers Cheerleading and Newbury Park softball, and she helped establish the inclusive Sparkles cheerleading program at Newbury Park, Westlake and Thousand Oaks high schools. The program is hosting athletes for the 2015 Special Olympics.

Susan Turgeon, who will serve as the alternate, has a been a Casa Conejo resident for 26 of the 46 years she has lived in the county. She has raised three children with her husband, and they own a small business in Newbury Park.

She volunteers at her church and the Oxnard Rescue Mission, and helps out with her children’s extracurricular activities. Her family also hosts international students for Cal Lutheran University and fosters dogs for a local rescue organization.

Claudia Bill- de la Peña, Gorell’s opponent in the race for supervisor, used social media to comment on Gorell’s appointments, calling them a “shake-up,” and saying that “someone” was “cashing in” on their “campaign investment.”

Bill-de la Peña, asked to comment, simply said, “My tweet speaks for itself.”

She did not respond when asked for clarification.

Municipal advisory councils provide input to the Board of Supervisors, and one of the most contentious issues for the Casa Conejo council has been the debate over whether to develop a 37-acre parcel on Borchard Road that skirts the 101 Freeway. Casa Conejo residents live nearby in an unincorporated island of homes between Old Conejo and Borchard roads.

Bill-de la Peña ran her campaign largely on the coattails of Parks, who termed out Jan. 1 after 20 years as supervisor. Owing to their shared slow- or no-growth environmental agenda, both were dead set against the Moradian family’s efforts to develop the property.

Parks and the Casa Conejo MAC recently delivered a letter to the Thousand Oaks City Council objecting to any form of development on the property and recommending that it be allowed to return to wetland status.

Gorell made several other appointments as well.

He chose Herb Gooch, a retired Cal Lutheran University political science professor, to sit on the county’s campaign ethics finance commission. Gooch previously served on the commission from 2003 to 2005.

Charles Sandlin, an insurance broker who has lived in Camarillo for 43 years, was appointed to the county planning commission. A longtime youth wrestling coach, he’s been a board member of both the Camarillo Chamber of Commerce and the Camarillo Boys & Girls Club. He also worked as chief operating officer of Roadrunner Shuttle and Limousine Service

Dianne McKay, appointed to the behavioral health advisory board, was raised in Thousand Oaks and returned in 1986 to raise her four children in east Ventura County. As a founding board member of Safe Passage Youth Foundation and the Ventura County Family Justice Center, she helps victims of mental health neglect or abuse receive needed services. She’s also worked with Casa Pacifica, Food Share and the United Way of Ventura County. From 2010 to 2022, she served as a Ventura County Community College District trustee.

Johanna Jones, a licensed marriage and family therapist in Thousand Oaks, was appointed to the Children and Family First Commission. The T.O. native has worked at Interface Children and Family Services as a community trainer and supported foster youth through Aspiranet, one of California’s largest social services organizations, She is a co-founder of the local activist group 805Resistance, a nonprofit dedicated to creating positive change in the Conejo Valley.

“My staff and I worked diligently to find appointees who would best serve the community that reflects a spirit of volunteerism and passion for public service,” Gorell said.
