Supervisor Gorell elected board chair; Supervisor Lopez elected vice chair

District 2 Supervisor Jeff Gorell, who represents areas of Thousand Oaks and Camarillo, was elected unanimously on Jan. 13 as chair of the Ventura County Board of Supervisors, during its first meeting of the year.

District 5 Supervisor Vianey Lopez, who represents areas of Oxnard and Port Hueneme, was elected unanimously as vice chair.

Both will serve in the roles for one-year terms.

Gorell, who previously served as vice chair, is taking over for District 4 Supervisor Janice Parvin, who represents areas of Moorpark and Simi Valley, and who served in the role for 2025.

“I’m excited at the opportunity to lead this board and to represent our county,” Gorell said at the meeting. “I step into big shoes that were recently held by our Supervisor Janice Parvin; you were solid as a rock for us for the last year.”

The board is the county’s main governing body, and oversees and makes crucial decisions on county government, including laws and ordinances, the county’s $3.23 billion budget, and the more than 10,000 employees in the County Sheriff’s Office, Health Care Agency, Public Works, Planning Division and other departments.

One of Gorell’s first key announcements as chair was the approval of extending the time for general public comment for non-agenda items from two minutes to three minutes, based on his recommendation.

“Ventura County is an extraordinary place to call home — defined by breathtaking beauty from the mountains to the coast, recognized as one of the safest large counties in California, and strengthened by a growing and innovative economy, with our life-saving biotech sector at the forefront,” he said in a statement. “Our county’s strong fiscal footing and dedicated public servants allow us to continue delivering essential services while planning responsibly for the future.”

Lopez was first appointed to the board by Gov. Gavin Newsom in October 2022, after Supervisor Carmen Ramirez died after being hit by a truck while walking in downtown Oxnard during her first term. Lopez was elected to the seat in 2025.

“I look forward to continuing to work with all of you this coming year in our respective capacities,” Lopez said during the meeting.

Lopez also volunteered to serve on the Ventura County Continuum of Care Alliance Board for a two-year term.
