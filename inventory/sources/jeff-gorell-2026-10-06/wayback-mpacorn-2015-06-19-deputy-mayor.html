Gorell new deputy mayor of Los Angeles

By Stephanie Sumell
ssumell@theacorn.com

Gorell Former Assemblymember Jeff Gorell will soon take over a new post as a leader of security for the second largest city in the U.S.

Ending speculation that he might run for the Ventura County Board of Supervisors, Gorell, 44, was appointed deputy mayor for homeland security and public safety for the City of Los Angeles earlier this month.

The naval reserve officer and former county prosecutor was hand-picked by Los Angeles Mayor Eric Garcetti. The two served together in the Navy Reserves.

Gorell will take his new post July 6.

“It’s a deep honor,” Gorell said. “In the era in which we live now, with heightened homeland security challenges abroad and domestically, as well as the challenges of increased crime rates in the state and the degrading relationship between the community and law enforcement, it is a significant challenge (that will) test my abilities as a communicator and consensus builder.”

The one-time deputy district attorney will have a wide range of responsibilities in his new role. He will oversee 10,000 police officers and be responsible for outfitting all patrol officers and cars with video cameras.

Gorell will also work to improve technology at the city fire department and stabilize buildings that are vulnerable to earthquakes.

A commander in the Navy Reserve who served two tours of duty in Afghanistan, Gorell is expected to work to expand programs that help prevent youths from joining gangs and increase the number of volunteers who respond to domestic abuse incidents.

“The deputy mayor for homeland security and public safety is the mayor’s primary liaison with the Los Angeles Police Department, the Los Angeles Fire Department and the Emergency Management Department,” said Vicki Curry, a spokesperson for the mayor. “The deputy mayor also interacts with community groups and neighborhood councils, coordinates with regional public safety and emergency response partners, and applies for and administers homeland security and public safety grants.”

Gorell, a Camarillo resident, said he is up for the job.

In 2013, Gorell—then an Assembly member representing the 44th District—endorsed Garcetti in his run for mayor. Gorell said they have worked together on creating public safety policies.

“I call him my friend, and it will be great to call him my boss,” Gorell said. “I have a high level of respect and deep admiration for him.”

Garcetti said the new deputy mayor is the right person for the position. “The safety of Los Angeles is my top priority, and I’m honored to have Jeff Gorell join my administration,” Garcetti said in a statement.

Gorell served as a member of the California Assembly for four years. Last November he was defeated by incumbent Julia Brownley in a race to represent Ventura County in Congress.
