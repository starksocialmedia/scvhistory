'''Anthony A. Strickland''' (born February 17, 1970) is an American politician who is a [[California State Senate|California state senator]] representing the [[California's 36th senatorial district|36th district]] since 2025. A member of the [[Republican Party (United States)|Republican Party]], he previously represented the [[California's 19th senatorial district|19th district]] from 2008 to 2012.

He was a member of the [[Huntington Beach]] [[City Council]] from 2022 to 2025 and was Mayor of Huntington Beach from 2022 to 2023. During his tenure as mayor, he was involved in conflicts with the state of California due to his opposition to new housing.

He was also a [[California State Assembly|state assemblyman]] representing the [[California's 37th State Assembly district|37th district]] from 1998 to 2004. He owns a political and business consulting firm.<ref name=OCRegisterMar2023>{{Cite web |date=2023-03-15 |author=Scott Schwebke|title=Huntington Beach mayor, a fierce critic of state housing mandates, lives in affordable housing |url=https://www.ocregister.com/2023/03/15/huntington-beach-mayor-a-fierce-critic-of-state-housing-mandates-lives-in-affordable-housing/ |website=Orange County Register |language=en-US}}</ref> He is the president and CEO of Strong America, an [[advocacy group]] and [[super PAC]].<ref name=Scheibe2016>John Scheibe, [https://www.vcstar.com/story/news/local/communities/moorpark/2016/01/12/tony-strickland-could-face-more-than-100000-in-fppc-fines/88644628/ Tony Strickland could face more than $100,000 in FPPC fines], ''Ventura County Star'' (January 12, 2016).</ref>

==Early life, education, and early political career==
Strickland was born at [[Fort Ord, California]], when his father was stationed there as a soldier in the [[United States Army]].<ref name=2010ProfileVCReporter>{{cite news |date=26 May 2010 |title=Meet the Stricklands |url=https://www.vcreporter.com/news/meet-the-stricklands/article_a1bb1a2d-aad3-5050-9c5d-d33211a2f1dd.html/ |accessdate=2012-08-14 |work=Ventura County Reporter}}</ref> Don Strickland met his wife, Antonie, when he was stationed in Germany. The family moved to [[Ventura County, California|Ventura County]] in 1973. Strickland earned his [[Bachelor of Arts]] in political science at [[Whittier College]], where he earned the Leadership and Service Award and was a [[Richard Nixon|Nixon]] Scholar.

From 1996 to 1997, Strickland served on the staff of [[California State Assembly|Assembly]]man [[Tom McClintock]].

==Political career==

=== California Assembly ===
Strickland was first elected to the Assembly in 1998 with 49% of the vote in a three-way race.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://vote98.sos.ca.gov/Returns/stasm/3700.htm|title=1998 Election Results - California Secretary of State|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20090501163155/http://vote98.sos.ca.gov/Returns/stasm/3700.htm|archivedate=2009-05-01}}</ref>

In 1998, Strickland distributed flyers in his campaign claiming [[Camarillo]] mayor Charlotte Craven endorsed him. Strickland was not endorsed by Craven and the mayor requested that Strickland remove her name from the flyer. With Craven's name removed, only one elected official, Mike Markey, had endorsed Strickland.<ref>{{cite news |author=Timm Herdt |date=1998-03-20 |title=Candidate's campaign irks mayor |newspaper=Ventura County Star |location=Ventura County}}</ref>

In 1998, the Strickland campaign had a complaint filed against it alleging that a mailer sent out by an independent group attacking Strickland's opponent coordinated with the campaign. The complaint was made by the president of the West Valley Republican Club, Michael Chulak. He claimed that the mailer was illegal because it was sent out "at the behest" of the Strickland campaign.<ref>{{cite news |author=Timm Herdt |date=1998-05-30 |title=Complaint alleges Strickland violation |newspaper=Ventura County Star |location=Ventura County}}</ref> The mailer was sent out by a Sacramento-based gun owner's group on Strickland's behalf.<ref>{{cite news |author=Timm Herdt |date=1998-05-31 |title=Campaigns vow no last-minute sleaze tactics |newspaper=Ventura County Star |location=Ventura County}}</ref>

He was re-elected in 2000 with 51% of the vote, again in a three-way race.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://vote2000.sos.ca.gov/Returns/stasm/3700.htm|title=2000 Election Results - California Secretary of State|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20090430192536/http://vote2000.sos.ca.gov/Returns/stasm/3700.htm|archivedate=2009-04-30}}</ref> He was re-elected to a third term with 63.1% of the vote in 2002.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://vote2002.sos.ca.gov/Returns/stasm/3700.htm|title=2000 Election Results - California Secretary of State|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20090501143732/http://vote2002.sos.ca.gov/Returns/stasm/3700.htm|archivedate=2009-05-01}}</ref> [[Term limit]]s prevented Strickland from seeking a fourth term in 2004.<ref name=2010ProfileVCReporter/><ref name=Scheibe2016/> [[Audra Strickland]], his then-wife, decided to run for the seat and won (she was re-elected in 2006 and 2008).<ref name=2010ProfileVCReporter/><ref name=Scheibe2016/>

=== 2006 run for state controller ===
{{Main|2006 California State Controller election}}
In 2006 Strickland ran for [[California State Controller|state controller]]. He won 41% of the vote in a four-way primary, besting state senator [[Abel Maldonado]], his nearest opponent, who received 37.0% of the vote.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://primary2006.sos.ca.gov/Returns/ctl/00.htm|title=2006 Primary Election Results - California Secretary of State|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20080513162001/http://primary2006.sos.ca.gov/Returns/ctl/00.htm|archivedate=2008-05-13}}</ref> Strickland was defeated by [[John Chiang (California politician)|John Chiang]] in the general election by a 50.7%-40.2% margin.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://vote2006.sos.ca.gov/Returns/ctl/00.htm|title=2006 Election Results - California Secretary of State|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20080907145440/http://vote2006.sos.ca.gov/Returns/ctl/00.htm|archivedate=2008-09-07}}</ref>

=== California Senate ===
[[File:Tony Strickland (2).jpg|thumb|Strickland as a [[California State Senator]] in 2014.]]
Strickland ran in California's 19th district against the Democratic nominee, former state assemblywoman [[Hannah-Beth Jackson]]. Senate District 19 includes areas of [[Santa Barbara County, California|Santa Barbara]], [[Ventura County, California|Ventura]], and [[Los Angeles County, California|Los Angeles]] counties. The race was highly competitive and the closest state Senate race in California that year.

The race was also contentious, with critics of Strickland upset at the alleged misrepresentations of his business background. On various campaign literature, the Strickland campaign portrayed Strickland as a vice president of Green Wave Energy Solutions, LLC—even though Strickland has worked in politics his entire adult life,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://mendocoastcurrent.wordpress.com/2008/04/23/green-wave-exec-candidate-selling-himself-green/ |title=Green Wave Exec & Candidate Selling Himself Green|publisher=Mendo Coast Curren |date=2008-04-23 |accessdate=2012-08-14}}</ref> and Green Wave Energy Solutions LLC has not done any business in California.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://kepler.sos.ca.gov/cbs.aspx |title=Business Search - Business Entities - Business Programs |publisher=Kepler.sos.ca.gov |accessdate=2012-08-14 |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20100402014221/http://kepler.sos.ca.gov/cbs.aspx |archivedate=2010-04-02 }}</ref>

Jackson conceded just five days before Strickland was sworn into the Senate. He had won the election by 900 votes. With Strickland's 2008 election to the State Senate and his wife, Audra, simultaneously winning re-election to the State Assembly, the Stricklands became the second husband and wife to serve concurrently in the [[California State Legislature]] (after Senator [[George Runner]] and Assemblywoman [[Sharon Runner]]).

=== 2010 state controller election ===
{{Main|2010 California State Controller election}}
In the June 2010 primary, Strickland won 60% of the vote to gain the Republican nomination for state controller, setting up a rematch of the [[California State Controller election, 2006|2006 controller election]] between Strickland and Chiang, who was unopposed in his bid for the Democratic nomination for re-election as state controller.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/sov/2010-primary/pdf/20-35-sov-summary-primary.pdf|title=Statement of Vote Summary Pages|publisher=[[California Secretary of State]]|date=July 16, 2010|page=20}}</ref>  In the general election, Strickland lost again to Chiang, this time by a wider margin: Chiang received 55% of the vote, while Strickland received 36%.

===Campaign finance penalties===
In December 1999, the [[California Secretary of State|California secretary of state]] sued Strickland for failing to report $116,140 in late contributions, in violation of the California Political Reform Act of 1974. He was fined $6,000.<ref name=2010ProfileVCReporter/>

In April 2010, the [[California Fair Political Practices Commission]] (FPPC) decided that Strickland and Strickland for State Senate, a member of the California State Senate, and his candidate-controlled committee sent out a mass mailing without the name of the committee on the outside of the mailing. The commission levied a $3,000 fine.<ref>{{cite web |date= |title=California Fair Political Practices Commission Press Releases |url=http://www.fppc.ca.gov/press_release.php?event_day=8&event_month=4&event_year=2010 |accessdate=2012-08-14 |publisher=Fppc.ca.gov}}</ref>

In May 2016, Strickland agreed to pay a $40,000 fine to settle a FPPC investigation that determined that he laundered campaign donations during his 2010 run for state controller. The commission found that Strickland had routed campaign contributions through the Ventura County Republican Party and Stanislaus County Republican Central Committee, in an effort to evade state limits on campaign contributions. The Commission said that Strickland did not cooperate with the investigation. Strickland accepted responsibility for eight violations, but said that he did not personally solicit the donations to the party organizations.<ref>Nick Cahill, [https://www.courthousenews.com/calif-republican-fined-for-campaign-laundering/ Calif. Republican Fined for Campaign Laundering], [[Courthouse News Service]] (May 20, 2016).</ref><ref>Taryn Luna, [https://www.sacbee.com/news/politics-government/capitol-alert/article78640762.html Tony Strickland agrees to $40,000 ethics fine], ''Sacramento Bee'' (May 19, 2016).</ref>

=== Huntington Beach city council===
[[File:Tony Strickland (3).jpg|thumb|Strickland as a mayor of [[Huntington Beach, California|Huntington Beach]] in 2022.]]
Strickland was elected to the Huntington Beach city council in November 2022.<ref name=Lafleur>Magnolia Lafleur, [https://www.ocregister.com/2022/12/17/huntington-beachs-new-council-majority-comes-in-with-priorities-including-fighting-state-mandates/ Huntington Beach’s new council majority comes in with priorities, including fighting state mandates], ''Orange County Register'' (December 17, 2022).</ref> Strickland and three other conservative candidates (Grace Van Der Mark, Casey McKeon and Pat Burns) campaigned as a [[Slate (elections)|slate]], and all four won election to the council, forming a new conservative majority.<ref name=Lafleur/><ref name=FryDec2023>Hannah Fry, [https://www.latimes.com/california/story/2023-12-13/huntington-beach-sticking-it-to-woke-california-at-what-cost-to-residents Huntington Beach is sticking it to 'woke' California. Some residents ask at what cost], ''Los Angeles Times'' (December 13, 2023).</ref>

====Term as mayor====
Strickland spent a one-year term as mayor of Huntington Beach. He was sworn in on December 7, 2022.<ref name=Szabo2022>Matt Szabo, [https://www.latimes.com/socal/daily-pilot/news/story/2022-12-07/huntington-beach-city-council-seats-new-conservative-majority-votes-tony-strickland-as-mayor Huntington Beach City Council seats new conservative majority, votes Tony Strickland as mayor], ''Los Angeles Times'' (December 7, 2022).</ref> In its December 2022 vote, the new conservative-majority council chose Strickland as mayor on a 4&ndash;3 vote, with Van Der Mark being elected mayor ''pro tempore '' on a 4&ndash;3 vote.<ref name=Szabo2022/> On December 6, 2023, on a 4&ndash;3 vote, Strickland was succeeded by Van Der Mark, and the ''pro tem'' position went to Burns.<ref name=SzaboNovember2023>Matt Szabo, [https://www.latimes.com/socal/daily-pilot/sports/story/2023-11-30/huntington-beach-holds-state-of-the-city-event Huntington Beach mayor talks about government's 'essential role' during State of the City event], ''Los Angeles Times'' (November 30, 2023).</ref><ref name=Cardine>Sara Cardine, [https://www.latimes.com/socal/daily-pilot/news/story/2023-12-06/huntington-beachs-first-latina-mayor-gracey-van-der-mark-sworn-in-after-4-3-vote Huntington Beach's first Latina mayor, Gracey Van Der Mark, sworn in after 4-3 vote], ''Los Angeles Times'' (December 6, 2023).</ref> In both 2022 and 2023, the council voted 4&ndash;3 to set aside previous council resolutions under which the ''pro tem'' post was granted to the longest-serving councilmember who had not served as mayor in the past four years.<ref name=Szabo2022/><ref name=Cardine/> During the 2023 vote, Strickland made the motion to set aside this previous custom of selecting the ''pro tem'' based on seniority.<ref name=Cardine/>

Strickland remains a member of the city council.<ref name=SzaboNovember2023/>

====Housing issues====
As a city councilmember and as mayor, Strickland staunchly opposed increasing housing supply.<ref name=LATimesMar2023>{{Cite web |date=2023-03-24 |title=Strickland, opponent of state housing mandate, responds to reports he lives in affordable housing |url=https://www.latimes.com/socal/daily-pilot/news/story/2023-03-23/huntington-beach-mayor-vocal-opponent-of-state-housing-mandates-responds-to-reports-he-lives-in-affordable-housing-unit |website=Daily Pilot |language=en-US}}</ref> During his tenure, the city stopped accepting applications for [[accessory dwelling unit]]s, which prompted [[California Attorney General|California attorney general]] [[Rob Bonta]] to sue the city, alleging that the city was violating the [[California HOME Act]] (SB 9), a state law requiring municipalities to allow greater housing supply.<ref name=LATimesMar2023/><ref>{{Cite web |date=2023-03-09 |title=California sues Huntington Beach over failure to follow housing laws in warning to other cities |url=https://www.mercurynews.com/2023/03/09/california-sues-huntington-beach-over-failure-to-follow-housing-laws/|website=The Mercury News |language=en-US}}</ref> The city also sued the state of California, alleging that the state's [[Regional Housing Needs Assessment]] infringed on the city's authority; a federal judge denied the city's request for a temporary restraining order blocking the application of state housing law.<ref name=LATimesMar2023 /> Bonta said that Huntington Beach's suit was a "baseless and obstructionist attempt by the city to defy state housing laws."<ref name=LATimesMar2023 />

Strickland says that he supports affordable housing but Huntington Beach is "95% built out" and he will not support "anything that guts our suburban coastal community."<ref name=OCRegisterMar2023/><ref name=LATimesMar2023/> In 2023, he claimed that "high rise high density urbanization" was being "forced" on the city.<ref name=LATimesMar2023 />

In 2024, Strickland supported a ballot initiative to allow California cities to impose [[Rent regulation|rent control]] on new apartments. Strickland said he did not support rent control as a policy, but would support the initiative because it would enable local governments to impose steep requirements on new housing which would block new housing construction.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2024 |title=Top California Democrats come out against rent control ballot measure |url=https://www.politico.com/news/2024/05/14/california-rent-control-ballot-measure-00157950 |work=Politico}}</ref>

==Career as political consultant==
In October 2015, Strickland announced the formation of Strong America, a [[super PAC]] of which he is president and CEO.<ref name=Scheibe2016/>

==Personal life==
Strickland has one daughter and one son with his first wife, [[Audra Strickland]], whom he married in 1998. The couple divorced in 2015.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://voiceofoc.org/2025/03/how-state-senator-tony-strickland-became-orange-countys-political-lazarus/|title=How State Senator Tony Strickland Became Orange County's Political Lazarus|date=March 11, 2025|first=Noah |last=Biesiada|publisher=[[Voice of OC]]}}</ref>  He married his second wife, Carla Dispalatro Strickland, on February 17, 2019.

=== Properties ===
Strickland and his wife live in a four-bedroom 1,800 square-feet condominium on the west side of Huntington Beach that she purchased for $319,000 in 2000.<ref name=OCRegisterMar2023/><ref name=LATimesMar2023/> The unit is publicly listed as a state mandated affordable housing unit.<ref name=LATimesMar2023/> Given that Strickland lives in affordable housing, his staunch opposition to affordable housing mandates prompted accusations of hypocrisy.<ref name=OCRegisterMar2023/>

Strickland also owns a home in [[Moorpark, California|Moorpark]] in Ventura County.<ref name=OCRegisterMar2023/>
