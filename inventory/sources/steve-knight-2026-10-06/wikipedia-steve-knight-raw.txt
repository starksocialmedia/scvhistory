'''Stephen Thomas Knight''' (born December 17, 1966) is an American politician, military veteran and former police officer. A member of the [[Republican Party (United States)|Republican Party]], he served as the [[United States House of Representatives|U.S. representative]] for [[California's 25th congressional district]] from 2015 to 2019. Knight previously represented [[California's 21st State Senate district]] from 2012 to 2015 and [[California's 36th State Assembly district]] from 2008 through 2012; he also served as Assistant Minority Leader in the [[California State Assembly]] from 2010 until 2012.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Smith |first1=Perry |title=Speculation Begins For Steve Knight's State Senate Seat |url=http://www.hometownstation.com/santa-clarita-latest-news/speculation-begins-for-steve-knights-state-senate-seat-93233 |access-date=February 17, 2016 |publisher=KHTS |date=November 10, 2014}}</ref>

Knight served in the U.S. Army from 1985 to 1993 and 18 years with the [[Los Angeles Police Department]] (LAPD). He was elected to the [[Palmdale, California|Palmdale]] [[City council|City Council]] from December 5, 2005, to December 1, 2008, preceded by Enrique Sanchez Lopez and succeeded by Laura Bettencourt. He lost his reelection bid in [[2018 United States House of Representatives elections in California|2018]] to [[Democratic Party (United States)|Democrat]] [[Katie Hill]]. After Hill resigned the following year, he ran in the [[2020 California's 25th congressional district special election|2020 special election]] to complete her term, as well as the [[2020 United States House of Representatives elections in California|concurrent election]] for the next term, but he did not advance past the primary in either election.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://steveknight.org/representative-steve-knight-announces-bid-for-congress/|title=Representative Steve Knight Announces Bid For Congress -|date=November 9, 2019}}</ref>

==Early life, military service, and private career==
Knight was born at [[Edwards Air Force Base]] in [[Antelope Valley, California]], in 1966.<ref>{{cite web |title=KNIGHT profile |url=http://bioguide.congress.gov/scripts/biodisplay.pl?index=K000387 |publisher=Biographical Directory of the United States Congress |access-date=February 17, 2016}}</ref> After graduating from [[Palmdale High School]], he served in the U.S. Army (1985–87) as a tracked vehicle systems mechanic in [[Friedberg, Germany]].<ref>{{cite news |title=New Members Guide 2015 |url=http://thehill.com/new-members-guide-2014/223672-rep-elect-steve-knight-r-calif-25 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141127235955/http://thehill.com/new-members-guide-2014/223672-rep-elect-steve-knight-r-calif-25 |url-status=dead |archive-date=November 27, 2014 |access-date=17 February 2016 |work=The Hill}}</ref> When his tour ended, he served in the Army Reserve until 1993.<ref>{{cite news |date=December 1, 2014 |title=New Members of the 114th Congress Who Have Served in the Military |url=http://www.ebmpubs.com/ECN_pdfs/ecn1214_News.pdf |newspaper=Exchange and Commissary News |publisher=Executive Business Media |location=Westbury, NY |page=11}}</ref> In 2006, Knight received an [[Associate of Arts]] (AA) from [[Antelope Valley College]].<ref name=rollcall>{{cite web |url=http://media.cq.com/members/53674?rc=1|title=House of Representatives Member Profile; Rep. Steve Knight |date=September 8, 2014 |website=RollCall.com |publisher=CQ-Roll Call, Inc. |location=Washington, D.C.}}</ref>

Knight served for 18 years with the [[Los Angeles Police Department]], where he was selected to serve on the [[Community Resources Against Street Hoodlums]] (CRASH) team.<ref>{{cite web |title=Steve Knight (R) - District 21 |url=http://archive.senate.ca.gov/content/steve-knight-r-district-21 |publisher=California State Senate |access-date=February 17, 2016}}</ref> He served on the [[Palmdale, California|Palmdale City Council]] and represented the north Los Angeles County area in both the [[California State Assembly]] and the [[California State Senate]].<ref name="latimes">{{cite news|last1=Panzar|first1=Javier|title=Democratic lawyer announcing challenge to GOP Rep. Steve Knight|url=https://www.latimes.com/politics/la-pol-ca-bryan-caforio-steve-knight-20151210-story.html|access-date=February 17, 2016|work=Los Angeles Times|date=December 10, 2015}}</ref> [[File:Steve Knight - LA County Sheriff Dept.jpg|thumb|Steve Knight speaks with members of the Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department]]

==California State Legislature==
Knight was elected to the California State Assembly in November 2008, succeeding [[Sharon Runner]]. From 2010 to 2012, he was the Assistant Minority Leader of the California State Assembly.<ref>{{cite web |title=Steve Knight's Biography |url=https://votesmart.org/candidate/biography/71098/steve-knight#.VsTXFvHvOac |publisher=Project Vote Smart |access-date=17 February 2016}}</ref> He was elected to the California State Senate in 2012, in which he represented the 21st District until 2014. His father, the late [[William J. Knight|William J. "Pete" Knight]], served as Republican state senator in the Antelope Valley.<ref name=latimes/> Knight's brother David is gay, and he had a gay paternal uncle who died of AIDS.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1996-09-11-me-42738-story.html |title=Foe of Gay Marriages Says His Son Is Homosexual |last=Hill-Holtzman |first=Nancy |date=11 September 1996 |website=Los Angeles Times}}</ref>

In the State Assembly, Knight was Vice Chairman of the Natural Resources and Public Safety Committee and served on the Aerospace; Local Government; Utilities and Commerce; and Rules Committees.<ref>{{cite news |title=Our View: Knight for state Senate |url=http://www.signalscv.com/m/archives/77155/ |access-date=February 17, 2016 |publisher=Santa Clarita Valley Signal |date=October 2, 2012}}</ref>

In September 2014, California Governor [[Jerry Brown]] signed a bill introduced by Knight that aimed to protect California's disabled veterans by fixing an issue with property tax exemptions.<ref>{{cite news |title=Gov. Brown passes bill for disabled veterans |url=http://www.vvdailypress.com/article/20140930/NEWS/140939994 |access-date=February 17, 2016 |publisher=Daily Press |date=September 30, 2014}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title=Knight Measures To Help Veterans, And Protect The Disabled Clear The Senate |url=http://highdesertdaily.com/2014/05/knight-measures-veterans-protect-disabled-clear-senate |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160224104745/http://highdesertdaily.com/2014/05/knight-measures-veterans-protect-disabled-clear-senate/ |url-status=usurped |archive-date=February 24, 2016 |access-date=February 17, 2016 |publisher=High Desert Daily |date=May 28, 2014}}</ref>

==U.S. House of Representatives==
===Elections===

==== 2014 ====
{{main|2014 United States House of Representatives elections in California#District 25}}

Knight focused his 2014 campaign on jobs and education.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Simon|first1=Melissa|title=Strickland, Knight focus on jobs, education|url=http://www.simivalleyacorn.com/news/2014-10-24/Community/Strickland_Knight_focus_on_jobs_education.html|access-date=17 February 2016|publisher=Simi Valley Acorn|date=October 24, 2014}}</ref> The ''[[Los Angeles Daily News]]'' endorsed Knight in the 2014 primary election.<ref>{{cite news |title=Endorsement: Steve Knight for Congress in California's 25th District|url=http://www.dailynews.com/opinion/20140508/endorsement-steve-knight-for-congress-in-californias-25th-district|access-date=17 February 2016|publisher=Los Angeles Daily News|date=May 8, 2014}}</ref>

Due to California's [[Nonpartisan blanket primary|blanket primary]] system, Knight faced fellow Republican [[Tony Strickland]] in the November 4, 2014, general election. Knight defeated Strickland with 53% of the vote.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Winburn|first1=Jim|title=2014 General Election results of interest to Antelope Valley voters|url=http://theavtimes.com/2014/11/05/2014-general-election-results-of-interest-to-antelope-valley-voters|access-date=February 17, 2016|publisher=The Antelope Valley Times|date=November 5, 2014}}</ref>

In April 2015, Knight had a verbal altercation with a protester. After the protester made physical contact by forcefully gripping Knight's hand and patting him on the shoulder, Knight told him, "I'll drop your ass", but later apologized.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/the-fix/wp/2015/04/21/california-rep-steve-knight-tells-a-protestor-ill-drop-your-ass|title=California Rep. Steve Knight tells a protester, "I'll drop your ass"|date=April 21, 2015|newspaper=The Washington Post|last1=Bump|first1=Philip|access-date=February 17, 2016}}</ref>

The [[Aliso Canyon gas leak|Aliso Canyon Gas Leak]] from an underground [[natural gas]] storage facility owned by the [[Southern California Gas Company]] (SoCalGas), a subsidiary of [[Sempra Energy]] occurred in Knight's district and became public in October 2015. Knight was criticized by some constituents for his reaction to the leak. He did not visit the [[Porter Ranch]] gas leak site until January 8, 2016, saying that he did not want to politicize the problem. Knight declined to co-sign a request for an investigation by the [[United States Environmental Protection Agency|Environmental Protection Agency]] (EPA) with Representative [[Brad Sherman]] of the neighboring Congressional district. He wrote in an op-ed that EPA involvement would "hinder the ongoing process".<ref>{{cite news|title=As Porter Ranch Gas Leak Lingers, Candidates Smell a Political Opportunity|url=https://www.latimes.com/local/california/la-me-0118-porter-ranch-pols-20160118-story.html|newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=January 18, 2016|access-date=March 16, 2017|first=Javier|last=Panzar}}</ref> Knight said in December 2015 that he was confident that SoCalGas was “working on this as diligently as they can.”<ref>[https://www.nytimes.com/2016/04/03/magazine/the-invisible-catastrophe.html "The Invisible Catastrophe"], ''New York Times'', March 31, 2016.</ref>

==== 2016 ====
{{main|2016 United States House of Representatives elections in California#District 25}}

In May 2015, the [[National Republican Congressional Committee]] named Knight to its Patriot Program. The program gives added attention by the party to reelection campaigns in districts most likely to switch to Democratic representation in upcoming elections.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Merl|first1=Jean|title=Republicans add Californians to list of its vulnerable House members|url=https://www.latimes.com/local/political/la-me-pc-knight-denham-20150501-story.html|access-date=February 17, 2016|work=Los Angeles Times|date=May 1, 2015}}</ref>

In June 2016, analyst David Wasserman moved Knight's district into the "toss-up" category, due to the negative influence of Republican presidential nominee Donald Trump on down-ticket races and the growth in Democratic voter registration in the 25th Congressional District.<ref>[https://www.latimes.com/politics/la-pol-sac-essential-politics-is-rep-steve-knight-california-s-most-1465608040-htmlstory.html "Is Rep. Steve Knight California's most endangered incumbent?"], ''L.A. Times'', June 30, 2016.</ref> In addition to Knight, two Democrats and one other Republican filed to run in the district's blanket primary, which took place on June 7, 2016.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Wenner|first1=Gretchen|title=Knight faces three primary challengers in 25th congressional district|url=http://www.vcstar.com/news/local/simi-valley/knight-faces-three-primary-challengers-in-25th-congressional-district-30de50c3-4f47-74fd-e053-010000-377049401.html |access-date=April 29, 2016|publisher=Ventura County Star|date=April 25, 2016}}</ref> Knight and Democrat Bryan Caforio advanced to the general election.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Daly|first1=Krista|title=It's Knight, Caforio in November election for 25th Congressional District|url=http://www.signalscv.com/section/36/article/153095|access-date=June 8, 2016|publisher=Santa Clarita Valley Signal|date=June 8, 2016}}</ref>

Knight was endorsed by the ''[[Los Angeles Daily News]]''.<ref>{{cite news|title=Re-elect Steve Knight to Congress in 25th District: Endorsement|url=http://www.dailynews.com/opinion/20160930/re-elect-steve-knight-to-congress-in-25th-district-endorsement|access-date=October 6, 2016|publisher=Los Angeles Daily News|date=September 30, 2016}}</ref> He was also endorsed by Lou Vince, an L.A. police lieutenant and Democrat who finished third (behind Knight and Bryan Caforio) in the 25th district's June 2016 primary.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Panzar|first1=Javier|title=Democrat who lost in the primary says he will vote for Republican Rep. Steve Knight in heated House race|url=https://www.latimes.com/politics/essential/la-pol-sac-essential-politics-updates-democrat-who-lost-in-the-primary-says-1475190691-htmlstory.html|access-date=October 6, 2016|work=Los Angeles Times|date=September 30, 2016}}</ref>

In October 2016, the Ventura County Star endorsed Knight's re-election, praising his "record of service to the residents" and saying that he "showed in his first term that generally he continues to tackle issues of local concern, provide local constituent services and represents the values of the majority of voters in that district."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.vcstar.com/story/opinion/editorials/2016/10/23/editorial-re-elect-rep-steve-knight-25th-district/92417496/|title=Editorial: Re-elect Rep. Steve Knight in 25th District|website=Vcstar.com|access-date=4 October 2018}}</ref>

Knight did not make an endorsement in the [[United States presidential election, 2016|2016 U.S. presidential election]], saying that he could not support Democratic nominee [[Hillary Clinton]] or Republican nominee [[Donald Trump]].<ref>{{cite news|last1=Samuels|first1=Nikolas|title=Congressman Steve Knight releases statement on Saturday saying he 'cannot support' Donald Trump|url=http://www.signalscv.com/2016/10/08/steve-knight-releases-statement-saying-cannot-support-donald-trump/|access-date=20 October 2016|publisher=Santa Clarita Valley Signal|date=October 8, 2016}}</ref> Knight said he was "deeply disturbed" by reports that in 2005 Trump bragged about grabbing and groping women without their consent.<ref>[https://www.latimes.com/politics/essential/la-pol-sac-essential-politics-updates-did-knight-say-he-ll-vote-for-donald-1476471534-htmlstory.html Did Knight Say He'll Vote for Donald] The Los Angeles Times, Oct 14, 2016</ref>

Knight was re-elected in the general election on November 8, 2016. He defeated Democrat Bryan Caforio with 54% of the vote.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Sullivan|first1=Bartholomew|title=Brownley, Knight, Carbajal win House seats|url=http://www.vcstar.com/story/news/politics/elections/2016/11/09/brownley-knight-carbajal-win-house-seats/93553618|access-date=November 11, 2016|agency=USA Today|publisher=Ventura County Star|date=November 9, 2016}}</ref>

==== 2018 ====
{{main|2018 United States House of Representatives elections in California#District 25}}

On February 21, 2018, Ambassador [[John R. Bolton|John Bolton]] endorsed Knight and nine other Congressional candidates who had served in the U.S. military, despite Bolton's endorsement of Knight's opponent in the 2014 primary.  Bolton's PAC contributed $70,000 to each candidate's campaign.<ref>{{cite press release|url=https://globenewswire.com/news-release/2018/02/21/1372781/0/en/AMBASSADOR-JOHN-BOLTON-ENDORSES-TEN-VETERANS-FOR-CONGRESS.html|title=AMBASSADOR JOHN BOLTON ENDORSES TEN VETERANS FOR CONGRESS|author=John Bolton|website=Globenewswire.com|date=21 February 2018|access-date=4 October 2018}}</ref>

In 2018, the ''Los Angeles Times'' described Knight as fighting "to keep his place as the last GOP incumbent representing an L.A. County-based district."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.latimes.com/politics/essential/la-pol-ca-essential-politics-updates-democrats-who-want-to-oust-rep-steve-1521212232-htmlstory.html|title=Democrats who want to oust Rep. Steve Knight make their case at forum|first=Javier|last=Panzar|website=[[Los Angeles Times]]|access-date=4 October 2018}}</ref>

On November 7, 2018, Knight conceded the election to Democrat [[Katie Hill]], saying "The voters have spoken."<ref>{{cite news |work=[[KCBS-TV]] |title=Rep. Steve Knight Concedes To Katie Hill In 25th District Race |url=https://losangeles.cbslocal.com/2018/11/07/rep-steve-knight-concedes-to-katie-hill-in-25th-district-race/ |date=November 7, 2018 |access-date=November 8, 2018}}</ref>

===Tenure===
In May 2018, Knight's H.R. 3210, the Securely Expediting Clearances Through Reporting Transparency (SECRET) Act, which seeks to improve the protection of classified information while also expediting security clearances for government employees, was signed into law.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://signalscv.com/2018/05/knights-secret-act-for-security-clearances-signed-into-law/|title=Knight's SECRET act for security clearances signed into law|date=23 May 2018|website=Signalscv.com|access-date=4 October 2018}}</ref>

Knight did not make an endorsement in the [[2016 United States presidential election|2016 U.S. presidential election]], saying that he could not support Democratic nominee [[Hillary Clinton]] or Republican nominee [[Donald Trump]].<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.signalscv.com/2016/10/08/steve-knight-releases-statement-saying-cannot-support-donald-trump|title=Congressman Steve Knight releases statement on Saturday saying he 'cannot support' Donald Trump|date=October 8, 2016|publisher=Santa Clarita Valley Signal|last1=Samuels|first1=Nikolas|access-date=October 20, 2016}}</ref> Knight said he was "deeply disturbed" by reports that in 2005 Trump bragged about grabbing and groping women without their consent.<ref>[https://www.latimes.com/politics/essential/la-pol-sac-essential-politics-updates-did-knight-say-he-ll-vote-for-donald-1476471534-htmlstory.html "Did Knight Say He'll Vote for Donald?"], ''Los Angeles Times'', October 14, 2016.</ref> In December 2016, Knight revealed that he voted for Trump after all.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.latimes.com/politics/essential/la-pol-ca-essential-politics-updates-rep-steve-knight-i-have-made-no-1479155714-htmlstory.html|title=Essential Politics November archives|access-date=2017-02-12|issn=0458-3035|newspaper=Los Angeles Times}}</ref>

In February 2017, he voted against a resolution that would have directed the House to request 10 years of Trump's tax returns, which would then have been reviewed by the [[United States House Committee on Ways and Means|House Ways and Means Committee]] in a closed session.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://indy100.com/article/republican-vote-donald-trump-tax-president-united-states-229-185-congress-7603851|title=These are all the Republicans who don't want you to see Donald Trump's tax returns|date=2017-02-28|work=indy100|access-date=2017-03-01}}</ref>

In May 2017, Knight became the third House Republican from California to support the appointment of a special prosecutor to probe Russian involvement in the 2016 election. "There is so much conflicting information from many sources; Americans deserve the opportunity to learn the truth," he said.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.latimes.com/politics/essential/la-pol-ca-essential-politics-updates-rep-steve-knight-joins-call-for-1494961125-htmlstory.html|title=Republican Rep. Steve Knight joins call for special prosecutor to take over FBI's Russia investigation|first=Sarah D.|last=Wire|website=[[Los Angeles Times]]|access-date=4 October 2018}}</ref>

'''[[FiveThirtyEight]]''' found that Knight voted with President Trump 99% of the time, and was the seventh-most partisan Trump supporter in the House when compared to his district's voting patterns.<ref name="Five38">{{Deprecated archive|url=https://archive.today/20170526151220/https://projects.fivethirtyeight.com/congress-trump-score/house/|title=Tracking Congress in the Age of Trump}}, accessed September 25, 2018</ref>

==Political positions==

===Vote Smart Political Courage Test===
According to [[Project Vote Smart|Vote Smart's]] 2016 analysis, Knight generally supported anti-abortion legislation,<ref name="auto">{{Cite web|author=Javier Panzar|url=https://www.latimes.com/politics/la-pol-ca-outside-spending-bryan-caforio-steve-knight-congress-20160930-snap-story.html|title='Dark money' funds flood of political canvassers in heated Los Angeles County congressional race|newspaper=Los Angeles Times|date=September 30, 2016|quote=his staunch opposition to abortion.}}</ref> opposed an income tax increase, supported federal spending as a means of promoting economic growth, supported lowering taxes as a means of promoting economic growth, opposed requiring states to adopt federal education standards, supported the building of the [[Keystone Pipeline]], supported government funding for the development of renewable energy, opposed the federal regulation of greenhouse gas emissions, opposed gun-control legislation, supported repealing the [[Affordable Care Act]], supported requiring immigrants who are unlawfully present to return to their country of origin before they are eligible for citizenship, and supported increased American intervention in [[Iraq]] and [[Syria]] beyond air support.<ref>{{cite web|title=Steve Knight's Issue Positions (Political Courage Test)|url=https://votesmart.org/candidate/political-courage-test/71098/steve-knight/#.WlbUWUtG0js|publisher=[[Vote Smart]]|access-date=January 10, 2018}}</ref>

===Abortion===
Knight is staunchly anti-abortion.<ref name="auto"/> Knight is not among those who make exceptions in cases of rape, incest or when a mother's life is at risk. “I am a pro-life candidate. I make no exception. I don’t have any of those three that a lot of my other Republicans have,” he said in an interview.<ref>Chicago Tribune, "Outside groups trade attack ads in heated North Los Angeles County Congressional race," October 7, 2016 [https://www.chicagotribune.com/la-pol-sac-essential-politics-updates-dccc-hits-rep-steve-knight-with-new-tv-1475866573-htmlstory.html]</ref>

===Second amendment===
When Knight responded to the [[2017 Las Vegas shooting]] by writing online that his prayers were "with the victims and their families" and describing it as "an unfortunate reminder of the evil in our world," he was greeted with angry comments about his opposition to gun control. Peter Marston, a professor at [[Cal State Northridge]], defended Knight responding to the negative comments by saying it "was bad enough when there was no middle ground for people to talk about. Now we've reached the point where if you don't say what I believe, you've committed an affront to me."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dailynews.com/2017/10/03/rep-steve-knight-urges-prayers-for-las-vegas-and-gun-control-advocates-are-angry/|title=Rep. Steve Knight urges prayers for Las Vegas — and gun-control advocates are angry|date=3 October 2017|website=Dailynews.com|access-date=4 October 2018}}</ref>

===Drug policy===
Knight had a "C" rating from [[NORML]] regarding his voting record on [[cannabis]]-related matters.<ref name="NORML">{{cite web|title=California Scorecard - NORML.org - Working to Reform Marijuana Laws|url=http://norml.org/congressional-scorecard/california|website=norml.org|access-date=22 December 2017|language=en-us}}</ref> Knight says he has never smoked marijuana. He believes that cannabis should be removed as a Schedule I drug and be reclassified as a Schedule 3 drug, due to its medicinal use. Knight believes legalization should be up to individual states, yet it should remain illegal federally.<ref name="Bellantoni101416">{{cite web|last1=Bellantoni|first1=Christina|title=Essential Politics October archive|url=https://www.latimes.com/politics/essential/la-pol-sac-essential-politics-updates-caforio-knight-marijuana-htmlstory.html|website=Los Angeles Times|access-date=22 December 2017}}</ref> 
He voted in favor, in 2016, for allowing veterans access to [[medical marijuana]], if legal in their state, per their Veterans Health Administration doctor's recommendation.<ref name="NORML"/>

===Confederate flag===
In 2014, Knight was one of three California legislators who voted against a measure barring the display or sale of [[Flags of the Confederate States of America|Confederate flag]] images from California state museums and gift shops.<ref name=":0">{{cite news|url=https://www.latimes.com/local/political/la-me-pc-steve-knight-flag-20140827-story.html|title=Confederate flag vote puts Knight on spot in congressional race|date=August 27, 2014|work=Los Angeles Times|last1=Merl|first1=Jean|access-date=February 17, 2016}}</ref> Knight condemned the flag as a symbol of hate but voted against the bill on constitutional grounds, stating "It's not that I condone the Confederate flag, but I believe there are constitutional issues."<ref name=":0" />

===Military ===
Knight introduced H.R. 1162, the No Hero Left Untreated Act of 2017, which established a pilot program to implement emergent advanced technology to treat service members and veterans with PTSD and traumatic brain injuries.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://signalscv.com/2017/05/rep-steve-knight-reflection-first-100-days-115th-congress/|title=Rep. Steve Knight: Reflection on first 100 days of the 115th Congress|date=26 May 2017|website=Signalscv.com|access-date=4 October 2018}}</ref>

On May 24, 2018, the House of Representatives voted 351 - 66 to approve the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) for 2019, which included the provisions of H.R. 5546, introduced in April by Knight to authorize the use of $20 million allocated to the National Guard's counterdrug programs to combat opioid trafficking and abuse. "The international illegal drug trade not only peddles poison into our communities, but is also a significant threat to national security." said Knight. "Terrorist organizations and international cartels often fund themselves through the trade of heroin, cocaine, and other illicit substances. By deploying already-existing DOD intelligence and surveillance assets to support ongoing anti-narcotics operations, we can give law enforcement agencies a huge advantage over criminals."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://signalscv.com/2018/05/knights-opioid-prevention-bill-passes-house/|title=Knight's opioid prevention bill passes House|date=29 May 2018|website=Signalscv.com|access-date=4 October 2018}}</ref>

The NDAA, passed by the House on May 24, 2018, also included the provisions of H.R. 5707, the Sexual Trauma Response and Treatment (START) Act, introduced by Knight in May. It would establish a pilot Department of Defense program to provide comprehensive outpatient programs to military service members with military sexual trauma.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://signalscv.com/2018/05/knight-introduces-sexual-trauma-response-and-treatment-act/|title=Knight introduces Sexual Trauma Response and Treatment Act|date=9 May 2018|website=Signalscv.com|access-date=4 October 2018}}</ref>

In April 2015, Knight voted to impose a one-year delay on new Department of Defense rules designed to shield military families from abusive terms on payday loans and other forms of expensive short-term credit.<ref>[https://www.huffingtonpost.com/2015/04/30/payday-lending-soldiers_n_7182510.html "Democrats Defeat GOP Plan to Block Predatory Lending Protections For Soldiers"], ''The Huffington Post'', April 30, 2015.</ref>

===Economy===
In November 2017, Knight voted in favor of the [[Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017]], the house version of the Republican Party's tax reform bill.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://edition.cnn.com/2017/11/16/politics/house-tax-plan-vote/index.html|title=House Republicans pass tax plan|author1=Lauren Fox |author2=Deirdre Walsh|work=CNN|access-date=2017-11-17}}</ref><ref name=":03">{{Cite news|url=https://www.latimes.com/politics/la-pol-ca-gop-house-tax-vote-20171116-htmlstory.html|title=Most California GOP House members vote to pass tax bill, with some hoping the Senate will help fix it|work=Los Angeles Times|access-date=2017-11-17|language=en-US|issn=0458-3035}}</ref> The House bill removes state and local tax breaks that many Californians use, such as the mortgage interest deduction.<ref name=":03" /> Several House Republicans representing Californian districts voted against the legislation because it raised taxes on Californians.<ref name=":03"/> Knight said after the vote that he hoped that a [[Reconciliation (United States Congress)|reconciliation version of the bill with the Senate]] would make the bill better: "I don't know if they're going to make it better, but we'll see. There are some things in the Senate bill I like, there are some things in there that I don't like."<ref name=":03"/>

According to the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'', immediately after the vote, the Senate version of the bill "contains even deeper cuts to state and local tax breaks that are popular with Californians but maintains the mortgage interest deduction at its current level instead of cutting it in half as the House plan does. It also repeals Obamacare's individual mandate, a move that could further complicate the situation for California members who represent districts with a lot of Obamacare enrollees."<ref name=":03"/> In December 2017, Knight voted in favor of the [[Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017|Tax Cuts and Jobs Act]].<ref name="Almukhtar">{{cite web|last1=Almukhtar|first1=Sarah|title=How Each House Member Voted on the Tax Bill|url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2017/12/19/us/politics/tax-bill-house-live-vote.html?_r=1|website=The New York Times|access-date=2 January 2018|date=19 December 2017}}</ref>

Knight stated that the new bill would "deliver tax relief to all Americans" and denied claims that the upper class would benefit more in the long-term than the middle- and lower-class. He said that the latter will get "a break on their personal taxes" and called the bill a step towards [[tax reform]] and a simplified tax code.<ref name="Simon122217">{{cite web|last1=Simon|first1=Melissa|title=Tax reforms unwrapped|url=https://www.simivalleyacorn.com/articles/tax-reforms-unwrapped/|website=Simi Valley Acorn|access-date=2 January 2018}}</ref>

===Environment===
In September 2014, asked on a candidate questionnaire if he believed the [[scientific consensus on climate change]], Knight wrote that California's efforts to curtail global warming were "rash," that  California's law the [[Global Warming Solutions Act of 2006]] (AB 32) was "killing California's economy", and that the federal government should not regulate [[greenhouse gas emissions]].<ref name=scvbeacon>{{cite news|title=Beacon Exclusive: Knight, Strickland Debate the 25th Congressional District Issues|url=http://www.westranchbeacon.com/visit-political-outlooks-view.php?type=11&id=3420|date=September 9, 2014|access-date=March 16, 2017|newspaper=SCV Beacon|location=[[Santa Clarita, California]]}}</ref>

In February 2016, Knight introduced a bill that would block mining at [[Soledad Canyon]].<ref>{{cite news|last1=Money|first1=Luke|title=Wilk introduces 'Plan B' to help block Cemex mine|url=http://www.signalscv.com/section/36/article/148699|access-date=February 17, 2016|publisher=Santa Clarita Valley Signal|date=February 16, 2016}}</ref> Knight also introduced legislation intended to increase federal regulation of gas storage facilities and prevent natural gas leaks.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Healy|first1=Patrick|title=Energy Secretary Calls for 'Fresh Look' at Regulation After Gas Leak|url=http://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/Energy-Secretary-Calls-for-Fresh-Look-at-Regulation-After-Gas-Leak-369068501.html|access-date=February 17, 2016|publisher=NBC|date=February 16, 2016}}</ref> Knight's legislation was merged into a bipartisan bill named the Protecting our Infrastructure of Pipelines and Enhancing Safety Act of 2016. In April 2016, the bill was passed unanimously by the [[House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee]].<ref>{{cite news|last1=Bartholomew|first1=Dana|title=Valley lawmakers continue push for safer natural gas storage|url=http://www.dailynews.com/government-and-politics/20160424/valley-lawmakers-continue-push-for-safer-natural-gas-storage|access-date=April 29, 2016|publisher=Los Angeles Daily News|date=April 24, 2016}}</ref> In June 2016, Knight's bill was signed into law by President Obama.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Bartholomew|first1=Dana|title=National bill signed to prevent gas leaks like the one near Porter Ranch|url=http://www.dailynews.com/government-and-politics/20160622/national-bill-signed-to-prevent-gas-leaks-like-the-one-near-porter-ranch|access-date=28 June 2016|publisher=Los Angeles Daily News|date=June 22, 2016}}</ref>

On June 27, 2017, H.R. 2156, the St. Francis Dam Memorial Act, co-sponsored by Knight and Julia Brownley, unanimously passed the House Natural Resources Committee. It would create a national memorial at the site of the 1928 St. Francis Dam Disaster that took 431 lives.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://signalscv.com/2017/07/house-okays-legislation-st-francis-dam-disaster-memorial/|title=House okays legislation for St. Francis Dam Disaster Memorial|date=11 July 2017|website=Signalscv.com|access-date=4 October 2018}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last1=Money|first1=Luke|title=Knight cites frustrations, progress during first year in Congress|url=http://www.signalscv.com/section/36/article/146812/|access-date=17 February 2016|publisher=Santa Clarita Valley Signal|date=January 1, 2016}}</ref>

===Natural disaster relief===
Knight co-sponsored the Emergency Disaster Aid Package, with [[Mimi Walters]], to fund over $2.6 billion in relief aid to areas impacted by the California wildfires and recent hurricanes.<ref name="Ender122217">{{cite web|last1=Ender|first1=Gina|title=Fire and earthquake losses are tax deductible under House bill|url=https://signalscv.com/2017/12/fire-earthquake-losses-tax-deductible-house-bill/|website=Santa Clarita Valley Signal|access-date=22 December 2017|date=21 December 2017}}</ref>

===Healthcare===
Knight was in favor of repealing the [[Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act|Affordable Care Act]] (Obamacare). He wrote "The Affordable Care Act is a prime example of bad policy" on a September 2014 candidate questionnaire.<ref name=scvbeacon/> On May 4, 2017, Knight voted to repeal the [[Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act]] (Obamacare) and pass the [[American Health Care Act of 2017|American Health Care Act]].<ref name=":02">{{Cite news|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/graphics/politics/ahca-house-vote/|title=How the House voted to pass the GOP health-care bill|newspaper=Washington Post|access-date=2017-05-04}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2017/05/04/health-care-vote-puts-pressure-dozens-vulnerable-gop-reps/101297824|title=Health care vote puts pressure on dozens of vulnerable GOP reps|work=USA TODAY|access-date=2017-05-04}}</ref><ref name=":1">{{Cite news|url=https://www.latimes.com/politics/la-pol-ca-how-gop-delegation-voted-healthcare-20170504-htmlstory.html|title=All of California's GOP House members voted to pass the Obamacare repeal bill|work=Los Angeles Times|access-date=2017-05-05|issn=0458-3035}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.businessinsider.com/republican-healthcare-bill-changes-house-vote-2017-5?r=US&IR=T|title=Here are the major changes to the GOP healthcare bill|work=Business Insider|access-date=2017-05-05}}</ref>

Knight highlighted how he contributed to an amendment to the bill that would provide $8 billion in funding over five years to help insure those with preexisting conditions in so-called "high-risk pools".<ref name=":2">{{Cite news|url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/lifestyles/health/sns-bc-ca--health-overhaul-california-republicans-20170505-story.html|title=Vulnerable California Republicans defend health bill votes|last=Bollag|first=Sophia|work=chicagotribune.com|access-date=2017-05-10}}</ref>

===Immigration===
In May 2016, Knight participated in a public debate with his Democratic challengers. In it he said that immigration reform should focus on those who want to move to the U.S. legally.<ref name=scvsignal>[http://www.signalscv.com/section/36/article/151806 Candid debate at Hart Hall], ''Santa Clarita Valley Signal'', May 5, 2016.</ref>

===Israel===
In May 2018 Knight was a member of the ten-person House delegation that attended the opening of the U.S. Embassy in Jerusalem. "It was an honor to be a part of this delegation and show support for our Israeli friends," said Knight. "Israel is our longest and closest ally in the region and still stands as the only modern democracy in the area. We share the same goals for national security, human rights, and economic relations."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://signalscv.com/2018/05/knight-meets-with-israeli-officials-at-new-embassy-responds-to-pastors-controversy/|title=Knight meets with Israeli officials at new embassy, responds to pastors controversy|date=15 May 2018|website=Signalscv.com|access-date=4 October 2018}}</ref>

===Social Security===
In a debate with other candidates in May 2016, Knight said, "“I think Social Security was a bad idea” and that we "should have had the government sit down with the private sector and build a system people could take with them.” Knight warned that the U.S. Social Security system retirement system will run out of money by 2030 if major changes are not implemented.<ref name=scvsignal/>

===Aviation===
In February 2016, Knight introduced legislation that would allow the [[Federal Aviation Administration]] to continue and expand upon its research, engineering and development programs through 2019.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Varela|first1=Rob|title=Knight pushes FAA bill to expand aerospace research|url=http://www.vcstar.com/news/local/simi-valley/knight-pushes-faa-bill-to-expand-aerospace-research--2b4b40f2-ff18-6b5f-e053-0100007f00ba-368099581.html|access-date=February 17, 2016|publisher=Ventura County Star|date=February 8, 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title=Knight Introduces Aerospace Research Legislation|url=http://www.hometownstation.com/santa-clarita-news/press-releases/knight-introduces-aerospace-research-legislation-166924|access-date=February 17, 2016|publisher=KHTS|date=February 9, 2016}}</ref>

Knight introduced the Aeronautics Innovation Act of 2017.<ref name="PressRelease">{{Cite press release|url=https://knight.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=1124|title=Knight Introduces Aeronautics Innovation Act Of 2017|date=June 23, 2017|publisher=Office of U.S. Representative Steve Knight}}</ref>

=== LGBT rights ===
Knight voted against an amendment, which ultimately failed narrowly, that stated that religious corporations, associations and institutions that receive federal contracts can't be discriminated against on the basis of religion.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Marcos |first=Cristina |date=May 19, 2016 |title=Chaos in House after GOP votes down LGBT measure |url=https://thehill.com/blogs/floor-action/house/280542-dems-cry-foul-after-house-gop-votes-down-lgbt-measure/ |access-date=February 12, 2017 |newspaper=TheHill}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=May 20, 2016 |title=Really, 30 Republicans Switched Sides on LGBT Discrimination |url=http://www.rollcall.com/news/politics/really-30-republicans-switched-sides-lgbt |access-date=February 12, 2017 |website=Roll Call}}</ref>

===Scientific and technological development===
On February 13, 2018, three Knight-authored bills were passed in the House intended to "advance cutting edge research initiatives and improve STEM education." H.R. 4376, The Department of Energy Research Infrastructure Act, would fund research in advanced photonics, x-ray energy applications, and other scientific fields. The other bills were H.R. 4254, The Women in Aerospace Education Act,  and H.R. 3397, The Building Blocks of STEM Act.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://signalscv.com/2018/02/congressman-knights-science-bills-get-house-approval/|title=Congressman Knight's science bills get House approval|date=13 February 2018|website=Signalscv.com|access-date=4 October 2018}}</ref>
