'''Cathie M. Wright''' (May 18, 1929 &ndash; April 14, 2012) was an American politician from [[California]] and a member of the [[Republican Party (United States)|Republican]] party.

==Early career==

A onetime [[Simi Valley, California|Simi Valley]] councilwoman, Wright was serving as mayor in 1980 when she won election to the [[California State Assembly]] seat vacated by Republican [[Bob Cline]], who had made an unsuccessful run for state senate.<ref name="joincalifornia">{{cite web|last=Vassar|first=Alex|author2=Shane Meyers|title=Cathi M. Wright, Republican|work=JoinCalifornia.com|year=2007|url=http://www.joincalifornia.com/candidate/4153|accessdate=2007-01-25}}</ref> She represented the [[Ventura County, California|Ventura]] county based 37th district until 1992, when she opted not to seek reelection.

==State senate==

After 12 years in the Assembly, Wright instead ran for the open 19th [[California State Senate|state senate]] district held since 1980 by Republican stalwart [[Edward M. Davis|Ed Davis]], a former Los Angeles police chief. Davis didn't much like Wright (whom he dubbed "The Peroxide Princess of Simi Valley") and recruited former Assemblywoman [[Marion W. La Follette]] to run for the seat.<ref name="california journal july 1992">"Complete Primary Results," by A.G. Block, California Journal, July 1992, p.347.</ref> After a bitter campaign, Wright prevailed, but just barely. She won reelection easily in 1996, but California state Term Limits prevented her from seeking reelection in 2000.

==Lieutenant governor race==

In 1994 she became the Republican nominee for Lt. Governor by defeating moderate Assemblyman [[Stan Statham]] in the party's primary. Statham was best known for a proposal to split California into three states. Wright beat him handily but was soundly defeated by then Democratic state Controller [[Gray Davis]] in the general election.<ref name="california journal december 1994">"Complete Election Results," by A.G. Block, California Journal, December 1994, p.14.</ref>

==Controversy==

One issue that has constantly dogged Wright was her seeking the help of then Democratic Assembly Speaker [[Willie Brown (politician)|Willie Brown]] in an effort to have a judge go easy on her daughter, who had racked up numerous traffic violations.<ref name="california journal july 1992"/>
