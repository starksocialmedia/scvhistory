'''Kathryn Ann Barger-Leibrich''' (born in 1960) is an American politician who is a member of the [[Los Angeles County Board of Supervisors]]<ref name="Wells 2017">{{cite web | last=Wells | first=Sandy | title=LA County Supervisor Leads Effort to Deescalate Controntations Between Deputies and Homeless | website=KABC-AM | date=January 23, 2017 | url=http://www.kabc.com/2017/01/23/supervisor-barger-seeks-expansion-of-lasd-mental-evaluation-teams-explained/ | access-date=February 3, 2017 | archive-date=February 4, 2017 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170204085116/http://www.kabc.com/2017/01/23/supervisor-barger-seeks-expansion-of-lasd-mental-evaluation-teams-explained/ | url-status=dead }}</ref> for the 5th District since 2016.<ref name="Abram 2016">{{cite web | last=Abram | first=Susan | title=Janice Hahn, Kathryn Barger give LA County Board of Supervisors historic female supermajority | website=Los Angeles Daily News | date=December 5, 2016 | url=http://www.dailynews.com/government-and-politics/20161205/janice-hahn-kathryn-barger-give-la-county-board-of-supervisors-historic-female-supermajority | access-date=February 4, 2017}}</ref> A member of the [[Republican Party (United States)|Republican Party]], Barger served as [[Chair of Los Angeles County]] from 2019&ndash;2020 and 2024&ndash;2025.<ref name="LAT Nov 2016">{{cite web |title=Why Political Party Matters So Much in a Nonpartisan Race to Replace a County Supervisor |url=https://www.latimes.com/local/lanow/la-me-ln-district-five-supervisor-race-antonovich-barger-park-20161031-snap-20161031-story.html |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |first=Hailey |last=Branson-Potts |date=November 2, 2016}}</ref> She previously served as Chief Deputy Supervisor and Chief of Staff to her predecessor Mayor [[Michael D. Antonovich]].<ref name="REPORTS 2016">{{cite web | title=Kathryn Barger to be Sworn in Today as Los Angeles County Supervisor Representing Pasadena |website=Pasadena Now | date=December 5, 2016 | url=http://www.pasadenanow.com/main/kathryn-barger-to-be-sworn-in-as-los-angeles-county-supervisor-representing-pasadena/ | access-date=February 4, 2017}}</ref>

== Political career ==
Barger began her career in government in 1988 when she [[intern]]ed in the office of Los Angeles County Supervisor [[Michael D. Antonovich]]. By 2001 she had risen up the ranks to become Antonovich's chief of staff.<ref name="LAT Nov 2016" />

=== Los Angeles County Board of Supervisors ===
She became a member of the L.A. County Board of Supervisors in December 2016, representing the Fifth District. Her district is the county's largest supervisorial district in area, spanning 2,785 square miles, and includes 20 cities and 83 unincorporated communities in the [[San Gabriel Valley|San Gabriel]], [[San Fernando Valley|San Fernando]], [[Santa Clarita Valley|Santa Clarita]], and [[Antelope Valley|Antelope]] Valleys.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Los Angeles County Fifth District |url=https://kathrynbarger.lacounty.gov/get-to-know-fifth-district/ |website=Los Angeles County Fifth District}}</ref>

In her role as a county supervisor, Barger has co-authored bills furthering the county’s support for veterans<ref>{{cite web |title=LA County Officials Approve $20 Million in Funding for Veteran Housing |url=https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/LA-County-Officials-Veteran-Housing-20-Million-Resources-484051371.html |website=NBC News|date=May 30, 2018 }}</ref> and foster children,<ref>{{cite web |title=LA County will let foster children stay at their original school, even if they change homes |url=https://www.dailynews.com/2017/05/06/la-county-will-let-foster-children-stay-at-their-original-school-even-if-they-change-homes/ |website=Los Angeles Daily News |date=May 6, 2017 |publisher=Southern California News Group |access-date=25 June 2018}}</ref> as well as modernizing the County's mental health system.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Release |first=Press |date=2023-01-24 |title=L.A. County Focuses on Bringing More Mental Health Beds Into Service |url=https://scvnews.com/l-a-county-focuses-on-bringing-more-mental-health-beds-into-service/ |access-date=2024-01-11 |website=SCVNews.com |language=en-US}}</ref>

Barger also co-authored motions to address homelessness in LA County, which notably includes a bill passed by the California State Assembly in May 2018 amending the state’s definition of “gravely disabled”, and allowing more state-sponsored medical care to be provided to those who may be suffering from a serious mental illness.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Abram |first1=Susan |title=following-la-county-vote-legislation-filed-to-amend-state-law-to-help-gravely-disabled-homeless |url=https://www.dailynews.com/2018/01/31/following-la-county-vote-legislation-filed-to-amend-state-law-to-help-gravely-disabled-homeless/?returnUrl=https://www.dailynews.com/2018/01/31/following-la-county-vote-legislation-filed-to-amend-state-law-to-help-gravely-disabled-homeless/?clearUserState=true |work=Daily News}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Signal Staff |title=State definition of "gravely disabled" on the road to be amended |url=https://signalscv.com/2018/06/state-definition-of-gravely-disabled-on-the-road-to-be-amended/ |website=The Signal |date=June 2, 2018 |access-date=25 June 2018}}</ref> Barger also established the County's Blue-Ribbon Commission on Homelessness to identify and correct the structural barriers to solving the County's homelessness crisis.<ref>{{Cite web |title=LA BOS |url=https://brch.lacounty.gov/ |access-date=2024-01-11 |website=LA BOS |language=en}}</ref>

Barger coauthored a motion creating the Blue Ribbon Commission on Public Safety, which was intended to explore the impact that Assembly Bill 109, [[California Proposition 47 (2014)|California Proposition 47]], and [[California Proposition 57 (2016)|California Proposition 57]], which were collectively aimed at converting many nonviolent drug offenses into misdemeanors and allowing for the early release of some inmates, has had inside of Los Angeles County.<ref name="Agrawal">{{cite web |last1=Agrawal |first1=Nina |title=County approves a new panel to study criminal justice reform: What's the impact of downgrading felonies and releasing inmates early? |url=https://www.latimes.com/local/lanow/la-me-ln-blue-ribbon-public-safety-20170815-story.html |website=Los Angeles Times |date=August 16, 2017 |access-date=25 June 2018}}</ref> The formation of the commission was a reaction to the murder of police Officer Keith Boyer, and ultimately passed on a 3-0 vote with abstentions. The commission membership at its inception was controversial, with critics citing that many of the 27 members drafted to the commission were directly affected by Proposition 47, coming from roles within the county’s judicial system.<ref name="Ender">{{cite web |last1=Ender |first1=Gina |title=Supervisors to propose public safety commission |url=https://signalscv.com/2017/08/supervisors-propose-public-safety-commission/ |website=The Signal |date=August 11, 2017 |access-date=25 June 2018}}</ref><ref name="Agrawal" /> Other critics noted that linking the murder of Officer Boyer to the passage of criminal reform efforts was misguided because the error that led to the release of Officer Boyer’s murderer was committed at the county level.<ref>{{cite news |title=County mistakes, not reform laws, allowed the alleged killer of a Whittier police officer to go fr |url=https://www.latimes.com/opinion/editorials/la-ed-mejia-report-20180510-story.html |access-date=25 June 2018 |agency=The Times Editorial Board |work=Los Angeles Times}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=The Times Editorial Board |title=County should seek the whole truth, and nothing but, on criminal justice |url=https://www.latimes.com/opinion/editorials/la-ed-county-blue-ribbon-20170812-story.html |website=Los Angeles Times |date=August 12, 2017 |access-date=25 June 2018}}</ref>

In 2017, Barger was the only opposition in a 4-1 vote to eliminate the "registration fee" that the Los Angeles County Public Defender's office and other court-appointed counsel charge defendants before providing them with legal services.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Stoltze |first1=Frank |title=LA County drops $50 public defender fee for criminal defendants |url=http://www.scpr.org/news/2017/06/06/72605/la-county-drops-50-public-defender-fee-for-crimina/ |website=Southern California Public Radio |date=June 6, 2017 |publisher=SCPR |access-date=25 June 2018}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.latimes.com/local/lanow/la-me-ln-registration-fee-20170606-story.html|title=L.A. County ends public defender 'registration fee'|last=Agrawal|first=Nina|website=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=June 6, 2017 |access-date=2018-02-10}}</ref>

In 2017, Barger was the only opposition in a 4-1 vote to establish the Business Registration program, which would levy a fee on businesses to create a registry and connect them with county resources.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Duan |first1=Crystal |title=Businesses, officials sound off on new county fee |url=https://signalscv.com/2018/05/businesses-officials-sound-off-on-new-county-fee/ |agency=The Signal |date=May 24, 2018}}</ref>

On December 3, 2019, Barger was elected by a unanimous vote of the Board to become its chair, succeeding [[Janice Hahn]].<ref>{{Cite tweet| user=kathrynbarger|number=1201943736382394368|title=My term as Chair will focus on our youth — our greatest hope for future success. Through what I'm calling Our County, Our Children, Our Commitment, we will renew our dedication to the well-being of youth as the forefront of our mission to serve LA County.https://vimeo.com/373191425|last=Barger|first=Supervisor Kathryn|date=2019-12-03|website=@kathrynbarger|language=en|access-date=2019-12-21}}</ref> She chaired the Board for the first year of the [[COVID-19 pandemic in California]] in 2020.<ref>{{Cite web |last1=Munoz|first1=Anabel|date=2020-04-24 |title=When will coronavirus restrictions be lifted in LA County? Board of Supervisors chair unveils 4-point plan |url=https://abc7.com/supervisor-kathryn-barger-board-of-supervisors-stay-at-home-los-angeles-county/6127563/ |access-date=2024-01-11 |website=ABC7 Los Angeles |language=en}}</ref>

==== Housing ====
In 2024, Barger pulled funding for 43 units of senior supportive housing between the boundaries of La Verne and San Dimas.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Rode |first=Erin |date=2024-08-22 |title=How LA County squashed a supportive housing project for seniors |url=https://lapublicpress.org/2024/08/san-dimas-la-verne-supportive-housing-senior-barger-la-county-homeless/ |website=LA Public Press |language=en-US}}</ref> Local NIMBYs had expressed opposition to the housing.<ref name=":0" />

In the aftermath of the [[January 2025 Southern California wildfires]], Barger requested that state housing laws be temporarily waived in Los Angeles County. The state housing laws in question incentivized increases in housing supply. Housing advocates criticized Barger's request, arguing that it would hinder needed housing construction in the aftermath of the fires.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2025-01-29 |title=L.A. County says state housing laws stand in way of rebuilding. Advocates disagree |url=https://www.latimes.com/california/story/2025-01-29/l-a-county-says-state-housing-laws-stand-in-way-of-rebuilding-advocates-disagree |website=Los Angeles Times |language=en-US}}</ref>

== Personal life ==
Kathryn Barger was born and raised in the 5th District. Barger attended [[Ohio Wesleyan University]] in Delaware, Ohio, earning a BA in Communications/Government in 1983.<ref>{{cite web |title=Kathryn Barger - Los Angeles County Supervisor |url=https://scvhistory.com/scvhistory/lw3109.htm |access-date=February 10, 2021 |website=SCVHistory.com}}</ref> She is married to a retired Sheriff’s deputy and lives in [[San Marino, California|San Marino]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Lehman |first=Mitch |date=2019-05-24 |title=Barger Promises to “Partner With City” |url=https://outlooknewspapers.com/sanmarinotribune/news/barger-promises-to-partner-with-city/article_01ddf6ab-9bc9-50c8-ba23-d5b87b70ef32.html |access-date=2025-01-16 |website=Outlook Newspapers |language=en}}</ref> Her brother is John M. Barger,<ref>{{cite news |last1=Rainey |first1=James |title=This Low-profile politico has become focus of rage over Trump post office policies |url=https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/politics/low-profile-los-angeles-politico-swept-up-in-the-postal-service-dispute/ar-BB18okSv |access-date=25 August 2021}}</ref> who was appointed to the [[Board of Governors of the United States Postal Service]] by then-president [[Donald Trump]] in 2019.<ref>{{cite web |title=Postal leadership |url=https://about.usps.com/who/leadership/board-governors/john-barger.htm |access-date=25 August 2021 |website=USPS Board of Governors John M. Barger - Who we are/Leadership - About.usps.com |archive-date=August 25, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210825172433/https://about.usps.com/who/leadership/board-governors/john-barger.htm |url-status=dead }}</ref>  Her late father Richards D. Barger was appointed California Insurance Commissioner by then-Governor [[Ronald Reagan]], serving from 1968 to 1972.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.hinshawlaw.com/newsroom-news-637.html | title=Insurance Industry Pioneer Richard D. Barger Has Died at the Age of 87 }}</ref>
