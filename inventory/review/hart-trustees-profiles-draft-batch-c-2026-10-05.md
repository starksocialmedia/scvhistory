## Linda Storli (#25157): full, 311 words

Linda Storli taught at Canyon High School for 30 years and then represented Trustee Area 1 on the William S. Hart Union High School District board for nine years, from December 2015 to December 2024.[1][2] She was the board's president from December 2019 to December 2020, and again for 2024.[2][3]

She served on the City of Santa Clarita's first Parks and Recreation Commission, which a list of the city's commissioners gives as 1988 to 1990, and the district's account of her adds that she was on the city formation committee of 1987.[4][5][6] She stood for the City Council three times without winning a seat: seventh of 26 candidates at the first council election, on November 3, 1987, with 5,550 votes; fourth of sixteen in April 1992, with 3,325; and seventh of thirteen in April 1994, with 2,406.[7]

She ran for the Hart board in 2015, the year she retired from teaching, and gave her occupation on the ballot as teacher and realtor.[4][8] It was the district's first election for Trustee Area 1, and she defeated the incumbent, Gloria Mercado-Fortine, with 1,428 votes to 1,078.[1][9] She was sworn in that December.[10] She was re-elected in November 2020, first of three candidates, with 10,140 votes, and lost in November 2024, third of three, with 6,543, behind Aakash Ahuja and Mercado-Fortine.[1]

She was the board's clerk for 2019, and her year as president was, in The Signal's words, "a year wrought with COVID-19 planning and Saugus High School shooting remembrances."[2][11] When the board voted 4-1 on July 14, 2021 to retire the Hart High School "Indian" mascot, she said before the vote that no one was deciding "without a tremendous amount of study and care."[12][13] As president in March 2024 she spoke at the ceremony at which the school announced its new mascot, the Hart Hawks, saying that "this change needed to be made."[13]

## Paul Strickland (#25425): full, 413 words

Paul Strickland served on two of the valley's school boards: the Sulphur Springs Union School District's from 1995 to 1999, and the William S. Hart Union High School District's from December 2001 until May 2013, when he resigned with plans to move out of state.[1][2] By Leon Worden's roster of the Hart board he was its president in 2005-06 and 2009-10.[3]

He came to the boards from animation: he gave his occupation as animation producer and director when he was elected at Sulphur Springs in 1995, and as parent and animation producer in 2001, and SCVNews.com wrote in 2011 that he had "a 30-year background in the film and television industry as an animator."[4][5]

At Sulphur Springs he was elected in November 1995, second of three candidates, with 1,149 votes.[1] He first stood for the Hart board in 1999 and came third of seven, with 4,193 votes; he was elected in 2001, second of six, with 11,105, and re-elected in 2005, second of eight, with 18,802, and in 2009, second of five, with 8,251.[1]

In 2005 he listed himself as president of the Santa Clarita Arts Advisory Committee and co-chair of the Hart district's ad hoc committee on diversity.[6] He was a founding member of the City of Santa Clarita's Arts Commission, on which the district gives his service as beginning in December 2009, and was appointed its chairman in September 2011; he still chaired it when he resigned in 2013.[7][2] The district's account of him also names his work on the bond committees for Measures V and SA, his membership of the city's Open Space Commission and his opposition to the Cemex mine.[8] He hosted a weekly arts program on KHTS AM-1220 and SCVTV, called "Thursday Matinee" in 2011 and 2012 and "Friday Matinee" in 2013.[9][10][11]

In September 2011, while he was field deputy to Assemblyman Cameron Smyth, he announced that he would run as a Republican for Smyth's seat in the redrawn 38th Assembly District.[12] The California Teachers Association endorsed him in March 2012, and in the primary of June 5, 2012 he finished out of the top two, with 14.7 percent of the vote.[13][14]

His resignation from the Hart board took effect on May 1, 2013.[15][16] Leon Worden's roster also lists him on the board of December 2013 as re-elected unopposed that year but not assuming office; the district's account names the three candidates who filed for the board that year as Chris Fall, Joseph Messina and Robert Jensen Jr.[17][18]

## Patricia Hanrion (#25437): short, 220 words

Patricia Hanrion sat on the William S. Hart Union High School District board for sixteen years, from December 1993 to December 2009, and by Leon Worden's roster she was its president in 1996-97, 2001-02 and 2006-07.[1][2]

Before she stood for the board she was vice president of the district's advisory council, and in 1991 was quoted on when the district might put a school bond measure on the ballot.[3] In 1993 the Los Angeles Times named her as a parent challenging the board's three incumbents.[4] She was elected on November 2, 1993, first of five candidates for three seats, with 9,794 votes, and she and Peter Warren, another newcomer, unseated two incumbents.[1][5]

She was re-elected in 1997, second of four candidates, with 5,102 votes; in 2001, third of six, with 10,102; and in 2005, first of eight, with 18,841.[1][6] By the roster she did not seek reelection in 2009.[7]

As a candidate she gave her occupation in 2001 as registered nurse and instructor of maternal and child health at College of the Canyons, and in 2005 as professor of nursing there; in both years she listed herself as the district's special education liaison.[8][9] In March 1994, when the board approved new secondary school boundaries, she said of the district's two new campuses that they were "in the wrong places, basically."[10]

## Dennis King (#25439): short, 270 words

Dennis King, a certified public accountant, sat on the William S. Hart Union High School District board for twenty years in two spells, from 1985 to 1993 and from 1997 to 2009, and by Leon Worden's roster he was its president in 1987-88, 1991-92, 1999-2000 and 2004-05.[1][2][3]

He was first elected in November 1985, third of seven candidates for three seats, with 1,395 votes, and ran unopposed in 1989.[4][5] A photograph of the board of 1986-87 shows him as its clerk.[6] In the campaign of 1993 he opposed Proposition 174, the school voucher initiative, saying that its funding mechanism "would be absolutely disastrous to public education."[7] The Times's returns for November 2, 1993 put him fifth of five candidates for three seats, with 7,934 votes; Leon Worden's roster says he did not seek reelection that year.[8][9]

He returned to the board at the election of November 4, 1997, third of four candidates, with 4,272 votes, giving his occupation as certified public accountant, and was re-elected in 2001, first of six, with 11,335, and in 2005, third of eight, with 17,261.[2][10] By the roster he did not seek reelection in 2009.[11]

He was a director of the Santa Clarita Valley Facilities Foundation, which the district's board helped to establish to build Golden Valley High School: he signed, with its other directors, a column of May 2005 on its work, and the foundation's history of 2014 lists him as a director and its vice president from 2013.[12][13][14] In 2011 the district named him to the founding board of its WiSH Education Foundation, as "Certified Public Accountant and past Hart Governing Board member."[15]

## Chris Fall (#28721): short, 221 words

Chris Fall, a Santa Clarita Valley businessman, sat on the William S. Hart Union High School District board by appointment from June to August 2013.[1][2][3]

He was president of the Santa Clarita Valley Chamber of Commerce in 2006, as owner of Advantage Disposal, and served on the City of Santa Clarita's Parks and Recreation Commission from 2010, chairing it in 2013.[4][5][6] In June 2013 his lighting company, LED Inc., which he had opened with Ryan Murphy in 2010, was named the 38th Assembly District's small business of the year.[6]

The board chose him on June 5, 2013, after nearly five hours of interviews and from four finalists, to complete the term Paul Strickland had left.[2] He resigned on August 16, 2013, citing a potential conflict of interest under section 1090: he had sold Advantage Disposal to Consolidated Services, LLC in December 2012 and had a consulting agreement with Consolidated Disposal Services, the district's waste and recycling contractor.[3] He had also filed for a full term at the election of November 2013, and on September 25 he renounced that candidacy, leaving the board to fill the seat by appointment.[7] In September 2013 the District Attorney's office told the district that the June voting appeared to have been by secret ballot, in violation of the Brown Act, a point moot after his resignation.[8]
