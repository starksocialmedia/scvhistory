## Summary

* **Dates confirmed.** Born **Feb 4, 1822, Washington DC**; died **Apr 22, 1893, at Decatur House, Washington DC**; buried at Chester Rural Cemetery, Chester PA. The site (bealeafb, Reynolds), Wikipedia, Wikidata Q1292161 (via SNAC), Find a Grave 11558486 and the LoC authority all agree. The only slip is 'age 72' on the Tejon Ranch Co. timeline (it should be 71).
* **Beale's Cut, best evidence (Ripley 1947–48, transcribing the LA Star, the Statutes and Board actions).** A staged cut: 1854 first cut (contractors Sanford & Carson, with a County $1,000 appropriation; Banning drove the first stage over, Dec 1854); 1858 lowered for Butterfield; state franchise May 7, 1861 to Brinley, Pico and Vineyard (Stats. 1861 p.303), amended Apr 18, 1862 (p.282); Beale held the franchise in 1862; most work in 1863; completed, and tolls set, Mar 1864 (LA Star 3-5-1864); the franchise ran out and the road went to the County in 1883–84. Depth: the statute required at least 50 ft; the traditional figure is 90 ft (unsurveyed); today it is about half that after the 1997–98 El Niño collapse. **Nothing credits Andrew Jackson with the Cut.** The 'Jackson' link is his 1830s appointment of Beale to the Naval School.
* **Offices.** Indian Superintendent for California and Nevada: appointed Mar 3, 1853 (the site's 1852 dates are too early); end date 1854 or 1856, unresolved. Surveyor General 1861–64; whether he was fired or resigned is unresolved, and the Lincoln 'monarch of all he surveys' quip exists in five versions with no contemporary source. Fort Tejon: Aug 1854 to Sept 11, 1864. Sebastian ('Tejon') Reserve: one reserve with two names, 1853 to 1862/64.
* **Conflicts:** 24 numbered groups (C1–C24). The biggest: Cut chronology, depth, credit and workforce (troops vs Chinese vs Coult's 1862 soldiers); franchise grantor and radius (2 miles by statute vs 3 miles vs 1 mile); naming; ranch acquisition and acreage (200,000 to 300,000); the camel stories (four landing dates, three origins, 'Fort Defiance, Texas'); film myths (the earliest film is 1913 Griffith, not 1917 Ford; Mix never jumped; no evidence of Hart at the Cut); and the Indigenous history, which needs tribal consultation.
* **New sections:** 10 live errors on the site (Section 6), including out-of-date 'private property' text now that the City of Santa Clarita approved buying the Cut (Oct 28, 2025), a mislabeled index link, a wrong page title, 1854 printed for 1864, and 'Fort Defiance, Texas'. 6 cases where the archive beats outside references (Section 7).
* **Beale Papers, verified.** The main collection is the **Library of Congress** 'Beale Family Papers from the Decatur House' (the National Trust passed them to LoC in 1959). **Huntington**: 'Collection related to Edward Fitzgerald Beale' (mssBeale, mostly research copies) and the B.D. Wilson papers. **Decatur House** (owned by the National Trust, operated by the WHHA) holds furnishings, not the papers.
* **Counts:** 116 pages classified (104 local, 9 career, 3 index); 30 further fetched pages checked and found peripheral. Beale's Cut index: 45 links / 38 content / 37 distinct targets. Details in Section 1.

## 2. Lifetime primary sources vs later accounts

Of the 116 classified pages, **14** carry an item made within Beale's lifetime and **4** span it (the Watkins 1888–1900 photos). The rest are later accounts, some of which transcribe primary sources.

**Within lifetime:** ap0625 (1872; not stated (earliest known photos; same set as AP2307)); ap2307 (1872; not stated); lw2143a (1853; USPRR / Williamson expedition report); lw3188 (1853; USPRR / Williamson); lw2580 (plat 11-8-1862 (copy); text 2013; traced copy photographed by Leon Worden 1997; original custody not stated (NEEDS_VERIFICATION)); lw2521 (~1842 map; text 2013; SCVHistory); ah_lastar062658 (6-26-1858; Los Angeles Star); ah5803 (9-16-1858; Butterfield Overland Mail Co.); tlp_lebeck080990 (1890; Kern County paper, 7-26-(1890); courtesy Tricia Lemon Putnam); heizer1972 (treaty 1851; Heizer 1972; Heizer 1972 (per page)); al2075 (2-20-1858; Porter's Spirit of the Times; Alan Pollack collection); lw2152a (Vischer print (date NEEDS_VERIFICATION); original print (per page)); sw_leaming070366 (7-3-1866; Robert S. Baker Collection, Huntington Library (per page); transcribed by Stan Walker); treatyguadalupehidalgo (2-2-1848; SCVHistory)

**Spans:** lo8803 (Carleton E. Watkins; Library of Congress (per page)); lo8801 (Carleton E. Watkins; Library of Congress (W535, per page)); lo8802 (Watkins; Library of Congress (W534, per page)); lo8804 (Watkins; LoC (per page))

Key reliability notes:
* **Ripley (HSSC Quarterly 1947–48)** is later, but it is the only source on the site that transcribes the LA Star (1858–64), the Statutes of California (1861, 1862) and Board actions, and it cites the Gaffey MSS at the Huntington. Use it as a guide to the originals.
* **lw2580** is a traced copy of a real 1862 government plat signed 'E.F. Beale' as Surveyor General. It is the best primary item on the site for Beale's Tejon dealings (original custody is NEEDS_VERIFICATION; likely GLO/BLM survey records).
* **al2075** (Porter's Spirit of the Times, 2-20-1858) and **ah_lastar062658** / **ah5803** (1858) are contemporary.
* **sw_leaming070366** (1866 letter to Beale; Huntington R.S. Baker collection, per the page) is primary for the oil period.
* **Reynolds (written 1976–94; book 1992; web ed. 1998)** is a popular history with several errors here (Naval Academy, Nov 1852, two reservations, troops dug 90 ft, $5,000, 297,000 acres, the camel idea from Death Valley). **The boilerplate** (Banning 30 ft / Chinese / 90 ft / El Niño) is repeated on 8 or more pages, unsourced.
* **1912 LAT and Chronicle** (lat051212, lp_sfchronicle062312) are the earliest printed sources seen for the Lincoln quip, and they postdate Beale.

### C11. How deep: 30, 50 or 90 feet, and today

## 8. Corrections to earlier findings

* **Brief premise, 'Cut credited to Andrew Jackson':** not found. None of the 146 fetched pages credits Jackson with the Cut; Jackson appears only as the President who appointed Beale to the Naval School (bealeafb, reynolds part28, WP). What does appear is **Phineas Banning** (the first stage over the 1854 cut) and **the County** (the 1854 $1,000 appropriation; Board-set tolls). See C2 and C12.
* **Working note superseded:** 'Army closed the reservation in 1854' (lo8801/2) had been logged as a date conflict. It is a typo for 1864, contradicted by the site's own pages, and is now listed as live error L4.
* **Hart dossier:** nothing overturned. It made no Beale's Cut claims. This dossier adds one Hart-related live error (L7) and confirms that no site page shows Hart filming at the Cut.
* **(2026-09-29, from the Reynolds dossier, correction 1) 'Straight Shooting as the first film at the Cut':** reynolds part59 was rewritten by Robert S. Birchard for the 1998 edition. As it stands it says Straight Shooting was Ford's 'first effort' for Universal and used Beale's Cut; it does not say it was the first film shot there. It is removed from the Reynolds error tally and from A4's outside-claim column. The earliest-use finding (Broken Ways, 1913) stands against Wikipedia. Fixed in place: the Section 1 row for part59, C16 and A4.
* **(2026-09-29, from the Reynolds dossier, correction 3) Reynolds chapter dates:** '©1998' on the signal/reynolds/partNN pages is the date of the SCVHS web edition (ed. Leon Worden). The texts were written 1976–94 (Signal series mid-1970s and mid-1980s; the 1992 book; the prologue and epilogue in 1985) and edited in 1998. Date columns now read 'web ed. ©1998; text written 1976–94'. Treat these chapters as later secondary accounts with an unknown per-chapter composition date.
