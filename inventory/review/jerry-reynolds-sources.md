Read-only research, compiled 2026-09-29 (CEST). 101 scvhistory.com URLs were fetched live on 2026-09-29 from 06:31 CEST, all HTTP 200. hs9029 (Wiley's Windlass) was not re-fetched; it is cited from the Beale dossier. **Privacy:** Reynolds is deceased (below), but this dossier still covers only his public work. The site's preface and lw2184 name family members; they are deliberately not repeated. No living private individuals are named; correspondents and donors on the pages are described, not named. **CARE:** Indigenous content is referenced without adding terms or locations; the Bowers Cave locational description is flagged (RL8). ## Summary

* **Living or deceased: DECEASED.** Born Gerald G. Reynolds, Torrance, CA, **July 16, 1937**; died **Feb 26, 1996**, of cancer, aged 58 (lw2184; the 1998 preface; lw2488). Independent corroboration: the Signal editorial of Apr 29, 1997 (rrsigtrb: he died 'last year'); Worden's Dec 1996 column (lw122296); and an SSDI/California Birth Index aggregator (sortedbyname.com) matching both dates and the birthplace. The obituary cited on the site is Carol Rock, 'SCV Historian Jerry Reynolds Dead at Age 58', The Signal, Feb 28, 1996 (not seen; Signal archive blocked). No Find a Grave record was found.
* **Reliability pattern.** Across the ledger (74 items), 38 Reynolds claims are contradicted by primary or better evidence, 2 are partly right, 1 is superseded, 8 conflict with other sources without resolution, 7 are unresolved, and 18 are confirmed. **Confirmed claims are overwhelmingly dates of major documented events** (16 of 18). **Errors cluster in** figures (acreage, money, ages, weights: 11), colourful anecdotes and dramatic endings (8), anachronistic titles and ranks (5), dates of lesser events (9), conflation of two people or events into one (3), and names (2). Many errors are inherited from an uncited secondary source (Wally Smith 1958; an 1889 county history; Van Valkenburgh's map), not invented.
* **How far to trust an uncorroborated Reynolds claim:** **dates of major events: provisionally yes** (cite with 'per Reynolds'). **Numbers, ages, money, acreage, superlatives ('first', 'only'), titles, and any anecdote with a vivid ending (morgues, burials, deathbed scenes, presidential visits, inventions): no**, not without a primary source. Watch for **two people merged into one** (the Nadeau, 'Buffalo' Vernon and Jenks Harris cases). No evidence of deliberate fabrication was found, but hs9029 says the 'Wiley's Windlass' illustration and story in his 1992 book were invented.
* **What the site holds:** the 1998 web book *History of the Santa Clarita Valley* (ed. Leon Worden, SCVHS): preface, prologue and epilogue (both 1985), 71 chapters, bibliography, and 8 notes. It descends from his Signal columns (1976–1994) and his book *Santa Clarita: Valley of the Golden Dream* (1992). The site also holds 7 other writings (1984–1993). **partNN.html = chapter NN of the 1998 web edition**, which is edited: some chapters were rewritten or adapted and some errors corrected silently. So 'Reynolds part NN' is not always Reynolds' own words (C5).
* **Counts:** 21 conflicts; 12 live errors; 8 archive-beats-outside; 6 corrections to earlier dossiers; 101 pages fetched (71 chapters + 9 front/back-matter and title pages + 21 others).

## 2. Lifetime primary vs later

* **Made in his lifetime (1937–1996):** gallery-hs7801 (1978 program naming him curator & historian); his own writings reynolds121484 (1984), the Presbyterian history (1986), the Kingsburry pieces (1989–93), hs9032 (1990), hs9101 (1991), the Saugus Café brochure (early 1990s, revised text online). Worden's columns lw061495 (1995) and lw122296 (Dec 1996, just after his death) are near-contemporary.
* **After his death:** the 1998 web book and preface (edited), lw2184, rrsigtrb (1997), sg123100a (2000), lw040997 (1997), lw090404 (2004), lw2585 (2014), lw3113 (2017), lw3550, lw2488.
* **As a source, Reynolds is secondary everywhere.** He was writing 1976–94 about events up to 200 years earlier, with no footnotes. The only place his method is visible is hs9032, where he labels his own conclusion 'conclusion and speculation' and reasons from maps, bricks and deeds. That is sound practice, and it is absent from the popular chapters.

### 2.3 Pattern of error

* **Contradicted/partly/superseded by type (41):** number 11, date 9, anecdote 8, label 5, conflation 3, name 2, quotation 1, superlative 1, care 1. **Confirmed by type (18):** date 16, number 1, anecdote 1.
1. **Numbers are the weakest point.** Acreage (254; 297,000; the impossible 13,599/21,307/4,684 partition; 16,599), money ($5,000; $11,000; $53,320), ages (Westover at 17), headcounts (8, 11 children), weights (2,350 lb). Some are internally inconsistent across his own chapters.
2. **Anecdotes with a dramatic payoff** are often wrong or unprovable: the cowboy-suit burial, the Hap-A-Lan morgue, the Death Valley camel inspiration, the Harrison meal at Saugus, the 'venereal disease' death, the Yant escape, Stagecoach at Melody Ranch, Wiley's Windlass.
3. **Anachronistic or inflated titles:** Naval Academy, mayor, state senator, 'Colonel' Porter, 'asistencia', 'first justice'.
4. **Conflation:** two Remi Nadeaus, 'Buffalo' Tom Vernon, the Jenks Harris robbery ('commingling a few different crime stories'), two Indian reserves.
5. **Dates of lesser events slip by a year or several** (Fustero 1916/1921; Harris 1925/1922; Lang 1873/1875; district 1875/1873; 1953/1954; Indianola). **Dates of major documented events are usually right** (Beale's birth, Fort Tejon's abandonment, the del Valle grant, gold discovery, deaths, Hi Jolly).
6. **Inheritance, not invention, is the usual mechanism.** Several errors trace to a named secondary source (Wally Smith 1958; the 1889 history; Van Valkenburgh). He sometimes contradicts himself across works (Mentry; the partition acreage).
7. **Positive record:** original building research (Kingsburry House 1878, which beat the assessor's 1911) and cautious labelling in his curator's reports (hs9032).

**Rule for CC:** accept an uncorroborated Reynolds **date for a major event** provisionally, attributed. **Do not** accept an uncorroborated figure, age, sum, acreage, rank or title, superlative, identity of a minor figure, or dramatic anecdote. Mark it NEEDS_VERIFICATION and look for Perkins, a newspaper or a deed first. When the 1998 web text and Reynolds' original differ (C5), cite the edition.

## 8. Corrections to earlier findings

* Beale dossier (ledger B12 in the brief's list, 'Straight Shooting as the first film at the Cut'): part59 was rewritten by Robert S. Birchard for the 1998 edition (preface), and as it stands it says Straight Shooting was Ford's 'first effort' for Universal and used Beale's Cut, not that it was the first film shot there. Withdraw it from the Reynolds error tally; treat any residual issue as the 1998 edition's.
* Hart dossier C (death toll): '450' is in Reynolds' own text ('at least 450 dead', part54), not only in 'the original 2003 column'. Reynolds died in 1996, so no 2003 column can be his; attribute that column to its actual author.
* All three dossiers dated Reynolds chapters 'web ©1998'. That is the edition date. The texts were written between 1976 and 1994 (the prologue and epilogue in 1985) and edited in 1998. Record them as later secondary accounts written 1976–94, with an unknown per-chapter date.
* Del Valle dossier C2 (mother's name 'María Josepha Carillo' on lw2052): the form traces to Reynolds part13 ('married María Josepha Carillo'), which rests on Wally Smith 1958 (notes page note 5). lw2052 is derivative, not independent.
* Del Valle dossier: Reynolds' Tejon statement in part26 (sold to Beale before 1861) was not logged. Add it as a contradicted claim (D14).
* Earlier references to the Signal series as 'Reynolds part NN': the site's partNN.html is chapter NN of the 1998 web book, which follows the Signal outline. It is not a numbered newspaper part, and no Signal part numbers are shown on the site.
