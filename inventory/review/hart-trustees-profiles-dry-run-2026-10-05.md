## Mary Bonelli (#28641), short

Mary P. Bonelli represented the Saugus School District on the first board of the William S. Hart Union High School District, elected on March 9, 1945, and by Leon Worden's roster of the board she was then a trustee of the Saugus district.[1]

## Thomas Frew Jr. (#28647), full

Thomas M. Frew Jr., also called Tom Frew III, ran the family's blacksmith shop on Spruce Street in Newhall after his father, Thomas McNaughton Frew.[1][2] Ruth Newhall's account of the family, as Tom Frew IV told it, has him about four years old when they arrived in the spring of 1900, and Leon Worden also gives 1900; a City of Santa Clarita staff report of 2011 has the father arriving in 1891.[2][1][3]

He served in the Army in the First World War and, by Ruth Newhall's account, spent it shoeing the horses that drew the artillery; he came home to find his father ill, and of the five brothers only he became the blacksmith.[2] Worden has him taking over the business on his father's death, which he dates to 1928.[1] The City's report says that in 1924 he converted the shop to a welding and machine shop.[3] The shop's sign in a photograph that Worden dates to 1936 reads "T.M. Frew & Son Practical Horseshoers & General Blacksmithing."[4]

He was a member of the Newhall Chamber of Commerce at its founding in February 1923, when he was nominated for secretary-treasurer and withdrew, and that March he was named to its juvenile welfare committee, whose duty was "to actively work with the Boy Scouts, Girl Scouts or Camp Fire Girls for higher citizenship."[5][6][7] In 1940 he was one of the trustees of the corporation that American Legion Post 507 formed to hold the land and money William S. Hart gave for the American Theater, by Bill Crowl's history of the theater.[8] By Leon Worden's account he served on the board of the Newhall School District for many years, several of them as its president, and had retired from it by 1945.[9][10]

He was one of the two Newhall members of the first board of the William S. Hart Union High School District, elected on March 9, 1945, and at its first meeting, on March 28, he was elected its president.[9][11] He is among the trustees in the yearbooks of 1949 and 1950, and the 1951 yearbook prints his name with "(resigned)", as the roster marks him on the board of 1951.[12][9]

In July 1958 Los Angeles County gave notice of its intention to buy 23.86 acres of the Frew property from him and others, for $82,500, as an addition to William S. Hart Park.[13] Worden gives his dates as January 31, 1895, to August 17, 1963; Ruth Newhall's account gives his birth year as 1896 and says he died in 1966.[1][2]

## Walter Cook (#28653), full

Walter Russell Cook came to Newhall in January 1920 and, with his wife Pearl, ran the Ridge View Dairy from the 1920s to the 1940s at the corner of Highway 6 (Sierra Highway) and San Fernando Road (now Newhall Avenue), a corner locals called "Cook corner."[1] Leon Worden's account, drawn from The Newhall Signal, gives his birth as November 26, 1896, in Riverside.[1] He married Mattie Pearl Walk, a schoolteacher, on her parents' land in the Newhall Pass on June 20, 1920.[1][2]

He was president of the Newhall-Saugus Kiwanis Club from its founding in 1928 until 1935, and was already Scoutmaster of Newhall Boy Scout Troop 2, which the Kiwanis came to sponsor.[1] A veteran of the First World War, he was a member of American Legion Post 507 and an active Mason, and helped bring about the county courthouse of 1932 on Market Street.[1]

He joined the board of the William S. Hart Union High School District in 1951, by Worden's roster of the board, and he is among the trustees in the yearbooks of 1951 and 1954.[3][4] The 1952 yearbook names him clerk of the board and the 1953 yearbook its president.[5][6] As president he gave the welcome and accepted the building at the dedication of the Henry Mayo Newhall Auditorium on September 12, 1952.[7][8]

By Worden's account from the Signal he died of a stroke in a Sunday school room at Newhall's First Presbyterian Church on May 9, 1954, at 57, while still serving on the board.[1] The roster lists him on the board of 1954 and not on that of 1955.[3] At the regular election two weeks after his death the only name on the ballot for his seat was Julio Lombardi's, and Lombardi won, 302 to 224, over write-in votes for Pearl Cook.[1]

## Chester Allen (#28655), short

## C. R. Huntsinger (#28673), full

Curtis R. Huntsinger was "an Acton turkey rancher by profession," in The Signal's words of 1970, and had served on the board of the Saugus elementary school district before the Hart board.[1]

Leon Worden's roster of the board of the William S. Hart Union High School District first lists him on the board of 1962, and the 1962 yearbook names him its assistant clerk; The Signal said in September 1970 that he "was in the midst of his tenth year in office."[2][3][1] He is president of the board in the yearbooks of 1964, 1965 and 1968, and clerk in that of 1967.[4] As board president he was grand marshal of the homecoming parade recorded in the 1965 yearbook.[5] When he ran again in the spring of 1969, The Signal reported, he "garnered the largest vote total of any school candidate in local election history," and he had won his last three elections with ease.[1]

On September 15, 1970, voters recalled him and Edward Duarte from the board, Huntsinger by 2,450 votes to 2,323 in the unofficial count.[1][2] Worden's note on the Signal's report names, as one element of the recall movement, the board's reversal, led by Huntsinger, of Superintendent C. T. Haan's decision to let Canyon High School students demonstrate and circulate petitions against the war in Vietnam the October before, and suggests that the board's plans to unify the valley's schools, rejected by voters in 1960, 1965 and 1967, may also have hurt the incumbents.[6]

## Earl Schmidt (#28675), full

Earl Schmidt grew up from 1918 on his parents' 6-S Ranch north of Soledad Canyon Road, where the North Oaks tract is today, and in 1946 he and his brother Helm Jr. opened an airport there, the 6-S Ranch Airpark, by the account of his granddaughter, Cassandra Skaggs.[1][2] Shell Progress described the airpark in May 1947, with a runway of 2,750 feet and seventeen planes based on the field.[2] He drove a tanker truck for the Newhall oil refinery until the ranch was sold in 1956, then moved to Castaic and managed the hog farm at the Wayside Honor Rancho until he retired, Skaggs writes.[1]

He was a director of the water agency formed in 1962 as the Upper Santa Clara Valley Water Agency, renamed the Castaic Lake Water Agency in 1970, from 1962 to 1979, and the agency named its Earl Schmidt Filtration Plant on Lake Hughes Road for him.[3][1] He also served on the Los Angeles County Sheriff's Auxiliary Force.[1]

He sat on the board of the William S. Hart Union High School District from 1962, by Leon Worden's roster of the board, which marks him resigned on the board of 1969; Skaggs gives the same years.[4][1] He is among the trustees in the yearbooks of 1962 and 1964 to 1969, and the Sierra Vista Junior High School yearbook of 1965 names him clerk of the board.[5][6]

Skaggs gives his birth as January 29, 1906, in Los Angeles, and says he was killed in 1979 when a tree struck him at his vacation property at Donner Lake.[1]

## Jereann Bowman (#28677), full

Jereann Bowman pressed for a continuation high school in the William S. Hart Union High School District, and when it opened, on September 29, 1969, next to Canyon High School, the district's board named it for her: Jereann Bowman High School.[1][2] A profile in The Signal in 1976, as her obituary quotes it, said she had "a place in her heart for the non-conforming and felt there should be a school where youngsters could have more freedom" than in traditional schools.[1]

Her obituary gives her years on the Hart board as 1963 to 1969, and so does the Bowman High School yearbook of 1996.[1][3] Leon Worden's roster of the board lists her on the boards of 1964 to 1967 and of 1969, when it marks her resigned, and not on those of 1963 or 1968; the Hart High and Sierra Vista yearbooks of 1968 also leave her off the board.[4][5][6] Yearbooks of 1965 and 1966 list her, as Mrs. Leland Bowman, with the board, and the dedication program of Canyon High School, on October 15, 1969, lists her among the trustees.[7][8][9]

She was secretary-manager of the Newhall-Saugus-Valencia Chamber of Commerce, which named her its Woman of the Year for 1973.[1][10][11] In November 1962, by Leon Worden's history of the hospital auxiliary, she, Martha Barton and Gwen Gillespie drafted the bylaws of the Santa Clarita Valley Women's Auxiliary, the volunteers organized after a Greyhound bus crash near Castaic that October.[12] During the Second World War, her obituary says, she conducted medical research for the Army, and she later held several county jobs.[1]

She was born on March 13, 1921, the daughter of Judge C.M. MacDougall, whom her obituary calls a pioneer of Newhall and Saugus.[1][13] Her obituary says she came to Saugus in 1927, and the 1996 yearbook that she came to Newhall that year; the 1981 yearbook's dedication says she moved to Newhall in 1920.[1][3][2] After she retired she moved to Murphys, in Northern California, where she died on March 22, 1995, at 74.[1]

## Elisha Agajanian (#28679), full

Elisha J. Agajanian, known as Aggie, founded Santa Clarita National Bank, which opened in the mid-1960s, and he was its chairman until he retired in 1975.[1][2][3] In 1953 a Champion Spark Plug advertisement pictured him as the manager of the Agajanian hog ranch near Saugus, a 170-acre farm in Haskell Canyon.[4] Leon Worden, on the word of Nazareth Chobanian and Michael Trueblood, writes that he founded Blue Barrel Disposal Co. to haul food waste to the farm and sold it in the 1960s.[4]

He was a director of the Upper Santa Clara Valley Water Agency, renamed the Castaic Lake Water Agency in 1970, from 1967 to 1974, by the list of the agency's directors on SCVHistory.com, and Worden writes that he sat on that board and the Hart board at the same time.[5][4] Leon Worden's roster of the William S. Hart Union High School District board lists him on its boards of 1968 through 1971, and the Hart High and Sierra Vista yearbooks of 1968 and the dedication program of Canyon High School, on October 15, 1969, list him among the trustees.[6][7][8][9]

In 1970 he was among the members of the committee that reorganized as the board of Henry Mayo Newhall Memorial Hospital, by Worden's history of the hospital, and in 1985 the hospital honored him among its founders.[10][11] For College of the Canyons' bond election of February 6, 1973, he and Blake V. Blakey headed the Citizens' Committee to Complete College of the Canyons, a group of 40 community leaders.[12] The College of the Canyons Foundation gave him its Silver Spur award in 1990.[13][4]

In November 1993, at the Newhall County Water District's first election in a decade, he kept his seat on its board, third of six candidates for three seats, with 1,592 votes.[14][15]

## Louis Brathwaite (#28703), full

Louis E. Brathwaite was a member of the City Formation Committee in the drive that made Santa Clarita a city in 1987, and from 1988 he sat on the city's first Planning Commission.[1][2][3] In January 1987, as a former Hart School District board member and the owner of a computer furniture firm, he told The Signal, "I feel that the area is growing, and that growth should continue," and that the people affected by it "should have more of a direct say about it."[4] His obituary, quoting Carl Boyer, one of the committee's two chairs, credits him with persuading Supervisor Kenneth Hahn to support the city's application and to attend a decisive meeting, and with securing $3 million from the county for the new city.[1]

By September 1987 the Los Angeles Times called him a former member of the formation committee, and he stood for the first City Council on November 3, 1987, coming eighth of 26 candidates for five seats, with 3,408 votes.[5][6] His candidate statement gave his occupation as federal property administrator and his qualifications as "an elected school board member, a director of the Boys and Girls clubs and Cityhood Formation Committee officer."[7] In January 1988 Councilman Carl Boyer appointed him to the new Planning Commission.[3][1] The list of commissioners on SCVHistory.com places him on the commission from 1988 to 1996; his obituary says he remained on it until he retired in 1998, and that no one else had served as long, and Boyer's history of the city says he served longer than any other commissioner.[8][1][9] In April 1996 he stood for the council again and came sixth of thirteen candidates for two seats.[6][10]

Before the city, he served on the William S. Hart Union High School District board. By Boyer's account in the obituary, he was on the parent advisory committee of Saugus High School when he was asked to run, and he won.[1] Leon Worden's roster of the board lists him from April 1977, as clerk from April 12, 1977, as president from July 1978 to November 1979, and as a member until November 1981.[11] The college newspaper, The Canyon Call, names him clerk of the board when he was master of ceremonies of College of the Canyons' Black History program in the 1977-78 school year.[12] Clyde Smyth, then the district's superintendent, recalled that "He was on the board when we went through some very hard times financially."[1]

He sat on the board of the Boys and Girls Clubs of the Santa Clarita Valley, where, by Smyth's account, he chaired the committee that oversaw the club's facilities, and on the board of the SCV Committee on Aging, whose executive director credited him with the idea of the Bouquet Canyon Seniors housing.[13][1] Boyer recalled that he had been president of the Monteverde Homeowners Association, and that when he first ran for office Ruth Newhall, then editor of The Signal, proposed to present him as the first black candidate for political office in the Santa Clarita Valley, and he asked not to be described that way.[1]

He was born in New York City on April 25, 1933, and moved to the Santa Clarita Valley in 1969; he held management posts with the U.S. Air Force for 38 years, retiring in 1990, and the Los Angeles Times described him in 1987 and 1988 as a federal property administrator.[1][5][3] A week before his death he completed an autobiography, "Black Man's Job, White Man's World."[1] He died in Santa Clarita on November 13, 2001, at 68.[1]

## Clara Stroup (#28713), full

Clara Jean Stroup was executive director of the Association to Aid Victims of Domestic Violence, which the Los Angeles Times described in 1993 as the only agency of its kind in the Santa Clarita Valley; she was then its only paid full-time employee.[1] She had been a member of the association's board of directors in 1990, and was still its executive director in 1994.[2][3] Her family's statement, printed with her obituary, credits her with securing a safe house for the people the association served.[4]

Hart Union High School District board from 1979 to 1991.[5][4] Leon Worden's roster records her election in 1979 and her re-election in 1983 and 1987; she was the board's clerk in 1981 and 1988-89, its assistant clerk in 1987-88, and its president in 1982, 1985-86 and 1989-90.[5] As president in April 1986, when the board took up a year-round school calendar, she said, "We really have an obligation to the people in the community to look at this carefully."[6] In November 1987 she was re-elected, second of four candidates for two seats, with 8,364 votes, 30.3 percent.[7] In 1991 she stood for a fourth term and came fifth of five candidates for two seats, with 2,827 votes, 16.7 percent.[8][9][10]

## Linda Storli (#25157), full

Linda Storli taught at Canyon High School for 30 years and then represented Trustee Area 1 on the William S. Hart Union High School District board for nine years, from December 2015 to December 2024.[1][2] She was the board's president from December 2019 to December 2020, and again for 2024.[2][3]

## Paul Strickland (#25425), full

Paul Strickland served on two of the valley's school boards: the Sulphur Springs Union School District's from 1995 to 1999, and the William S. Hart Union High School District's from December 2001 until May 2013, when he resigned with plans to move out of state.[1][2] By Leon Worden's roster of the Hart board he was its president in 2005-06 and 2009-10.[3]

He came to the boards from animation: he gave his occupation as animation producer and director when he was elected at Sulphur Springs in 1995, and as parent and animation producer in 2001, and SCVNews.com wrote in 2011 that he had "a 30-year background in the film and television industry as an animator."[4][5]

At Sulphur Springs he was elected in November 1995, second of three candidates, with 1,149 votes.[1] He first stood for the Hart board in 1999 and came third of seven, with 4,193 votes; he was elected in 2001, second of six, with 11,105, and re-elected in 2005, second of eight, with 18,802, and in 2009, second of five, with 8,251.[1]

In 2005 he listed himself as president of the Santa Clarita Arts Advisory Committee and co-chair of the Hart district's ad hoc committee on diversity.[6] He was a founding member of the City of Santa Clarita's Arts Commission, on which the district gives his service as beginning in December 2009, and was appointed its chairman in September 2011; he still chaired it when he resigned in 2013.[7][2] The district's account of him also names his work on the bond committees for Measures V and SA, his membership of the city's Open Space Commission and his opposition to the Cemex mine.[8] He hosted a weekly arts program on KHTS AM-1220 and SCVTV, called "Thursday Matinee" in 2011 and 2012 and "Friday Matinee" in 2013.[9][10][11]

In September 2011, while he was field deputy to Assemblyman Cameron Smyth, he announced that he would run as a Republican for Smyth's seat in the redrawn 38th Assembly District.[12] The California Teachers Association endorsed him in March 2012, and in the primary of June 5, 2012 he finished out of the top two, with 14.7 percent of the vote.[13][14]

His resignation from the Hart board took effect on May 1, 2013.[15][16] Leon Worden's roster also lists him on the board of December 2013 as re-elected unopposed that year but not assuming office; the district's account names the three candidates who filed for the board that year as Chris Fall, Joseph Messina and Robert Jensen Jr.[17][18]

## Patricia Hanrion (#25437), short

Patricia Hanrion sat on the William S. Hart Union High School District board for sixteen years, from December 1993 to December 2009, and by Leon Worden's roster she was its president in 1996-97, 2001-02 and 2006-07.[1][2]

## Robert Hall (#28723), short

Robert Hall, director of human resources at Advanced Bionics in Valencia, sat on the William S. Hart Union High School District board from October 2013 to February 2017, and was its president in 2016.[1][2][3]

The board chose him by a vote of 3-1 on October 16, 2013, from eleven candidates, to fill the seat Chris Fall had left, Gloria Mercado-Fortine voting against.[4][1] Because of the timing of Fall's resignation, the appointment was for a four-year term, beginning at the board's organizational meeting of December 11, 2013.[4][1][5] The district's account of him in 2013 noted his work linking Advanced Bionics with the district's Regional Occupational Program and his service on the board of the Valley Industrial Association.[1]

He was the board's assistant clerk in 2015.[6] He resigned effective February 1, 2017, to take a post with his company in Singapore.[3][7]
