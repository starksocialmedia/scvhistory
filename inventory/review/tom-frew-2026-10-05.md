# Tom Frew: one man or two? (5 October 2026)

Claude Code, read-only research. No database writes. Records: "Tom Frew" (#18783) and "Thomas Frew Jr." (#28647).

## Verdict

**Two different men, father and son.** #28647 is Tom Frew III, called Thomas M. Frew Jr. in his lifetime (born 1895 or 1896, died 1963 or 1966). #18783 is his son, Tom Frew IV (born Thanksgiving Day 1928, left the valley in April 2003). The "Tom Frew II" of tf1000 is a third man, the grandfather (1852 to 1928), and has no record.

The decision Nathan made on 3 October, to keep the two records apart, stands. The portrait census on 5 October said they were "possibly one man". That note was wrong and should be withdrawn. One thing in the 3 October notes is also wrong: they say "no source joins them". The sources do join them, as father and son.

## The decisive evidence

1. **Ruth Newhall, "The Accidental Blacksmiths Of Old Newhall," Old Town Newhall Gazette, December 1996** (`frew1296.htm`). This is the family's own account, told by Tom IV. It names three generations and makes IV the son of III:
   - "That was Tom Frew II. Today his grandson, Thomas Frew IV, tells the story of his grandfather"
   - "Thomas III was born in 1896, so he was about four years old when they came to Newhall."
   - "in 1927, when Tom was 30 and Patsy was 28, they were married. Tom IV was born on Thanksgiving Day, 1928."
   - "His father was ill and said, 'I need your help in the shop.' ... Tom III died in 1966"
2. **Pacific Telephone directory, Newhall, January 1958** (`files/hb5801`, page 12). It lists the two men at the same time, at different addresses: "Frew TM IV 24327 N Newhl ... Newhall 1114"; "FREW TM INC 24313NSnFrndoRd ... Newhall 35-W"; "Frew Thos M Jr 24321NWalnt ... Newhall 35-R". Of all the sources, this is the only one written in their lifetime that sets the two apart.
3. **HS9019, "City Council Honors Tom Frew," April 8, 2003** (`hs9019.htm`). This page identifies the man of #18783: "Former SCV Historical Society President Tom Frew (third from right) receives a key to the city". It continues: "Tom's grandfather, Tom Frew II, arrived in 1900 and took over Newhall's blacksmith shop, and passed down the business to his son and, in time, to his grandson ... Tom Frew IV."
4. **Leon Worden, AP0724 / TF1000 / AP1603** (same note on all three). This note gives the man of #28647 the "Jr." name: "One was Tom III, aka Thomas Frew Jr. (Jan. 31, 1895 - Aug. 17, 1963), took over the family business upon his father's death". It continues: "In like fashion, Tom IV later ran the shop, which he ultimately shut in 1970."
5. **Leon Worden, "History of Newhall School"** (`newhallschool.htm`). It names the trustee as III: "Tom Frew III and S.S. Donaldson represented the Newhall district." HS5802 (1958 County report, Worden's note) says the same: "Sellers of the property were Thomas M. Frew III (aka Thomas Frew Jr.)".

No source on the mirror calls Tom IV "Jr." Every "Jr." found falls between 1923 and 1958, and each one fits Tom III.

## The family

The family's own numbering starts in Newhall at II. AP0724 says: "Tom Frew II (Newhall's first Tom Frew, at right)". No Tom I appears in any Newhall source. In local use of the 1910s to 1950s, II was "Sr." and III was "Jr.". The 1923 Chamber roster lists "33 — T.M. Frew Sr. 34 — T.M. Frew Jr.", and the Signal's 1949 Swall obituary has "T.M. Frew Sr.'s blacksmithing shop" in the 1914 highway story.

## Errors in the archive

1. **AP1535, TF2601a and TF2601b (Worden, on the 1936 shop photograph)**: "Did we mention that Tom Frew II was on the Newhall School Board at the time?" By Worden's own dates, Tom II had died in 1928. The board president of the 1930s was Tom III ("T.M. Frew Jr., president of the board", 1933). This sentence mixes up two generations. Do not carry it into either record. The 5 October draft profile already leaves it out.
2. **Tom III's dates**: Worden has "Jan. 31, 1895 - Aug. 17, 1963". Ruth Newhall has "born in 1896" and "died in 1966". Her "in 1927, when Tom was 30" fits 1896 or early 1897. Neither source cites a document. Both should stay, as the draft proposes.
3. **Evangeline's birth year**: Worden has "b. 1872 in Wisconsin". Ruth Newhall has her as the "24-year-old wife" in 1900, which would put her birth about 1876.
4. **Year of arrival**: 1900 in Worden, Ruth Newhall, Perkins, the SCVHS 2000 minutes and HS5802. 1891, "on Railroad Avenue", in the City's 1991 General Plan and its 2011 staff report.
5. **Who sold the shop**: Perkins says Sam Smith. Ruth Newhall says the sale came through Gulley. Worden flags the conflict ("someone is wrong about that").
6. **Gladys Laney profile, Signal, August 16, 2000 (sg081600)**: "Tom Frew IV, the son of Laney's Aunt Evangaline Atlantis". This conflicts with Ruth Newhall: Tom IV's mother was Clara "Patsy" Hofer, Evangeline was his grandmother, and "Aunt Vangie" was his aunt. Treat this as a garble and do not use it.
7. **"Thomas F. Frew, Jr."** in the Yant appellate case of 1935-36 (via Pollack, pollack0317yant). The F is probably a misprint for M. The date and "and wife" fit Tom III, who married in 1927. Identity rests on the name only.
8. **"Thomas E. Frew becomes a member"**, 1904, in the First Presbyterian timeline (hs8601). The E is probably a misprint, and the man is probably Tom II. Not resolved.
9. **AP0724 and TF1000**: "upon his father's death (in Lasalle, Ill., where he is buried)". The grammar does not make clear whose death took place in La Salle.
10. **1929 directory**: "Thomas M. Frew, Spruce" is listed in a directory "revised to January 19, 1929", after Worden's death date for Tom II (August 11, 1928). Directory lag is the likely reason. This is noted only.
11. **Craft relation**: "71. Requiem" (#2167) is linked to #18783 (Tom IV), but the text names "Tom Frew II". The link is wrong.
12. **Portrait census note on #28647 and #18783**: "possibly one man in two records". Superseded by this report.

## Recommended action: keep both records and make each one plainly a different generation

### #18783 "Tom Frew": becomes Tom Frew IV

- **Title** "Tom Frew IV". **fullName** "Thomas M. Frew IV". The M rests on the 1958 directory ("Frew TM IV") and the 2000 memo ("Thomas M. Frew").
- **Aliases** "Tom Frew IV", "Thomas Frew IV", "T.M. Frew IV". Do not add "Tom Frew" or "Thomas M. Frew" as aliases: II and III share them.
- **Birth** 1928, retrospective. Ruth Newhall's text is "born on Thanksgiving Day, 1928", a family account. Thanksgiving fell on November 29 that year, but that would be our inference, not the source's date. Recommend EDTF `1928-11`, with the quotation in a footnote. Death: not found.
- **Relations**:
  - Remove #2167 "71. Requiem", which is about Tom II.
  - Keep #817 Preface, #2171 Bibliography, #12422, #12252 and #12184.
  - Add photograph #2743 (LW2043, 1998) as a subject link.
- **Affiliations (proposal)**, with Santa Clarita Valley Historical Society (#15493), on the pattern of #29098:
  - president, by April 1997, end not given ("Former ... President" by 2003);
  - director, 1998 (LW2043);
  - corresponding secretary, 2000 to at least February 2001.
  - Newhall Redevelopment Committee from 1996 (HS9019; no body record checked).
- **Editor's note** (replaces "Two men named Thomas Frew"):
  > This is Tom Frew IV, born in 1928, the grandson of Tom Frew II, who bought the Spruce Street blacksmith shop in 1900, and the son of Tom Frew III. He closed the shop in 1970, served as president of the Santa Clarita Valley Historical Society in the late 1990s, and left the valley in 2003 (Ruth Newhall, "The Accidental Blacksmiths Of Old Newhall," Old Town Newhall Gazette, December 1996, /scvhistory/frew1296.htm; HS9019, /scvhistory/hs9019.htm). His father, called Thomas M. Frew Jr. in his lifetime, has his own record, Thomas Frew Jr.; the two are listed separately in the Newhall telephone directory of January 1958 ("Frew TM IV"; "Frew Thos M Jr", /scvhistory/hb5801.htm). Not to be confused with him.

### #28647 "Thomas Frew Jr.": stays Tom Frew III

- **Title**: keep "Thomas Frew Jr.", the name he used. Alternatively use "Thomas M. Frew Jr.", which matches the roster and the draft heading. Either is fine; recommend the draft's "Thomas M. Frew Jr.".
- **Aliases**: add "Tom Frew III" and "Tom Frew Jr.". Keep "Thomas M. Frew Jr." and "T.M. Frew Jr.". Drop the bare "Thomas M. Frew": it names Tom II in 1929 and Tom IV in 2000, so it would pull their pages into his counts. The aliases dry run of 5 October would drop all three of the current aliases for a different reason. The alias policy is Nathan's call, but "Tom Frew III" is the alias that keeps the two men apart.
- **Dates**: as the 5 October draft proposes, birth {1895-01-31, 1896} and death {1963-08-17, 1966}, both retrospective.
- **Body**: apply the 5 October draft (Hart trustees batch A). It already reads him as Tom III and cites the right sources.
- **Holdings (proposal)**: keep #28731 (Hart, 1945-03-09 to 1951, resigned). Add a Newhall School District holding. The end is September 1941: "The occasion also marked the close of nine years of service of Frew" at the auditorium dedication of September 16, 1941 (sg113003b, a 2003 retrospective). The start is about 1932, from "nine years", and should be marked approximate. Note that he was president in 1933, 1939 and 1940. The district body is the one the existing Newhall holdings use, for example #28451.
- **Editor's note** (replaces "Two men named Thomas Frew"):
  > This is Tom Frew III, called Thomas M. Frew Jr. in his lifetime, the son of Tom Frew II, who bought the Spruce Street blacksmith shop in 1900, and the father of Tom Frew IV (Ruth Newhall, "The Accidental Blacksmiths Of Old Newhall," Old Town Newhall Gazette, December 1996, /scvhistory/frew1296.htm; Leon Worden, AP0724, /scvhistory/ap0724.htm: "One was Tom III, aka Thomas Frew Jr."). His son, the Historical Society president of the 1990s, has his own record, Tom Frew IV. Not to be confused with him.
- Remove the portrait census's "possible duplicate" line.

## Method

- Both records and their incoming relations were read with read-only Craft queries (`storage/runtime/_frew*.php`).
- All Craft content naming "Frew" was searched: 3 entries carry the name in their titles, and about 30 more mention it in their content.
- The mirror's full-text index (`storage/runtime/pc/index.jsonl`, 33,690 pages) was searched for "frew": 185 pages. Pages that only carry the Hart Park navigation ("Frew Addition 1958") were set aside.
