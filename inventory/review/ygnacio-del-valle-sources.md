## Summary

* **Vitals.** Born **July 1, 1808** in Nueva Galicia (the site, the 1889 Pen Pictures, Wikipedia, JoinCalifornia and NHM say 'Jalisco'; one genealogy says Compostela, now Nayarit, which was then in the same province). Died **March 30, 1880**, at Rancho Camulos (Reynolds; the Wikipedia infobox; JoinCalifornia's 'Los Angeles' is unsupported). Age at death **71**, not the 72 on two site pages. First buried at Camulos; a parish burial entry of Apr 1, 1880 at Mission San Buenaventura is reported (NEEDS_VERIFICATION). The 1905 move to Calvary is family tradition ('Or so the story goes', lp1501).
* **Antonio vs Ygnacio.** **Antonio** Seferino del Valle (1788–1841) was the grantee of Rancho San Francisco (Alvarado, **Jan 22, 1839**; the Land Case also recites an 1833 Sespe grant). He died intestate in June 1841 (the 21st by most sources; 'June 12' in Perkins). Ygnacio, his son by his first marriage, held an **undivided share** (5/11 by the 1850s) against his stepmother Jacoba Feliz, her second husband José Salazar and her children, until the **1870 partition** set off Camulos (about **1,340 acres**) to him.
* **Land case.** Claim by Jacoba Feliz et al. filed Sept 2, 1852; confirmed Jan 2, 1855; appeal dismissed June 8, 1857; **patent Feb 12, 1875 for 48,611.88 acres** (Bancroft Land Case 303 SD).
* **Sale.** Debts to Wolfskill (1858–64) and the 1862–64 drought led to the **1865 sale to Thomas R. Bard** (for the oil interests; $47,519.71 on Mar 18 per Perkins, or $53,320 on Apr 29 per Reynolds and Robinson). Henry M. Newhall bought the rest on **Jan 15, 1875 for $90,000**, from the post-1873 holders, **not from Ygnacio**. **Nothing seen supports a del Valle sale to Jenkins.**
* **Offices.** Junta member (1845), departmental treasurer (1846), **alcalde of Los Angeles in 1850**, County Recorder (1850), **Assembly in the 1852 session** (elected Sept 3, 1851; not the 1849–50 first Legislature the site claims), and LA Common Council 1852–53 and 1856. He was never mayor of the incorporated city.
* **Camulos adobe.** 1853 (NRHP 1996) is best supported; the site also carries 1841/1846 (HABS/Rubel), 1854–61 (Robinson), a pre-existing adobe (Reynolds) and '1862'. Ygnacio moved there permanently in 1861.
* **Counts:** 21 conflict groups; 12 live errors on the site; 9 archive-beats-outside; 10 corrections (3 added 2026-09-29 from the Reynolds dossier); 71 pages classified plus 49 peripheral pages checked.
* **Papers, verified:** NHM Seaver Center **GC 1002** Del Valle Family Papers 1789–1929 (and P-14 photographs); **Rancho Camulos Museum** del Valle Family Papers 1847–1924 (OAC). **Huntington:** no del Valle collection found (the catalog returned 403), but the Abel Stearns papers cover the 1842 gold and ranch business.

## 2. Lifetime primary sources vs later accounts

Of the 71 classified pages, **13** carry an item made within his lifetime: lastar18640514wolfskill (legal notice + editor's note, 5-14-1864 (notice); note undated); hl7101 (map + essay, 1871 (map)); gallery-lw2706 (ledger (original found at Camulos 2014), June-July 1853); lw2570a (diseño (reproduction, NHM exhibit), early 1840s (map); NHM signage 1854); lw2570b (diseño detail, early 1840s); lw2570c (diseño detail, early 1840s); lw2526a (branding iron (NHM exhibit), 1830s-40s (object)); lw3903 (tax certificate + boilerplate text, 1876 (certificate); photographed 3-17-2023); lw2463a (building photos + boilerplate, 1867 (building); photos 2013); lw2633a (object photos + boilerplate, c.1867-1900 (object)); dac18530727camulos (newspaper, 7-27-1853); venturasignal18790712vc (newspaper, 7-12-1879); gallery-lw2792 (object photos, ~1850 (buggy); photos 2014). Everything else is later.

Key reliability notes:
* **gallery-lw2706** (1853 livestock ledger, found at Camulos in 2014) is the only del Valle-generated document of the period on the site. It shows the undivided family holding (accounts for Jacoba Feliz, the younger children, 'Ygnacio y Magdalena' and María).
* **lastar18640514wolfskill** (LA Star, 1864 sheriff's-sale notice) is the key contemporary legal item: Wolfskill v. Salazar, Feliz de Salazar, Ignacio del Valle et al.
* **perkins part03** transcribes the Apr 4, 1842 Lopez petition and Arguello's May 3, 1842 appointment of Ygnacio (originals at the Bancroft, per Perkins).
* **Perkins 1957** (perkins-rsf-1957) is later, but it is the only site text built from the probate, deed and court record. Use it as the guide to originals. **Reynolds (written 1976–94; book 1992; web ed. 1998)** chapters 14–15 rest on Wally Smith's 'The Camulos Story' (1958) and carry the impossible partition figures. **Pen Pictures (1889)** is the nearest-to-life biography but has errors (age 72; 'six children' vs 12 born).
* **The Camulos boilerplate** (1,500 acres; 'two families since Mexican independence'; 1853 adobe) is repeated on at least 5 pages, unsourced.

## 3. Conflicts

### C1. Birth date and place; age at death

* lw2052, reynolds part14, penpictures_ydelvalle (1889): born July 1, 1808, Jalisco
* times111101 and penpictures_rdelvalle (1889): died 1880 'at age 72' / 'at the age of seventy-two years'; the lw2752 tree carries a handwritten '72 yrs'
* reynolds part14: 'the lad of seventeen' landed at Monterey July 27, 1825 (fits 1808)
* **Outside:** Wikipedia: July 1, 1808, New Galicia (Jalisco). Wikidata Q8053412: P569 1808-07-01, P19 Jalisco (unreferenced). JoinCalifornia: July 1, 1808, Jalisco. NHM GC 1002 finding aid: 'Ygnacio (1808-80), born in Jalisco'. Schwald family genealogy (secondary): 'Abt. 1808, Compostela, Nayarit'.
* **Evidence favors:** July 1, 1808 is consistent everywhere. Place: 'Jalisco' and 'Compostela' can both be right, because Compostela lay in Nueva Galicia (the Guadalajara intendancy) in 1808 and is in today's Nayarit. Use 'Nueva Galicia (now Jalisco/Nayarit)' until a baptismal entry is seen: NEEDS_VERIFICATION. Age at death: born July 1, 1808 and died March 30, 1880 makes him **71**, not 72.

### C2. Mother's name

* lw2052: 'María Josepha (Carillo) del Valle'
* gallery-lw2706 (1853 ledger caption): 'his first wife, Maria Josepha Carillo'
* reynolds part13 (written 1976–94; web ed. 1998): Antonio 'married María Josepha Carillo and had two children, Ygnacio and María'. This is the earliest occurrence of the form on the site. lw2052 and the 2014 lw2706 caption are later and very likely derivative. Part13 names no source; the editor documents Wally Smith's 'The Camulos Story' (1958) as the basis only for chs. 14–15 (notes page, note 5). [added 2026-09-29]
* **Outside:** Schwald family genealogy: mother 'Josefa de la Peña'. Wikipedia and Wikidata do not name her.
* **Evidence favors:** Unresolved: NEEDS_VERIFICATION. The site's 'Carillo' may come from confusion with Ygnacio's first wife, María de los Angeles Carrillo. Do not migrate a maiden name until a sacramental record is seen.

### C3. Antonio's children and the heirs (Magdalena; how many)

* perkins-rsf-1957 text: Antonio 'mourned by his widow, two children from his first marriage, and four children from his second marriage'; fn 26: Jacoba bore 'five children'; 'Ygnacio and sister Magdalena, by first wife'
* reynolds part14: 'María remained in Mexico'; lists Jacoba's household as José Antonio (called illegitimate), Victor, newborn Magdalena, unborn José Ygnacio, then María Concepción (born four months after Antonio's death): 'a total of eight children'
* reynolds part15 / lw2052: Jacoba had 'four sons and two daughters'; 'each of her six children received 4,684 acres'
* lw2752: Ygnacio was 'one of seven children'
* gallery-lw2706: Magdalena was Jacoba's daughter and Ygnacio's half-sister; María was a first-wife daughter
* **Outside:** Wikipedia is silent on the count. NHM GC 1002: Antonio Seferino del Valle (1788–1841).
* **Evidence favors:** Most site sources make Magdalena Jacoba's daughter (Perkins fn 26 is the outlier). Count of heirs: 6 to 8, NEEDS_VERIFICATION. Perkins says Ygnacio bought Magdalena's share for about $6,000.

### C4. Antonio del Valle's death date

* reynolds part14, lw2052, sg090199a, gallery-lo3401 (editor's note): June 21, 1841
* perkins-rsf-1957: 'June 12, 1841'
* gallery-lo3401 (HABS 1934 text): 'until his death in 1841 or 1842'
* **Outside:** NHM GC 1002 gives the year only (1841).
* **Evidence favors:** 1841 is certain (the April 4, 1842 gold petition, transcribed on perkins part03, calls him 'the late Don Antonio del Valle'). Day: June 21 is the majority reading; June 12 may be a transposition. NEEDS_VERIFICATION.

### C5. Antonio vs Ygnacio: who held what, and from when (grant basis)

* reynolds part14: Alvarado signed the grant on January 22, 1839; the deathbed offer to Ygnacio was only 'the place extending from the portsuelo ... towards the west', half a Santa Barbara house and 300 cattle
* perkins-rsf-1957: Antonio 'petitioned ... January 22, 1839'; a further petition of April 5, 1839 claims a right 'from about 1825, with further rights as of 1833'; possible del Valle grazing from 1824; the Mission protested a Camulos grant to Francisco Avila in 1804
* lw2052 / gallery-lw2706: 'formal possession' January 22, 1839, having held it 'since about 1824'
* lw3903 boilerplate: Camulos 'owned by just two families since the end of war for Mexican independence' (1821)
* penpictures_ydelvalle: 'In 1841 he settled on the San Francisco Rancho'; reynolds part15: he did not live there until 1861
* sg090199a: Antonio 'a Mexican-born missionary who owned 48,000 acres'
* lw2526a: NHM signage 'Owned by Antonio del Valle / 48,612 acres / 1834-1844' (the page questions the dates)
* **Outside:** Bancroft/Calisphere description of Land Case 303 SD: two grants to Antonio del Valle: Sespe, by Figueroa, Nov 20, 1833 (6 leagues), and San Francisco, by Alvarado, Jan 22, 1839 (about 4 leagues). Wikipedia: Antonio's deathbed letter offered Ygnacio 'several properties, including the 48,612-acre Rancho San Francisco' (cited to sg090199a).
* **Evidence favors:** **Antonio** was the grantee (Jan 22, 1839). **Ygnacio** held an undivided heir's share from 1841–42 (5/11 by the 1850s, per Robinson), and after the 1870 partition held Camulos alone. Earlier del Valle occupancy (1824/1825/1833) is claimed, not proven. How the 1833 Sespe grant relates to the 11-league total can only be settled from the case file: NEEDS_VERIFICATION. Wikipedia's 'offered him Rancho San Francisco' overstates the offer (see A9).

### C6. Size and boundaries of Rancho San Francisco

* reynolds part14 and lw2052: 'eleven leagues, or 48,829 acres' (11 × 4,439 acres)
* times111101, lastar18640514wolfskill, hl7101, lw2526a signage: 48,612 acres; ra20070929, lw3903 and others: '48,000 acres'
* perkins-rsf-1957 and perkins part02: mission-era claim of 'over 102,000 acres'; the Hancock survey (1858) was 'just under' 11 leagues; Scott's Thompson survey (1865) took in 102,025 acres; 'about 11,000 acres' fell in Ventura County
* lw2570a (Rolle 1991): 1858 survey described 'some one hundred thousand acres'
* reynolds part14: boundary from Arroyo Piru east to the Arroyo Taburga (the grant's own description); perkins-rsf-1957: 'up to and including a place called La Soledad'
* **Outside:** Land Case 303 SD: patent Feb 12, 1875 for **48,611.88 acres** (Los Angeles County 37,870.32; Ventura County 10,741.56); GLO plat 399. Wikipedia: 48,612 acres.
* **Evidence favors:** Patented area 48,611.88 acres (Hancock-based survey). The 48,829 figure is nominal (11 square leagues at 4,439 acres), not a survey result, so the page should say so. The 100,000+ figures are the mission claim and the rejected 1865 Thompson survey, not the grant.

### C7. Partition: the 13,599 / 21,307 / 4,684-acre award, and later shares

* reynolds part15 and lw2052: a judge awarded 13,599 acres to Ygnacio, 21,307 to Jacoba and 4,684 to each of her six children. **Total 62,010 acres**, about 13,400 more than the patented 48,612
* reynolds part29: 'From the original grant of **16,599** acres inherited from his father'
* robinson_storyofvalencia_1967: Ygnacio held an **undivided 5/11**; the Salazars 6/11
* lw2052: the 1861 Wolfskill deal gave Ygnacio the 'western five-elevenths'
* perkins-rsf-1957: estate distributed 'by undivided portions'; 'The land could not be partitioned before title confirmation'; formal partition in 1870 (L.F. Cooper; effective July 30, signed by Judge Pablo de la Guerra): Ygnacio's undivided 2/21 carried Camulos, 1,340 acres
* lw2752: 'Upon Antonio's death ... the ranch was divided among his widow and children'
* **Outside:** Wikipedia repeats 13,599 / 21,307 / 4,684 and calls the 13,599-acre share 'Rancho Camulos'.
* **Evidence favors:** The Reynolds/Wally Smith figures cannot all be right: they exceed the grant. The documented sequence is undivided shares (1841–1870), then a partition decree in 1870. A Mexican-era judicial division ('the juez') is NEEDS_VERIFICATION. Do not migrate the three figures without the decree.

### C8. The Carrillo contest; Land Commission and patent dates

* perkins-rsf-1957 / lw2570a: 'In 1843, the title of Camulos was momentarily clouded by a grant to Pedro Carrillo' and the grant 'was nullified'
* delcastillo1980: Pedro Carrillo applied in 1841; 'a year later, governor Micheltorena ruled in Carrillo's favor'; the del Valles faced losing 'over 17,000 acres'; final settlement in 1855 by the Board of Land Commissioners
* lw2570a (Rolle 1991): competing claims dismissed only in 1855; first patent survey 1858
* perkins-rsf-1957: claim filed Sept 1852; patent Feb 12, 1875
* **Outside:** Land Case 303 SD: petition filed Sept 2, 1852; confirmed by the Board Jan 2, 1855; appeal dismissed June 8, 1857; patent Feb 12, 1875. hometownstation.com gives the patent as 'March 18, 1875' (see A8).
* **Evidence favors:** Land Commission dates as in the case file. Patent **Feb 12, 1875**. The Carrillo episode: 1841–43 to 1855, outcome favoring the del Valles; the two accounts differ on whether Micheltorena first ruled for Carrillo: NEEDS_VERIFICATION.

### C9. Losing Rancho San Francisco: who sold what to whom, when and for how much (incl. 'Jenkins / Newhall 1875')

* perkins-rsf-1957: 1858 Salazar borrowed $8,500 from Wolfskill on 6 leagues; 1859 Jacoba's mortgaged equities deeded to Ygnacio for $1,000; sheriff's deeds to Wolfskill 1863 and 1864; deeds from the heirs to Ygnacio from 1865, 'culminating in a deed from Ygnacio del Valle to Thomas R. Bard, as of March 18' [1865] for the LA County lands, **$47,519.71**; to Philadelphia & California Petroleum Co.; 1873 sheriff's deed to Fernald & Richards; **deeded to Henry M. Newhall, $90,000, January 15, 1875**
* lastar18640514wolfskill (note): sold to Bard March 1865 for $47,519.71; 'Henry Newhall bought the 46,460 acres ... at a sheriff's sale in 1875'
* reynolds part29: 'Bard acquired Rancho San Francisco from William Wolfskill for **$53,320** by deed dated **April 29, 1865**'
* robinson_storyofvalencia_1967: Bard paid **$53,320** to Wolfskill, Ygnacio and José del Valle; Fernald & Richards bought in 1873 for $33,000 at a sheriff's auction; a second sheriff's sale was 'called off when a private buyer was found'; Newhall Jan 15, 1875, $90,000
* delcastillo1980: Ygnacio wrote to J.L. Brent (1863) that he had sold interests for **$21,000** to a petroleum company
* lw2052: lost the ranch 'to financiers, then oil speculators'; it went to Newhall in 1875
* reynolds part41 / perkins part05: W.W. Jenkins (Ranger captain) staked a Castaic Creek claim in 1872 and settled in 1878. **No del Valle sale to Jenkins appears on any page.**
* **Outside:** hometownstation.com ownership list: Ygnacio del Valle, Thomas R. Bard, Robert H. Gratz, Philadelphia & California Petroleum Co., Newhall 1875. Wikipedia: lost to financiers after the drought.
* **Evidence favors:** Ygnacio's sale was in **1865, to Bard** (two prices, perhaps two deeds: $47,519.71 on Mar 18 for the LA County lands, and $53,320 on Apr 29 from Wolfskill: NEEDS_VERIFICATION). **Newhall bought in 1875 from the post-1873 holders, not from Ygnacio.** Whether that was a sheriff's sale or a private sale is disputed; Robinson (private) is better documented. **The brief's 'sale to Jenkins' is not supported by anything seen:** NEEDS_VERIFICATION in LA County deed indexes.

### C10. How much of Rancho San Francisco Ygnacio kept (Camulos acreage)

* robinson_storyofvalencia_1967: Bard 'sold back the **1,300**-acre Camulos area to Ygnacio alone for $500'
* perkins-rsf-1957, reynolds part29, wood_overtherange: **1,340** acres (Perkins: the 1870 partition)
* delcastillo1980: Reginaldo (1930) remembered an 1,800-acre partition, but 'Actually, this had dwindled to **1,340** acres by 1886'
* lastar18640514wolfskill, hl7101: 'westernmost **1,350** acres'
* lw3903, lw2463a, lw2633a, lw2632a, lw2695 (shared boilerplate): 'the **1,500**-acre Camulos section'; lp_oxnardpresscourier092657 (1957): '1,500-acre ranch'
* times111101: Ygnacio 'carved out **1,800** acres'; lp1501, ra20070929, camulos-nrhp1 (1996): Camulos today is about 1,800 acres
* **Outside:** Wikipedia: kept 1,500 acres of Camulos.
* **Evidence favors:** **About 1,340 acres** at the 1870 partition (Perkins, citing the decree) is the best-supported figure for what Ygnacio kept; 1,300 and 1,350 are roundings. 1,800 is the **modern** ranch (NRHP 1996) and Reginaldo's recollection, and should not be read back to 1870. 1,500 has no source.

### C11. When the Camulos adobe was built

* camulos-nrhp1 (Triem & Stone 1996): 'a four-room adobe, was constructed by Ygnacio del Valle in 1853'; the site repeats 1853 on mu0538, lw3903, lw2463a, latta1976ramona notes, carewe1928ramona, oxnardcourier19100407ramona, ncis1102camulos
* gallery-lw2706: 1853 'the year they built their adobe home'
* robinson_storyofvalencia_1967: 'Begun in 1854 ... completed in 1861'
* reynolds part29: 'majordomo's quarters, four rooms built in 1854'; '1861 a 20-room adobe home rose'
* reynolds part15: Ygnacio 'found a four-room adobe' already standing; 'No one knows exactly when the adobe was built'
* gallery-lo3401 (HABS 1934): owner Rubel 'gives the date of erection as 1841 for the older portion, the balance having been added in 1846'; editor: 'generally believed ... 1853'
* christopher0917: living room 'of the adobe that had been added in 1862'
* delcastillo1980: '16 of the 20 rooms ... were built after 1850'
* **Outside:** Wikipedia (Rancho Camulos article not fetched; Ygnacio article silent). The NRHP/NHL nomination is the formal record.
* **Evidence favors:** The first four rooms date to 1853 (NRHP; Triem & Stone), with expansion to about 20 rooms by the 1860s–70s. The 1841/1846 (HABS/Rubel) and pre-existing-adobe (Reynolds) claims are unsupported. The exact date is NEEDS_VERIFICATION (no dated primary source seen).

### C12. Who managed Camulos, and Reginaldo's office in 1880

* lw3664: Juventino was ranch manager 1862–1886 (citing Triem & Stone)
* delcastillo1980: 'In 1878, Ygnacio retired and turned the management ... over to Joventino'
* reynolds part29: after Ygnacio's death, 'Management of the estate passed to his eldest surviving son, Reginaldo, who was then a state senator'
* penpictures_rdelvalle (1889): Reginaldo elected State Senator in 1882; lat18900822delvalle: Assembly 1880–81; lw2752: Juventino (1841–1919) was older
* **Outside:** Rancho Camulos Museum finding aid (OAC): Josefa del Valle Forster helped her mother manage the ranch after 1880.
* **Evidence favors:** In March 1880 Reginaldo was not a state senator (Assembly 1880–81; Senate from 1882), and he was not the eldest surviving son (Juventino). Management passed to Ysabel, with Juventino and later Josefa and Ulpiano.

### C13. Marriages and children

* reynolds part15: married María de los Angeles Carrillo, Jan 1, 1842 (before Bishop Durán); a son Juventino was born before the marriage
* penpictures_ydelvalle (1889): 'no children by the first marriage'; second marriage 'in 1851'; 'Six children' by Ysabel, all living in 1889
* times111101: Juventino was 'Ygnacio's illegitimate son'; Ysabel and Ygnacio 'had 12 children, five of whom lived to adulthood'
* reynolds part29: raised 'eleven children'
* lw2752: Juventino 1841–1919; Ysabel 1837–1905
* obituary_ysabelvareladelvalle (1905): four living children named
* **Outside:** Wikipedia: first marriage 1842 (ECPP); second marriage **1851 (infobox) vs 1852 (text)**, 'at the age of 44 married 15-year-old'. Schwald family genealogy (citing the Plaza Church register #238): married Dec 7, 1851 at the Plaza Church, LA; also lists 'other spouse' María del Rosario Lorenzana (NEEDS_VERIFICATION).
* **Evidence favors:** Second marriage Dec 7, 1851 (Pen Pictures 1889 and the register citation agree), so Ysabel (b. 1837) was 14 or 15. Children: 12 born, with 5 or 6 surviving, depending on the date and on whether Juventino is counted (born 1841, about the time of the Jan 1842 marriage). 'Illegitimate' is a characterization to attribute, not state.

### C14. Death date, place and burial

* reynolds part29: 'On March 30, 1880, Don Ignacio del Valle died and was buried in the family crypt' at Camulos
* penpictures_ydelvalle (1889): at his death in 1880 'his home was on the beautiful Camulos Rancho'; quotes Judge Ygnacio Sepúlveda's published death notice
* lp1501: 'initially interred in an above-ground coffin or vault'; the 1905 move to Calvary is 'Or so the story goes'; ra20070929: 'is said to have had his body exhumed'
* times111101: the 1905 exhumation and shared coffin told as fact
* most site pages give only '1808-1880'
* **Outside:** Wikipedia: died March 30, 1880 at Rancho Camulos (infobox); buried at the rancho, exhumed 1905 to Calvary. Wikidata: P570 '1880' (year only); P20 'United States'. JoinCalifornia: died March 30, 1880 'in Los Angeles, CA'. Schwald: died Mar 30, 1880, Ventura County; burial **Apr 1, 1880, Mission San Buenaventura** (register).
* **Evidence favors:** **March 30, 1880** (independent agreement). Place: Camulos (Ventura County) is best supported; JoinCalifornia's 'Los Angeles' is unsupported (A4). The Mission San Buenaventura entry is most likely the parish burial record for a burial at the Camulos family plot (Camulos lay within that parish): NEEDS_VERIFICATION. The 1905 re-interment at Calvary is family tradition: NEEDS_VERIFICATION against Calvary records.

### C15. Civic offices: alcalde, 'mayor', Recorder, Assembly, Council, and the Rangers

* penpictures_ydelvalle (1889): treasurer 1846; 'in 1850 he was alcalde [similar to mayor] of Los Angeles; and later was a member of the city council and also of the State Legislature'
* perkins-rsf-1957: Junta 1845; treasurer 1846; alcalde 1850; 'At the first election under new laws, was elected County Recorder'; 'Served as assemblyman in 1852 and 1856'
* lw2052: 'Ygnacio lived at Los Angeles, where he was mayor'; 'When statehood came in 1850, Ygnacio served a short stint in the first Legislature'; office list: City Council May 4, 1852 – May 3, 1853 and May 7, 1856 – resigned Dec 15, 1856; committees to 1859
* lw2532, lw3768: 'after serving in the state Legislature at statehood in 1850 he was elected to the L.A. City Council in 1852'
* reynolds part25: 'The mayor of the town, Don Ygnacio del Valle ... formed the California Rangers'; reynolds part41: the Rangers had made the town safe 'by 1855'
* dac18530727camulos (Daily Alta, July 27, 1853): LA citizens met July 15, 1853 and organized volunteers against robbers
* engelhardt_sanfernando56: Ygnacio sat on Pico's 1845 assembly
* carter-ramona1902: Antonio F. Coronel 'elected mayor of Los Angeles in 1853'
* **Outside:** JoinCalifornia (Ignacio Del Valle): elected **Sept 3, 1851, AD-02**; biography line '1853: Mayor, City of Los Angeles?'. Wikipedia infobox: de facto mayor (alcalde) Jan 1 – Jul 1, 1850; Assembly, 2nd district, Jan 5, 1852 – Jan 3, 1853; Common Council 1852 and 1856. Wikipedia text: founded the Los Angeles Rangers during his 1850 alcalde term; 'remained on the City Council for five years'.
* **Evidence favors:** Alcalde 1850 (the pre-incorporation office, sometimes called 'de facto mayor'); County Recorder 1850; Assembly **1852 session** (elected 1851), **not the first Legislature (1849–50)**; Council 1852–53 and 1856. He was never mayor of the incorporated city (the 1853 mayor was A.F. Coronel). The Rangers were organized in 1853; his role is NEEDS_VERIFICATION. The 1856 Assembly term in Perkins is NEEDS_VERIFICATION (possibly confused with the 1856 Council term).

### C16. Arrival and military career; which missions he secularized

* reynolds part14: landed at Monterey July 27, 1825; 2nd lieutenant March 1828; later captain and chief customs officer at San Diego
* penpictures_ydelvalle: came with Echeandía in 1825; lieutenant 1828–1840; 1834 secularization commissioner for Santa Cruz and Dolores
* reynolds part15: 1834 commissioner for 'Missions San Gabriel, San Francisco Dolores and Santa Cruz'; resigned 1840
* perkins-rsf-1957 fn: captain and customs officer at San Diego until 1833; Monterey to 1836; discharged 1840; 'Commandante in secularization of the missions in 1834'
* **Outside:** Wikipedia: cadet 1825 at the Santa Barbara Presidio; 2nd lieutenant 1828; secularized Mission Santa Cruz and Mission San Francisco de Asís.
* **Evidence favors:** Santa Cruz and San Francisco de Asís (Dolores) are agreed. San Gabriel (Reynolds only) is NEEDS_VERIFICATION. Arrival in 1825 is agreed; whether he landed at Monterey or came in by Santa Barbara is unresolved.

### C17. Rancho El Tejon and Rancho Temescal

* penpictures_ydelvalle: 'Don Ygnacio and Don J.A. Aguirre received a concession in 1843 of the rancho of Tejon, twenty-two Spanish leagues', 'which now belongs to General E.F. Beale'
* perkins-rsf-1957 fn: 'With Jose Antonio Aguirre, received grant of the 97,000 acre Tejon rancho in 1843'
* lw2052: 'Gen. Edward F. Beale had already picked up the deed to Rancho Tejon to satisfy some of the Del Valle creditors'
* reynolds part26: 'He had already sold Rancho Tejon to General Beale to satisfy the creditors of his in-laws', set in the narrative before 'By 1861 the Salazárs were out of the picture', so it implies a sale before 1861. Beale's El Tejon purchase is dated 1865 (tejonranchtimeline; Beale dossier C8). The part26 timing is contradicted, and lw2052's line appears to derive from it. [added 2026-09-29]
* reynolds part16/part17: Temescal granted to Francisco Lopez (1842 diseño); delcastillo1980: Ygnacio bought Temescal in 1857 for $4,000; lw3903: 'bought the adjacent 13,339-acre Rancho Temescal' in 1857
* **Outside:** Wikipedia: del Valle received Rancho El Tejon in 1843 'for his military service' and later sold it to pay debts (no co-grantee named). Beale dossier (edward-f-beale-sources.md C8): Beale acquired El Tejon (97,612 acres) in 1865; the chain of title there is also NEEDS_VERIFICATION.
* **Evidence favors:** Tejon was a joint 1843 concession with J.A. Aguirre (A2). The 'Beale picked up the deed ... for Del Valle creditors' line needs the Kern County deed trail: NEEDS_VERIFICATION. Temescal: granted to Lopez, bought by Ygnacio in 1857 (the accounts can be reconciled).

### C18. The 1842 gold discovery and the del Valles

* reynolds part16, perkins part03, lwhist, worden-coinage1005: Francisco Lopez, March 9, 1842; the petition of April 4, 1842 (transcribed on perkins part03) places it 'at the place of San Francisco, appertaining to the late Don Antonio del Valle'
* perkins part03 / reynolds part16: Prefect Santiago Arguello named Ygnacio 'encargado de justicia' of the placer, **May 3, 1842** (original letter in the Bancroft Library, per Perkins); perkins-rsf-1957 fn: 'In 1843, was Juez ... at mining district of San Francisquito'
* lp_oaklandtrib110418 (1918, from Juventino): gold found 'in 1844' and shipped 'to England'
* output: reynolds part16 '125 pounds of ore' by 1848; worden lw012496 '1,300 pounds of gold' 1842–47; perkins part03 (Bancroft) '2,000 ounces' by end of 1843; camp closed by raids in 1844 (Perkins) vs 'petered out in 1848' (Reynolds)
* Lopez's relationship: uncle of Doña Jacoba (reynolds part16); 'his niece's ranch' (worden-coinage1005); 'a relative' (robinson)
* **Outside:** Wikipedia: the gold discovery was on the disputed land. The Huntington's Abel Stearns papers cover 'the gold discoveries in Southern California in 1842' (OAC).
* **Evidence favors:** March 9, 1842 on the del Valle estate land (Placerita), with Ygnacio appointed the placer's justice on May 3, 1842 (contemporary document). The 1844 date is a late family recollection. Production figures conflict and should be attributed.

### C19. Drought dates and what Ygnacio owned before it

* lw2052, robinson_storyofvalencia_1967: drought 1862–64
* lw3903: 'the drought of 1863-64 that followed the torrential rains of 1861-62'
* lp_oaklandtrib110418 note: 'Prior to the Great Drought of 1864-65, he owned the entire Rancho San Francsico'
* delcastillo1980: 1863; reynolds part26: flood of 1856 and mortgages
* gallery-lw2706: in 1853 'the Del Valles were in possession of the entire 48,000-acre Rancho San Francisco' (the family collectively)
* **Outside:** Wikipedia: flooding, then drought, early 1860s.
* **Evidence favors:** Drought 1862–64 (the standard dating; 1864–65 is wrong). Before the drought Ygnacio held an undivided share (5/11 by Robinson), not the entire rancho.

### C20. Ramona and Camulos: Helen Hunt Jackson's visit

* reynolds part29: 'Jackson arrived at Camulos on Jan. 23, 1882'
* delcastillo1980: visited 'on January 23, 1883', 'for about four hours'; none of the elder del Valles were home
* latta1976ramona (José Jesús López): 'There was no "Ramona" such as Helen Hunt Jackson created'
* lw2963 (Odell 1939), edwardsroberts_ramonashome (1886), carter-ramona1902: the Camulos-as-Ramona's-home tradition
* **Outside:** Not checked against HHJ's letters in this pass.
* **Evidence favors:** The day (Jan 23) agrees; the year does not. 1882 fits the known timeline (Ramona written in New York in 1883–84), but it is NEEDS_VERIFICATION against Jackson's dated letters. Either way the visit was after Ygnacio's death: he had no personal role in the novel.

### C21. Indigenous content (Tataviam, Chumash) at Camulos (CARE)

* reynolds part15: at Camulos 'the grass huts of the Tataviam Indians'; the name means 'juniper tree' in Spanish, but Ygnacio 'preferred the Chumash translation, "House of Refuge"'
* times111101: 'the former Indian village of Camulos, which means juniper tree'
* robinson_storyofvalencia_1967 (editor): Camulos 'had been a Tataviam Indian village' and gives two village names (not repeated here)
* engelhardt_sanfernando56: the del Valles took possession 'against the will of the neophytes'
* delcastillo1980: Reginaldo's 1930 history told of 'burying a dead Indian child in the walls'; almost 200 'Indians and Mexicans' lived on the rancho
* tv1703: 'Tataviam Mortar and Pestle' (Smithsonian loan, 2017); lw2632a: 'Native American grinding stones'
* reynolds notes (note 1): Tataviam descendants; cites a spiritual leader of the Fernandeño-Tataviam tribe
* **Outside:** Self-name verified: Fernandeño Tataviam Band of Mission Indians (tataviam-nsn.us). Chumash representation for the Santa Clara River valley would need to be identified with the tribes. Not further verified here.
* **Evidence favors:** **Flag for tribal consultation; do not settle.** The meaning and origin of 'Camulos' (Spanish 'juniper' vs 'Chumash' 'House of Refuge' vs a Tataviam village name), the 'grass huts' description, the burial-in-walls story, and the artifact labels all need review by the Fernandeño Tataviam Band (and Chumash representatives where Chumash attribution is made). No coordinates or site locations are given in this dossier; the Camulos cemetery and artifact find-spots are not located here.

## 8. Corrections to earlier findings

* **'Sales to Jenkins / Newhall (1875)'** (from the brief): nothing seen supports a del Valle sale to W.W. Jenkins. Jenkins appears only as a Ranger captain, Castaic claimant (1872) and district recorder. **Ygnacio's sale was to Thomas R. Bard in 1865.** Henry M. Newhall bought in Jan 1875 from the post-1873 holders, not from Ygnacio. Ygnacio later mortgaged Camulos to Newhall (1876, $10,000; 1879, $15,776).
* **'Camulos ≈ 1,800 acres'** (working note from lp1501 and times111101): 1,800 is the modern ranch (NRHP 1996) and Reginaldo's 1930 recollection. What Ygnacio kept after the 1870 partition was about 1,340 acres.
* **'48,829 acres on lw2052 is an error'** (working note): withdrawn as a live error. 48,829 is the nominal 11 square leagues at 4,439 acres per league (Perkins). It should be labeled nominal, but it is not wrong. The patent figure is 48,611.88.
* **The Land Case record (Bancroft/Calisphere) describes two grants**: Sespe (Figueroa, 1833, 6 leagues) and San Francisco (Alvarado, Jan 22, 1839, about 4 leagues). This is not simply the single '11-league 1839 grant' assumed earlier. How they add up to the 48,611.88-acre patent must be read in the case file (NEEDS_VERIFICATION; the Bancroft page returned 403 on recheck, so this rests on the earlier read and a search snippet).
* **hometownstation's 'March 18, 1875' patent date**, noted earlier as a straight conflict, is explained as confusion with the March 18, [1865] deed to Bard (A8).
* **Magdalena del Valle**: an early working note followed Perkins fn 26 (Ygnacio's full sister). Most site sources, including the 1853 ledger caption, make her Jacoba's daughter (half-sister). Left open in C3.
* **Huntington holdings**: no 'del Valle family papers' collection was found at the Huntington (web search; catalog.huntington.org returned 403). The verified repositories are NHM Seaver Center GC 1002 and P-14, and the Rancho Camulos Museum. The Huntington's Abel Stearns papers (OAC) are relevant for 1842 gold and business dealings.
* **(2026-09-29, from the Reynolds dossier, correction 4) C2, mother's name:** the site's 'María Josepha Carillo' traces to reynolds part13, the earliest site occurrence. lw2052 and the lw2706 caption are later and should not count as independent confirmation. Part13's own source is unstated. (The Reynolds report said part13 rests on Wally Smith 1958; strictly, the editor's note 5 documents Smith only for chs. 14–15, so that is corrected to 'source unstated'.) C2 stays NEEDS_VERIFICATION. Bullet added to C2.
* **(2026-09-29, from the Reynolds dossier, correction 5) C17, Tejon sale timing:** reynolds part26's statement that Ygnacio 'had already sold Rancho Tejon to General Beale' before 1861 was not logged. It is contradicted by Beale's 1865 acquisition of El Tejon (Beale dossier C8), and lw2052's parallel wording likely derives from it. Bullet added to C17; part26's Section 1 row now carries C17.
* **(2026-09-29, from the Reynolds dossier, correction 3) Reynolds chapter dates:** '©1998' on the signal/reynolds/partNN pages is the date of the SCVHS web edition (ed. Leon Worden). The texts were written 1976–94 (Signal series mid-1970s and mid-1980s; the 1992 book; the prologue and epilogue in 1985) and edited in 1998. Date columns now read 'web ed. ©1998; text written 1976–94'. Treat these chapters as later secondary accounts with an unknown per-chapter composition date.
