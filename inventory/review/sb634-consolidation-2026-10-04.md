# SB 634 and the SCV Water consolidation: two bodies or four?

## Short answer

Nathan is right. SB 634 reorganized **two** public bodies, the Castaic Lake Water Agency (CLWA) and the Newhall County Water District (NCWD), into the new Santa Clarita Valley Water Agency, and made the new agency the successor of those two only.

The other two were CLWA's:

- The **Santa Clarita Water Division (SCWD)** was a retail division of CLWA with no separate legal existence. It passed to SCV Water inside CLWA.
- The **Valencia Water Company (VWC)** was a real, separate private corporation, but CLWA had owned all its stock since December 2012. It was not reorganized by the statute. It became SCV Water's property on 1 January 2018 as part of CLWA's assets, and the statute *directed* SCV Water to dissolve it afterwards. That was done in January 2018.

"Four" is SCV Water's own public framing ("the four largest water providers", "the former service areas of" four), and it is fair if it is put that way: four retail water suppliers or service areas came under one agency. It is wrong as "four bodies merged" or "took the place of four".

## 1. The statute's own words

SB 634 (Wilk), **Statutes of 2017, Chapter 833**. Approved by the Governor and filed with the Secretary of State on 15 October 2017 (leginfo chaptered text, saved as `sb634-billTextClient.html`). It was not an urgency measure, so it took effect on 1 January 2018; LA LAFCO's hearing notice says the same ("now adopted law which took effect on January 1, 2018").

Title: "An act to repeal Section 57114.5 of the Government Code, to amend Sections 21530 and 21531 of the Public Contract Code, to repeal the Castaic Lake Water Agency Law (Chapter 28 of the First Extraordinary Session of the Statutes of 1962), and to create the Santa Clarita Valley Water Agency..."

Legislative Counsel's Digest: "This bill would reorganize the Newhall County Water District and the Castaic Lake Water Agency into the Santa Clarita Valley Water Agency, which this bill would create, and prohibit the Castaic Lake Water Agency and the Newhall County Water District from operating as separate entities or exercising independent functions."

**Findings (bill section 1)** describe what each body was:

- (b) "Castaic Lake Water Agency was created pursuant to the Castaic Lake Water District Law [sic] ... The agency is a State Water Project contractor and provides wholesale water service to the Santa Clarita Valley region..."
- (c) "The agency provides retail water service to a portion of the valley through its Santa Clarita Water Division, which has approximately 30,700 service connections."
- (d) "Newhall County Water District is a county water district formed pursuant to the County Water District Law (Division 12 (commencing with Section 30000) of the Water Code)."
- (e) "Castaic Lake Water Agency is the owner of all of the outstanding stock of Valencia Water Company, which is a private company that provides retail water service in the valley to approximately 31,350 service connections."
- (f) "...settling litigation between the entities arising from the agency's purchase of the company's stock. As the settlement discussions progressed, both sides began to see merit in and discuss the possibility of combining the two entities into a new public agency."
- (k) "...combining the two entities into one new water entity..."
- (n) "It is the intent of the Legislature that, following the enactment of this act, Valencia Water Company will be dissolved and integrated into the entity."

**Santa Clarita Valley Water Agency Act (bill section 5), section 4**, the operative words:

- 4(a): "The Castaic Lake Water Agency ... and the Newhall County Water District ... shall be reorganized into the Santa Clarita Valley Water Agency and shall no longer operate as separate entities or exercise independent functions."
- 4(b): "The agency shall be the successor to the Castaic Lake Water Agency and the Newhall County Water District for the purpose of succeeding to all of the rights, duties, obligations, contracts, responsibilities, assets, entitlements, and liabilities of the Castaic Lake Water Agency and the Newhall County Water District..."
- 4(c), (d): all property, assets and liabilities, and all employees, of CLWA and NCWD (only) go to the agency.
- 4(h), on **SCWD**: "The indebtedness of the Newhall County Water District and the Santa Clarita Water Division, a retail division of the Castaic Lake Water Agency, that exists as of December 31, 2017 ... shall be borne by retail divisions of the agency that correspond with the Newhall County Water District and Santa Clarita Water Division areas, respectively..."
- 4(j), on **VWC**: "The agency shall continue to supply water at wholesale to Valencia Water Company. No later than January 31, 2018, the agency, as the successor in interest to Castaic Lake Water Agency, shall take the appropriate steps together with the board of directors of Valencia Water Company to authorize the dissolution of Valencia Water Company and the transfer of the company's assets, property, liabilities, and indebtedness to the agency... The dissolution and transfer shall be finalized no later than May 1, 2018, but the board of the agency may postpone this deadline until no later than July 1, 2018... A transfer pursuant to this subdivision is not subject to the Cortese-Knox-Hertzberg Local Government Reorganization Act of 2000..."
- 4(k): "If Valencia Water Company or any other retail water supplier is integrated into the agency, the indebtedness ... shall be borne by the customers in the area that corresponds with the boundaries of the retail water supplier..."
- 4(l): the agency may treat the retail divisions as one unit, and "When all such preexisting indebtedness has been retired, the agency shall dispense with the retail divisions for all purposes as soon as feasible."
- Section 8(a): the first board is "The five members of the Newhall County Water District board of directors in office as of December 31, 2017" and "The appointed member representing the purveyor [District 36] ... and the nine elected members of the Castaic Lake Water Agency board of directors in office as of December 31, 2017." Fifteen in all; VWC's board had no seats.
- Section 29(a): the LAFCO plan for services "shall include Valencia Water Company, which will be integrated into the agency pursuant to subdivision (j) of Section 4."

So the statute never says "merge" or "consolidate". It says "reorganized into" and "successor to", for CLWA and NCWD only. It calls SCWD a retail division of CLWA, and it treats VWC as a company CLWA owned, to be dissolved later by board action.

**LA LAFCO** (Application for Conditions No. 2018-01, hearing notice for 14 March 2018, `lalafco-march-notices-2018.pdf`): "Due to the State's approval of SB 634, and as of January 1, 2018, two former water agencies (the Castaic Lake Water Agency and the Newhall County Water District) were dissolved and no longer exist. These two agencies were consolidated into a new district, the Santa Clarita Valley Water Agency, as of January 1, 2018." SCV Water's board started the application by resolution on 29 January 2018. LAFCO's final written report was not found online: NEEDS_VERIFICATION.

**SCV Water's own wording, which is not consistent:**
- "Who We Are": "created January 1, 2018 by Senate Bill 634 ... which merged three water agencies."
- "History": "home to three water divisions: Newhall Water Division, Santa Clarita Water Division, Valencia Water Division, as well as Castaic Lake Water Agency, the regional water wholesaler."
- News release, 12 January 2018: VWC's dissolution is "the final step to unify the four largest water providers," and it "follows the January 1, 2018 inception of SCV Water, when CLWA and Newhall County Water District became one entity."
- Plan for Services sent to LAFCO, 2018: "CLWA also operated Santa Clarita Water Division (SCWD) and owned the stock of Valencia Water Company (VWC)"; "as the successor in interest to CLWA, SCV Water became the owner of all of the outstanding stock of VWC on January 1, 2018."

## 2. What each body was on 31 December 2017

| Body | Legal form on 31 Dec 2017 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Castaic Lake Water Agency | Public agency created by special act (Castaic Lake Water Agency Law, Stats. 1962, 1st Ex. Sess., ch. 28). A State Water Project contractor and wholesaler. It also sold at retail within the old Santa Clarita Water Company area under its act's section 15.1, added by AB 134 (2001). | SB 634 sec. 1(b)-(c); Klajic II (2004) |
| Newhall County Water District | Independent public district: a county water district under the County Water District Law (Water Code sec. 30000 ff.), formed at an election on 13 January 1953 | SB 634 sec. 1(d); archive #27534 |
| Valencia Water Company | Private corporation, a water utility regulated by the PUC, incorporated 7 April 1954. CLWA owned all of its stock and appointed its board, but it had its own staff | SB 634 sec. 1(e); SCV Water Plan for Services 2018; SCV Water History page; SCOPE v. CLWA (2016) |
| Santa Clarita Water Division | A retail division of CLWA, with no separate legal existence. (The old Santa Clarita Water Company corporation had been formally dissolved on 31 October 2017.) | SB 634 Act sec. 4(h); CLWA board memo, October 2017; Plan for Services ("operated as a subsidiary division of the CLWA") |

In 2016 the Court of Appeal held that VWC had **not** become CLWA's alter ego. The trial court found that CLWA's ownership of all its stock and its choice of most of its directors "did not constitute sufficient evidence of merger" (SCOPE v. CLWA, 1 Cal.App.5th 1084). So VWC was legally separate up to the end, which is why the statute had to order its dissolution.

## 3. When CLWA acquired the two retailers

### Santa Clarita Water Company: September 1999, not 1998

The court record settles it. Klajic v. Castaic Lake Water Agency (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 987, B137258:

- 11 August 1999: CLWA approved the Retail Service Agreement and its Resolution No. 2065, authorizing condemnation of "all of the issued and outstanding capital stock of the Water Company."
- 12 August 1999: CLWA filed its complaint to condemn the stock.
- 23 August 1999: four residents (Klajic and others, including NCWD directors Lynne Plambeck and Joan Dunn acting as individuals, per CP&DR) sued.
- 1 September 1999: CLWA "executed a new stock purchase agreement to purchase the Water Company's stock for $63 million in cash."
- 2 September 1999: stipulated judgment for $63 million entered in the eminent domain action.
- **3 September 1999**: "the consideration of $63 million in cash was transmitted to the shareholders of the Water Company ... and the court executed a final order condemning in favor of the Agency all rights, title, and interest in the Water Company's outstanding shares." After that the company passed resolutions to wind up its business and "distribute its remaining assets to the Agency."

Two more records agree:

- CPUC Decision 99-10-047 (21 October 1999, Johnson v. Santa Clarita Water Company, filed 6 July 1999): the company "acknowledges that it is in the process of being purchased" by CLWA and quotes the 11 August 1999 resolution of necessity. The company was still independent in mid-1999.
- AB 134 (2001), sponsored by CLWA while Klajic I was pending, gave CLWA retail authority within the company's area as served "on September 2, 1999, the day before the Agency acquired the Water Company" (Klajic II, 2004, 121 Cal.App.4th 5). The chapter number is NEEDS_VERIFICATION. SB 634's repeal clause lists "Chapter 929 of the Statutes of 2001" among the CLWA Law amendments, which is probably AB 134, but this was not confirmed.

**Where "31 August 1998" comes from.** It traces to the CLWA staff memo for the 25 October 2017 board meeting: "SCWC has been inactive since August 31, 1998", with financial statements "through August 31, 1998." Leon Worden's report (clwa102517.htm) repeated it as "inactive since August 31, 1998, when CLWA absorbed its assets." The court record contradicts it as a date for the acquisition. It may be a misdated "1999": the Retail Service Agreement recites that it was made on 31 August 1999. The memo date itself is NEEDS_VERIFICATION, but the acquisition year is 1999. Leon also gives 17 September 1999 as the date CLWA converted the empty corporation to a nonprofit, which is consistent with 1999.

"Bought all assets" (Leon) and "acquired the stock" (SCV Water, the courts) are both true: CLWA took the stock first, and then the company distributed its assets to CLWA.

No LAFCO action on the 1999 acquisition was found. It was done by eminent domain of stock, and LAFCO was not involved; the legal fight was Klajic I and II and AB 134. NEEDS_VERIFICATION if Nathan wants a LAFCO negative confirmed.

### Valencia Water Company: December 2012, stock taken from Newhall Land by eminent domain on a settlement

From SCOPE v. CLWA (2016) 1 Cal.App.5th 1084, B264284, opinion filed 28 July 2016:

- 2011: Newhall Land and Farming owned 100 percent of VWC's stock and offered to sell it to CLWA. VWC was "a private corporation regulated by the California Public Utilities Commission."
- 10 December 2012: staff reported a proposed agreement to acquire VWC's stock for $73.8 million.
- 12 December 2012: at a special meeting, the board adopted Resolution No. 2890, a resolution of necessity to acquire "all issued and outstanding shares," and Resolution No. 2893, authorizing a $73.8 million settlement.
- 13 December 2012: CLWA filed its eminent domain suit. Five days later it filed the settlement with Newhall Land.
- **18 December 2012**: "The trial court approved the settlement and entered judgment on the eminent domain action."
- 19 December 2012: CLWA appointed VWC's new five-member board, three of them CLWA employees.

Other sources:

- CLWA said in 2015 that the purchase was "approved by the CLWA Board of Directors in December 2012 and completed in early 2013" (SCVNews, 13 March 2015). In February 2013 the CPUC was still asserting that it had to approve any transfer of control (SCVNews, 1 and 25 February 2013). The date CPUC-side control passed is NEEDS_VERIFICATION.
- On price: SCV Water's own memo of 29 December 2017 says "The Agency purchased the stock in 2012 at a price of $58.6 million," paid from 2006A and 2011A bond proceeds. The court gives the settlement as $73.8 million. The difference is unexplained (NEEDS_VERIFICATION). Use $73.8 million, the court's figure, or leave the price out.

The litigation:

- SCOPE sued in 2013. It lost in the trial court (Judges Lavin and O'Brien, judgment of 8 April 2015) and on appeal in 2016.
- NCWD sued CLWA in April 2014 over the VWC purchase under AB 134 (SCVNews/KHTS, 29 April 2014). That is the suit settled at the joint meeting of 13 December 2016, the settlement that led to SB 634 (SB 634 sec. 1(f), (j)).
- Newhall County Water District v. CLWA (2016) 243 Cal.App.4th 1430 is a separate case, about wholesale rates.

### VWC's dissolution, 2018

From SCV Water's board memo of 29 December 2017 (scvwa010918):

- VWC's own board approved a Plan of Dissolution on 28 December 2017.
- The plan was put to SCV Water "as the sole shareholder."
- Assets were to transfer by 29 January 2018 and employees on 20 January 2018.

SCV Water's news release of 12 January 2018 reports the board's approval and expects completion by 31 January 2018. Still unconfirmed (NEEDS_VERIFICATION): the exact board meeting date (the archive titles it 9 January 2018, while the release says "tonight's actions") and the date the certificate of dissolution was filed with the Secretary of State.
