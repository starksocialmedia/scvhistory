## William G. Bonelli Jr. (#30199), full

William G. Bonelli Jr. was president of the first board of the Santa Clarita Community College District, elected with it in November 1967, and the college's first permanent building, the Instructional Resource Center, was named for him after he died in office in 1972.[1][2][3][4]

He was in the valley long before the college: the program of the Newhall-Saugus Rodeo of April 1948, held at the Bonelli Ranch in Saugus, names "Dr. W. G. Bonelli, Jr." as one of its resident veterinarians.[5] A study written for the Newhall Land and Farming Company in December 1953 credits him with a residential tract a few miles north of Newhall, "by far the best residential development in the Newhall area"; Leon Worden's biography of his father, William G. Bonelli (1895-1970), describes the Bonelli tract as the father's.[6][7] By Worden's lists he was president of the local chamber of commerce in 1955, with the Santa Clarita Water Co. as his business, and a director of the agency that became the Castaic Lake Water Agency from its organization in 1962 to 1969.[8][9] The Newhall-Saugus Chamber of Commerce named him its Outstanding Citizen of the Year for 1966, and the 1969 telephone directory lists "Dr. Bill Bonelli" among the chamber's directors.[10][7][11]

At the board's first meeting in December 1967, The Newhall Signal reported, he asked that the rules of order be modified so that he could vote, and two days later, when the board named the district the Santa Clarita Valley Junior College District, he refused an audience member's request for a roll call: "Only a board member can ask for a roll-call vote."[2] He was still president in May 1969, when the board named the college College of the Canyons, and told objectors that the valley's lack of a name was "a problem all public districts face here."[12] He was re-elected in April 1971.[1]

He died on February 22, 1972, during his second term; Carl Boyer, who later sat on the same board, writes that it was of cancer.[13][14] The Canyon Call, announcing his death, called him "the board's first president, serving for two years," and in March 1972 reported the contract for the Instructional Resource Center, "named in honor of the late trustee."[3] His seat was filled at a special election that June.[15] Governor Ronald Reagan dedicated the Dr. William G. Bonelli Center for Instructional Resources on April 22, 1974.[4] The college's homecoming scholarships to the senior class presidents of Hart and Canyon high schools carried his name in the 1970s.[16]

## Edward Muhl (#30201), full

Edward Muhl was a founding trustee of the Santa Clarita Community College District, its first vice president, and in 1972 its president.[1][2]

Carl Boyer, who served with him, calls him "Charge of Production at Universal International" and a trustee who "had pull," and Leon Worden reads Boyer as saying Muhl used his connections at Universal to bring Governor Ronald Reagan to dedicate the college's first permanent building in April 1974.[3][4] When he stood for re-election in 1975, The Canyon Call described him as a "retired film producer" of Saugus.[5]

He was elected with the first board in November 1967.[1] In May 1969, when the board named the college College of the Canyons over objections from the audience that the name had no place in it, he answered one protester, "That's the problem."[6] He was re-elected in April 1971.[1] By February 1972 he was the board's president; that spring he presented the trophy to the college's scholar-athlete of the year, and in June he conferred the degrees at commencement.[2][7]

On March 4, 1975, he was one of nine candidates, three of them incumbents, for three full terms on the board.[5] He is not among the members The Canyon Call names in May 1976, and Boyer writes that Lou Reiter and Jim Rentz "succeeded Ed Muhl and Don Allen."[8][3] The district's history of its board lists him to 1974-1975.[9] The result of 1975 for him is not known.[5][9]

## Sheila Dyer (#30203), retrospective

Sheila Dyer was one of the five trustees elected with the first board of the Santa Clarita Community College District on November 21, 1967.[1] In December 1967 The Newhall Signal reported that she "had been quite open about her support" of the name Santa Clarita from the board's first meeting, and that she read to the board from the minutes of a state geographic subcommittee meeting at which the name had been urged; the board chose the name Santa Clarita Valley Junior College District that night.[2]

Her years on the board are given two ways: the district's history of its board lists her in 1967 only and John Hackney from 1968, while the college's histories say Hackney replaced her in 1969.[3][1] How her service ended is not known.[3]

That Signal report is the one account written at the time that is cited here; the rest comes from the college's later histories.[2][1][3]

## Bruce D. Fortine (#30205), full

Bruce Fortine was a founding trustee of the Santa Clarita Community College District, and served on its board in two spans, from 1967 to 1973 and again from the early 1990s until 2016; between them he worked for the college, where he started its community services department.[1][2][3]

The Signal called him a Hart High School alumnus of 1955.[4] In 1968 he chaired the coronation ball of the Jaycees' July 4th Old West Celebration in Newhall, and the 1969 telephone directory lists him among the directors of the Newhall-Saugus-Valencia Chamber of Commerce.[5][6] The Canyon Call wrote in 1973 that he had been on the steering committee that planned the college.[7]

He was elected with the first board in November 1967, and the college gives December 11, 1967 as the day he was sworn in.[1][8] At the meeting that named the district that December, The Newhall Signal reported, he "argued at some length that there were a number of other names that would probably serve just as well or better," and proposed a neutral name.[9] He was vice president-clerk in May 1971 and vice president in February 1972, and was board president in 1972-1973.[10][11][12] He was re-elected to a four-year term in the spring of 1973.[7] In July 1973, by the college's account, he was hired by the college and had to resign from the board; as an employee he created its community services and non-credit department and served as its public information officer, and he worked there until August 1978.[1] The Canyon Call names him director of community services in 1977.[13]

He returned to the board in 1991, which the college describes as election to fill a seat and The Signal as filling a vacancy; the district's history of its board lists him again from 1992.[1][2][3] He was re-elected to Seat No. 4 unopposed in November 1995, with 6,131 votes, under the ballot designation "Corporate Executive"; in November 2003, first of three with 5,126; and in November 2011, by 6,954 votes to Diana Shaw's 6,127.[14] In the first election by trustee area, on November 8, 2016, he lost Trustee Area 2 to Edel Alonso, 7,261 votes to 6,532, with Ronda Baldwin-Kennedy third.[14][15] He was board president at his last meeting, in December 2016.[2]

The college says he worked behind the scenes on each of its five local bond measures and raised money for its foundation, including for the Dr. Dianne G. Van Hook University Center, the Institute for Culinary Education and the Santa Clarita Performing Arts Center, and that he served as the trustees' liaison to the foundation's board.[1] Its foundation named him the 2020 recipient of its Silver Spur community service award, presented on May 14, 2022.[1][16]

## Peter F. Huntsinger (#30207), full

Peter F. Huntsinger, a turkey rancher, was a founding trustee of the Santa Clarita Community College District and was its president more than once in its first decade.[1][2][3][4]

His family raised turkeys in Bouquet Canyon from 1951 and moved the business to Acton in 1964; Leon Worden writes that Peter's standing as a business leader lent the college credibility with voters on its first construction bond, and that he was a Rotarian and president of the Acton Chamber of Commerce in the 1970s.[5] In 1975 The Canyon Call gave his occupation as "turkey rancher."[6]

He was elected with the first board in November 1967, and at the board's meeting of December 1967 it was his motion that gave the district the name Santa Clarita.[1][7] He was re-elected in April 1971, and was board president at the college's sports awards banquet of May 1971.[1][2] As president in April 1974 he received the Instructional Resource Center for the district at its dedication, and he presided at that June's commencement.[3] He stood as an incumbent in March 1975 and remained on the board; he was its outgoing president in May 1976.[6][4] In March 1978 he voted, with Kevin Lynch and Francis Claffey, to sell the college's remaining $2 million in authorized bonds before the Jarvis initiative could stop it.[8] That year a recall drive led by William Broyles named him, Claffey and Lynch.[9]

Carl Boyer writes that Huntsinger "resigned from the district governing board in the middle of the board meeting on June 3," in an account of 1983; the district's history of its board lists him last in 1980-1981.[9][10]

## Francis T. Claffey (#30211), full

Francis T. Claffey was the Santa Clarita Valley's postmaster for more than two decades, first in Newhall and then over the consolidated Newhall and Saugus post offices, and a trustee of the Santa Clarita Community College District from 1972 into 1979.[1][2][3]

He was installed as acting postmaster of Newhall on March 31, 1964, succeeding Eva Pullar.[1] Leon Worden notes that in 1973 he became postmaster of Saugus, the valley's first postmaster appointed on merit rather than by patronage.[1] His obituary gives him 22 years as postmaster in all.[2]

He joined the college board in 1972 in the seat of William G. Bonelli Jr., who had died in office.[1] The Signal reported that he won a special election on June 20, 1972, on a turnout of 11 percent, by 71 votes over Don Allen, because "State law now requires that vacancies be filled by election, not appointment"; Carl Boyer gives the count as 398 of 1,949 votes.[1][4] The college's histories, and Worden's caption to a photograph of 1974, say he was appointed.[5][6] He stood again as an incumbent in March 1975, listed as "postmaster," and remained on the board.[7][8] In March 1978 he voted with Peter Huntsinger and Kevin Lynch to sell the college's remaining authorized bonds before the Jarvis initiative could stop it, and that year a recall drive named him with them.[9][10] He was board president in the spring of 1979 and conferred the degrees at that June's commencement.[11] The district's history of its board lists him to 1978 and has no list for 1979; how his service ended is not known.[3]

He was born on July 11, 1925, in Alderly, Wisconsin, and was an Army veteran.[2] He died in Lancaster on June 9, 2015, at 89.[2]

## Don Allen (#30213), full

Don Allen was president of the College of the Canyons student body in 1971-1972 and, from 1973, a trustee of the Santa Clarita Community College District, elected while still a student; he resigned before the election of March 1975.[1][2][3] The district's history of its board does not list him.[4]

The Canyon Call introduced him in October 1971 as a sophomore, past president of the Ecology Club and the previous year's campus "Man of the Year," who had spent 16 years as an industrial supervisor and was preparing for a second career as an industrial arts teacher.[1] As student body president he led the quiet opposition to the new student constitution adopted in the spring of 1972.[5] At the awards banquet that June he received a gavel and the Student Senate's plaque, and he took his associate in arts degree at the commencement.[6]

In the special election of June 20, 1972 for William G. Bonelli Jr.'s seat on the board, he was second of eleven candidates, 71 votes behind Francis T. Claffey; Carl Boyer gives his total as 327.[7][8] In the spring of 1973 he was elected to the board, "a COC student" voted a member "for the first time," as The Canyon Call put it; the paper described him as a 40-year-old trustee formerly in business management who was preparing to teach.[2] Boyer lists him among the board at the dedication of the Instructional Resource Center in April 1974.[9] In February 1975 The Canyon Call reported that "the recent resignation of trustee Don Allen" had added an unexpired term to the March 4 election, and Boyer writes that Jim Rentz succeeded him.[3][9]

## Kevin Lynch (#30219), full

Kevin G. Lynch, an attorney of Canyon Country, was a trustee of the Santa Clarita Community College District from 1977 to 1981 and a director of the Castaic Lake Water Agency.[1][2][3]

In 57 years of practice he began as a prosecutor for the Los Angeles City Attorney, became a partner in Lewis, Varni, Ghirardelli and Lynch, and later formed his own firms; he was president of the San Fernando Valley Bar Association and a member of the State Bar's executive committee, by his obituary.[1] Leon Worden's list of the water agency's directors gives him 1969 to 1980.[2]

He took the oath as "the board's newest member, Kevin Lynch, an attorney," in April 1977, succeeding Jim Rentz.[3][4] In March 1978 he voted with Peter Huntsinger and Francis Claffey to sell the college's remaining $2 million in authorized bonds before the Jarvis initiative could stop it, and that year a recall drive named him with them.[5][6] In November 1981 he lost his seat, Office No. 3, to Linda Cubbage, 1,985 votes to 1,865, by Carl Boyer's account.[6]

He was born on September 18, 1933, and died on October 9, 2018, at 85.[1]

## William J. Broyles (#30221), full

William J. Broyles, a custodian at College of the Canyons, led a drive to recall its trustees in 1978 and was elected to the board of the Santa Clarita Community College District the next year; he later served on the board again, and stood for it as late as 2001.[1][2][3][4]

In March 1978 The Canyon Call reported a recall movement "headed by William Broyles, custodian," among classified employees unhappy with the board; he said the trustees had "put too much power in the hands of the administration" and held too many closed sessions.[1] Carl Boyer, then a trustee, writes that Broyles's letter to the board named Peter Huntsinger, Francis Claffey and Kevin Lynch, and that the recall "went nowhere."[5] In November 1978 Broyles ran unopposed, listed as a "school employee," for supervisor of the fifth district of the proposed Canyon County, and was elected with 1,935 votes to an office that never existed, because the county's formation failed.[6]

Boyer, drawing on the Daily News of August 1987, writes that Broyles "parleyed that win into a successful run for a seat on the college district board in 1979," became president of the board, and lost a bid for re-election in 1983 by four votes.[2] The district's history of its board lists him for 1980-1981 and 1982-1984.[3] In August 1987 he filed for the new city's first council and, by Boyer's account, "for reelection to the community college board"; told he had to choose, he chose the college board.[7] The district's history lists him again from 1988-1989 to 1991, and not for 1984-1985 or 1986-1987.[3] How that service ended is not known.[3] The Daily News described him in 1987 as a maintenance worker for the Glendale Unified School District.[2] On November 6, 2001, he stood for Seat No. 3 as a "School Facility Supervisor" and lost to Joan Whaling MacGregor, 9,815 votes to 7,388.[4]

## Donald M. Benton (#30223), full

Donald M. Benton was a student trustee of College of the Canyons and then, from 1981, an elected trustee of the Santa Clarita Community College District for two terms, before a career in the Washington legislature.[1][2][3]

He later served one term in the Washington House of Representatives and twenty years, from 1997 to 2017, in the Washington State Senate, and directed Clark County's Environmental Services Department from 2013 to 2016.[3] In April 2017 President Donald J. Trump announced his intent to nominate Benton as Director of the Selective Service System.[3]

## Michele R. Jenkins (#30233), full

Michele Jenkins sat on the board of the Santa Clarita Community College District, which runs College of the Canyons, for nearly forty years, until her death in February 2023.[1][2] She had been a student at the college in its first semester, in the fall of 1969, when its classes met in the evenings at Hart High School.[3][2] In 2015 the college called her the longest continuously serving member of its board.[3]

The district's accounts date her first election to 1984, and a college release of September 2004 said that by that December she would have served on the board for twenty years; the district's board history first lists her on the board of 1986-1987.[4][5][6]

She was the board's president in 1988, when it hired Dianne G. Van Hook to lead the college, and the college credits her with leading that search.[7][3] The college counted her presidency for 2014 as her eighth; The Signal, from figures the college supplied in 2023, gave her six terms as president, five as vice president and seven as clerk.[8][9]

She held Seat No. 2 while the trustees were elected at large: unopposed in 1995, re-elected in 2003 with 6,156 votes over Gordon Frank Richiusa and Michael Erwin, and unopposed in 2011.[10] After the district moved to trustee areas she won Trustee Area 4 in 2016 with 10,396 votes to Jerry Danielsen's 8,276, and again in 2020 with 13,306 to his 13,103.[10]

She sat on the board of the California Community College Trustees from 1994 to 2005, by the district's biography of her, and in September 2004, while president of that board, became president of the Community College League of California.[4][5] In 2015 the League gave her its "Fulfilling the Trust" award for her years of service.[3] The same biography lists an associate degree from the college, a bachelor's degree in anthropology from UCLA and a master's in counseling from the University of North Dakota, and gives her work as managing a family medical practice.[4]

She died on February 6, 2023, while a trustee, and on March 22 the board appointed Jerry Danielsen to her seat.[2][1] That month the board named the college's softball team room for her, and in January 2024 the college's foundation created the Michele R. Jenkins Leadership Endowment.[11][12]

## Joan W. MacGregor (#30239), full

Joan MacGregor sat on the board of the Santa Clarita Community College District, which runs College of the Canyons, for 31 years, from her election in 1993 until she resigned in August 2024; before that she had been a trustee of the Sulphur Springs Union School District.[1][2][3] The district's biography of her gives her twenty years on the Sulphur Springs board, and lists her as founder of the Santa Clarita Valley Trustees Association, a past PTA president and president of the valley's PTA Council.[4]

Carl Boyer, in his account of his first term as mayor, which began in December 1990, describes her as president of the Sulphur Springs board, criticizing Councilwoman Jan Heidt after the City Council sent back for revision the district's plan to build a shopping center on the site of an old school to pay for a new one.[5] In 2012 she was on the campaign committee for Measure CK, a Sulphur Springs school bond.[6]

On the college board she held Seat No. 3 while the trustees were elected at large: unopposed in 1997, and re-elected in 2001 over William J. Broyles, in 2005 over Theresa Todd and Ronald A. Vitale, by 16,751 votes to 15,621 and 3,024, and in 2009 over Randy R. Moberg.[7][8] In 2013 she was the only candidate for her seat and was appointed in lieu of election.[9] After the district moved to trustee areas she won Trustee Area 5 in 2018 over Ann-Marie Bjorkman, 7,229 votes to 5,882, and in 2022 she ran unopposed.[7][1]

Her candidate statement of 2005 gave her occupation as owner of Valley Oaks Properties and named her a past education director of the California Association of Realtors; the district's biography calls her vice president of Valley Oaks Properties, a real estate brokerage.[10][4] The biography calls her a three-time president of the board; its board history lists her as president in 1997, 2002, 2005 and 2009.[4][11] She sat on the Canyon Country Site Selection Advisory Committee, by the same biography, and in 2024 she named the opening of the Canyon Country campus in 2007 among the things she was proudest of.[4][2] In December 2022 the college called her the longest-serving local public official.[1]

She announced in April 2024 that she would leave before her term ended in 2026, and her resignation took effect on August 5, 2024.[2][3] Because she did not resign in time for a special election to be held with the November election, the board filled the seat by appointment, and she said at one of her last meetings, as The Signal reported, that she had timed it so on purpose, because more people apply for an appointment than run for a seat.[12][13] The board appointed Carlos Guerrero to the seat on September 18, 2024.[14]

## Ronald E. Gillis (#30241), short

## Michael D. Berger (#30245), full

Mike Berger was president of the Santa Clarita Valley Chamber of Commerce in 1989, sat on the City of Santa Clarita's Planning Commission for most of the years from 1994 to 2008, and was a trustee of the Santa Clarita Community College District from 2009 to 2022.[1][2][3][4]

Leon Worden's captions to two chamber photographs call him president-elect of the chamber in May 1988 and its president in September 1989, when he was with Shearson Lehman Hutton.[5][1] By Worden's account he served on the Planning Commission from 1994 to 2008 except for 1996 to 1998, and the City's list of commissioners agrees.[1][3] As a commissioner in 2005 he spoke for the Downtown Newhall specific plan, which the commission approved and sent to the City Council.[6]

He was elected to Seat No. 1 on the college board on November 3, 2009, first of four candidates with 6,581 votes, under the ballot designation "Finance Businessman."[7] In 2013 he was the only candidate for the seat and was appointed in lieu of election.[8] The board's history lists him as its president in 2012, 2013 and 2019.[9] He retired from the board in December 2022 after thirteen years, and Chuck Lyon succeeded him in Trustee Area 1.[4]

Before he joined the board he had served twenty years on the board of the College of the Canyons Foundation, which gave him its Silver Spur Award for Community Service in 2000, and he was named Santa Clarita Valley Man of the Year for 2006.[4][10][11][12] A former student of the college, he was a senior vice president of Morgan Stanley by 2016.[13]

## Steven D. Zimmer (#30247), full

Steve Zimmer headed the Newhall Ranch division of The Newhall Land and Farming Company from 1999, leading the planning and approval of the company's development west of Interstate 5, and was a trustee of the Santa Clarita Community College District from 2013 to 2020.[1][2]

A real estate lawyer, he came to Newhall Land in 1999 from the Oxnard firm of Rupp, Holmberg & Zimmer, and had earlier been city attorney of El Centro and chief assistant county counsel of Imperial County.[1][3] By Leon Worden's report of 2013 he was responsible for getting the Newhall Ranch specific plan approved by the county Board of Supervisors in 2003.[2] After eleven years with Newhall Land, which he left as executive vice president, he joined the law firm of Gilchrist & Rutter in October 2011.[3]

He joined the board of the College of the Canyons Foundation in 2002 and chaired it from 2004 to 2007.[4][5] On January 14, 2013 the college board chose him from thirteen applicants to fill the seat left by Scott Wilk, who had been elected to the Assembly.[2] Because an appointee could serve no more than eighteen months without an election, he filed for the seat that summer; as the only candidate he was appointed in lieu of election in 2013, and again in 2016 for Trustee Area 3.[5][6][7]

The board's history lists him as its clerk in 2014-2015, vice president in 2016 and president in 2017-2018.[8] He retired from the board in December 2020 after eight years, and Sebastian Cazares succeeded him.[4] By the district's biography he also chaired the board of directors of the Child and Family Center.[9]

## Charles L. Lyon (#30253), full

Chuck Lyon was a player, coach, athletic director and dean at College of the Canyons, twenty-three years on its staff in all, and from 2022 to 2024 he sat on the board of the Santa Clarita Community College District, which runs the college.[1][2]

As the college's quarterback in 1973-1974 he scored the first touchdown in Cougar Stadium, by the district's biography of him, and he was the quarterback of the team that won the conference title in 1975 under coach Larry Reisbig.[1][3] The college's student newspaper reported two touchdowns by him in the homecoming win over Moorpark in November 1974.[4]

After twelve years as a faculty member and coach at Pasadena City College, he came back to College of the Canyons in 1998 as a faculty member and head football coach; his team won the national and state community college championships in 2004, and he was named National Community College Football Coach of the Year.[2][1] In February 2004 he was featured, as the college's head football coach, on SCV Newsmaker of the Week.[5] He was the college's athletic director from 2007 to 2016 and its dean and athletic director from 2017.[2]

With the endorsement of the retiring trustee Mike Berger, he stood for Trustee Area 1 in 2022, ran unopposed, and took his seat in December.[6][2] The board chose him as its vice president for 2024.[7] He resigned effective June 27, 2024, without giving a reason; because he resigned in time, the seat went on the November ballot, and Darlene Trevino won it.[8][9][10]

## Fred Arnold (#30261), full

Fred Arnold, a mortgage adviser who founded American Family Funding in Santa Clarita, was chairman of the Santa Clarita Valley Chamber of Commerce in 2013 and was elected to the board of the Santa Clarita Community College District for Trustee Area 3 in 2024.[1][2][3]

The chamber's announcement of his chairmanship, in April 2012, said he hosted the SCVTV program "Out of the Rough" and a KHTS radio program for chamber members.[1] He had been president of a state association of mortgage brokers, which the announcement calls the California Association of Mortgage Professionals and the district's biography the California Association of Mortgage Brokers.[1][4] In June 2012, as the chamber's chairman-elect, he appeared on SCV Newsmaker of the Week to speak about the valley's military veterans.[5]

He chaired the board of the College of the Canyons Foundation and the district's Independent Citizens Bond Oversight Committee, by the district's biography, and was chairing the foundation when he ran for the board in 2024.[4][6] He first stood for Trustee Area 3 in 2020, as a "Finance Business Advisor," and lost to Sebastian Cazares, 12,572 votes to 13,608.[3] In 2024, with Cazares not running, he won the seat with 12,271 votes, over Andrew "Andy" Taban and Tasha C. Hoggatt.[3][7] The board chose him as its vice president for 2025 and 2026.[8][9]
