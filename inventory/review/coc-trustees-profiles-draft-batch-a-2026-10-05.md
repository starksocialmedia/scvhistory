## William G. Bonelli Jr. (#30199), full, 416 words

William G. Bonelli Jr. was president of the first board of the Santa Clarita Community College District, elected with it in November 1967, and the college's first permanent building, the Instructional Resource Center, was named for him after he died in office in 1972.[1][2][3][4]

He was in the valley long before the college: the program of the Newhall-Saugus Rodeo of April 1948, held at the Bonelli Ranch in Saugus, names "Dr. W. G. Bonelli, Jr." as one of its resident veterinarians.[5] A study written for the Newhall Land and Farming Company in December 1953 credits him with a residential tract a few miles north of Newhall, "by far the best residential development in the Newhall area"; Leon Worden's biography of his father, William G. Bonelli (1895-1970), describes the Bonelli tract as the father's.[6][7] By Worden's lists he was president of the local chamber of commerce in 1955, with the Santa Clarita Water Co. as his business, and a director of the agency that became the Castaic Lake Water Agency from its organization in 1962 to 1969.[8][9] The Newhall-Saugus Chamber of Commerce named him its Outstanding Citizen of the Year for 1966, and the 1969 telephone directory lists "Dr. Bill Bonelli" among the chamber's directors.[10][7][11]

At the board's first meeting in December 1967, The Newhall Signal reported, he asked that the rules of order be modified so that he could vote, and two days later, when the board named the district the Santa Clarita Valley Junior College District, he refused an audience member's request for a roll call: "Only a board member can ask for a roll-call vote."[2] He was still president in May 1969, when the board named the college College of the Canyons, and told objectors that the valley's lack of a name was "a problem all public districts face here."[12] He was re-elected in April 1971.[1]

He died on February 22, 1972, during his second term; Carl Boyer, who later sat on the same board, writes that it was of cancer.[13][14] The Canyon Call, announcing his death, called him "the board's first president, serving for two years," and in March 1972 reported the contract for the Instructional Resource Center, "named in honor of the late trustee."[3] His seat was filled at a special election that June.[15] Governor Ronald Reagan dedicated the Dr. William G. Bonelli Center for Instructional Resources on April 22, 1974.[4] The college's homecoming scholarships to the senior class presidents of Hart and Canyon high schools carried his name in the 1970s.[16]

## Edward Muhl (#30201), full, 236 words

Edward Muhl was a founding trustee of the Santa Clarita Community College District, its first vice president, and in 1972 its president.[1][2]

Carl Boyer, who served with him, calls him "Charge of Production at Universal International" and a trustee who "had pull," and Leon Worden reads Boyer as saying Muhl used his connections at Universal to bring Governor Ronald Reagan to dedicate the college's first permanent building in April 1974.[3][4] When he stood for re-election in 1975, The Canyon Call described him as a "retired film producer" of Saugus.[5]

He was elected with the first board in November 1967.[1] In May 1969, when the board named the college College of the Canyons over objections from the audience that the name had no place in it, he answered one protester, "That's the problem."[6] He was re-elected in April 1971.[1] By February 1972 he was the board's president; that spring he presented the trophy to the college's scholar-athlete of the year, and in June he conferred the degrees at commencement.[2][7]

On March 4, 1975, he was one of nine candidates, three of them incumbents, for three full terms on the board.[5] He is not among the members The Canyon Call names in May 1976, and Boyer writes that Lou Reiter and Jim Rentz "succeeded Ed Muhl and Don Allen."[8][3] The district's history of its board lists him to 1974-1975.[9] The result of 1975 for him is not known.[5][9]

## Sheila Dyer (#30203), retrospective, 151 words

Sheila Dyer was one of the five trustees elected with the first board of the Santa Clarita Community College District on November 21, 1967.[1] In December 1967 The Newhall Signal reported that she "had been quite open about her support" of the name Santa Clarita from the board's first meeting, and that she read to the board from the minutes of a state geographic subcommittee meeting at which the name had been urged; the board chose the name Santa Clarita Valley Junior College District that night.[2]

Her years on the board are given two ways: the district's history of its board lists her in 1967 only and John Hackney from 1968, while the college's histories say Hackney replaced her in 1969.[3][1] How her service ended is not known.[3]

That Signal report is the one account written at the time that is cited here; the rest comes from the college's later histories.[2][1][3]

## Bruce D. Fortine (#30205), full, 447 words

Bruce Fortine was a founding trustee of the Santa Clarita Community College District, and served on its board in two spans, from 1967 to 1973 and again from the early 1990s until 2016; between them he worked for the college, where he started its community services department.[1][2][3]

The Signal called him a Hart High School alumnus of 1955.[4] In 1968 he chaired the coronation ball of the Jaycees' July 4th Old West Celebration in Newhall, and the 1969 telephone directory lists him among the directors of the Newhall-Saugus-Valencia Chamber of Commerce.[5][6] The Canyon Call wrote in 1973 that he had been on the steering committee that planned the college.[7]

He was elected with the first board in November 1967, and the college gives December 11, 1967 as the day he was sworn in.[1][8] At the meeting that named the district that December, The Newhall Signal reported, he "argued at some length that there were a number of other names that would probably serve just as well or better," and proposed a neutral name.[9] He was vice president-clerk in May 1971 and vice president in February 1972, and was board president in 1972-1973.[10][11][12] He was re-elected to a four-year term in the spring of 1973.[7] In July 1973, by the college's account, he was hired by the college and had to resign from the board; as an employee he created its community services and non-credit department and served as its public information officer, and he worked there until August 1978.[1] The Canyon Call names him director of community services in 1977.[13]

He returned to the board in 1991, which the college describes as election to fill a seat and The Signal as filling a vacancy; the district's history of its board lists him again from 1992.[1][2][3] He was re-elected to Seat No. 4 unopposed in November 1995, with 6,131 votes, under the ballot designation "Corporate Executive"; in November 2003, first of three with 5,126; and in November 2011, by 6,954 votes to Diana Shaw's 6,127.[14] In the first election by trustee area, on November 8, 2016, he lost Trustee Area 2 to Edel Alonso, 7,261 votes to 6,532, with Ronda Baldwin-Kennedy third.[14][15] He was board president at his last meeting, in December 2016.[2]

The college says he worked behind the scenes on each of its five local bond measures and raised money for its foundation, including for the Dr. Dianne G. Van Hook University Center, the Institute for Culinary Education and the Santa Clarita Performing Arts Center, and that he served as the trustees' liaison to the foundation's board.[1] Its foundation named him the 2020 recipient of its Silver Spur community service award, presented on May 14, 2022.[1][16]

Peter F. Huntsinger, a turkey rancher, was a founding trustee of the Santa Clarita Community College District and was its president more than once in its first decade.[1][2][3][4]

His family raised turkeys in Bouquet Canyon from 1951 and moved the business to Acton in 1964; Leon Worden writes that Peter's standing as a business leader lent the college credibility with voters on its first construction bond, and that he was a Rotarian and president of the Acton Chamber of Commerce in the 1970s.[5] In 1975 The Canyon Call gave his occupation as "turkey rancher."[6]

He was elected with the first board in November 1967, and at the board's meeting of December 1967 it was his motion that gave the district the name Santa Clarita.[1][7] He was re-elected in April 1971, and was board president at the college's sports awards banquet of May 1971.[1][2] As president in April 1974 he received the Instructional Resource Center for the district at its dedication, and he presided at that June's commencement.[3] He stood as an incumbent in March 1975 and remained on the board; he was its outgoing president in May 1976.[6][4] In March 1978 he voted, with Kevin Lynch and Francis Claffey, to sell the college's remaining $2 million in authorized bonds before the Jarvis initiative could stop it.[8] That year a recall drive led by William Broyles named him, Claffey and Lynch.[9]

Carl Boyer writes that Huntsinger "resigned from the district governing board in the middle of the board meeting on June 3," in an account of 1983; the district's history of its board lists him last in 1980-1981.[9][10]

## Francis T. Claffey (#30211), full, 288 words

Francis T. Claffey was the Santa Clarita Valley's postmaster for more than two decades, first in Newhall and then over the consolidated Newhall and Saugus post offices, and a trustee of the Santa Clarita Community College District from 1972 into 1979.[1][2][3]

He was installed as acting postmaster of Newhall on March 31, 1964, succeeding Eva Pullar.[1] Leon Worden notes that in 1973 he became postmaster of Saugus, the valley's first postmaster appointed on merit rather than by patronage.[1] His obituary gives him 22 years as postmaster in all.[2]

He joined the college board in 1972 in the seat of William G.
