Yes, on every count in "Who gets a person record": he owned The Signal from 1963 to 1978 and edited it to 1988; he is the subject of photographs and pages in the archive (TN4401, LW2849, LW3081, the 1963 purchase, the 1992 special edition, the oral history UC8901); his wife's record (#15477) and her obituary (#28051) point at him. His connection is his own, not inherited: The Signal, not the land.

## Descent: verdict

**Scott Newhall is a great-grandson of Henry Mayo Newhall, through Henry's third son, Edwin White Newhall, and Edwin's son, Almer Mayo Newhall.** Six sources in the archive say so, one of them Scott's own words:

Two cautions. On p. 318 Scott also says "Almer Halls' granddaughter married Henry Mayo Newhall's grandson"; that is a generation off in both places (Fannie Hall was Almer Ives Hall's daughter, and Edwin was Henry's son), and his own p. 1 and LW3325 and LW3564 settle it. Most of these are not independent: LW3325, LW2915, LW3564 and TN4401 are Leon Worden's, and the family's own book (Ruth Newhall, 1992) likely lies behind several. The independent ones are Scott's own interview, the 1936 wire caption and the 1963 Signal.

Wikipedia (finding aid only, not cited) also calls him a great-grandson; nothing here rests on it.

### Body (editorial-2026)

> Scott Newhall owned The Newhall Signal and Saugus Enterprise from 1963 to 1978 and, with his wife, Ruth Newhall, ran it until 1988, while it grew from a small weekly into a daily known for his front-page editorials.[10][2][1][3] He bought it, effective November 1, 1963, while executive editor of the San Francisco Chronicle, and in 1968 he and Ruth moved to the Piru Mansion.[4][1] They sold a controlling interest to the Morris Newspaper Corp. in 1978 and stayed on as editors; when their ten-year noncompete expired in 1988 they started the Santa Clarita Valley Citizen, which closed on May 3, 1989.[2] He was a director of The Newhall Land and Farming Company.[3][5]
>
> He was a great-grandson of Henry Mayo Newhall, the town's founder. His father was Almer Mayo Newhall, and his grandfather was Edwin White Newhall, Henry Mayo Newhall's son by his first wife, Sarah Ann White.[6][7][8][9] Born in San Francisco on January 21, 1914, he joined the Chronicle as a photographer, in 1934 by one account and 1935 by another, and was its executive editor from 1952; in 1971 he ran for mayor of San Francisco.[1][4] He died on October 26, 1992, at Henry Mayo Newhall Memorial Hospital in Valencia.[1]

### Discrepancies shown, not settled

- Chronicle start: 1934 (TN4401, and the #4583 caption) or 1935 (The Signal 1963; the oral history's own title, "1935-1971"). The body gives both.
- Publisher: the 1994 Signal anniversary edition lists him as owner 1963 to 1978 but publisher only 1963 to 1977, with Tony Newhall publisher 1977 to 1988. The body says owner 1963 to 1978 and that he and Ruth ran it until 1988 (UC8901), which both lists allow.
- Atholl McBean "vowed to make his nephew pay" (California Business 1989). McBean was the husband of Scott's father's first cousin (LW3325), not his uncle. Not used.
