# State Senate: is the 2021-plan SD 23 the 2011-plan SD 21 renumbered?

Claude, 4 October 2026. Read-only research for Nathan. Nothing was written to the database.

Question: Scott Wilk held the 21st State Senate District (2011 Citizens Redistricting Commission lines) from December 2016 to December 2024. Suzette Martinez Valladares has held the 23rd State Senate District (2021 CRC lines) since 2 December 2024. Same territory under a new number, or a different seat?

**Answer: the same territory, renumbered.** About 92 percent of the new 23rd's people lived in the old 21st, and about 93 percent of the old 21st's people are in the new 23rd. The number 21 went to a coastal district (Santa Barbara County, western Ventura County and the southern end of San Luis Obispo County). That district shares no territory with Wilk's old 21st.

## Plain statement for the public record

For Scott Wilk's record and Suzette Martinez Valladares's record:

> Under the 2021 redistricting, the 21st State Senate District's territory became, with small changes, the 23rd District: 92 percent of the new 23rd's 2020 population had lived in the old 21st, and 93 percent of the old 21st's population went into the new 23rd. The number 21 passed to an unrelated coastal district built around Santa Barbara County, so Valladares holds Wilk's seat under a new number.

(The record pages will need a short form. Suggested wording: "The 21st District was renumbered the 23rd in the 2021 redistricting, with small boundary changes.")

## 1. Where the 2021-plan SD 23's population came from

2020 Census population (unadjusted) of the 2021-plan SD 23: **1,036,642**.

| 2011-plan district | Population | Share | Where |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **SD 21** | **953,201** | **91.95%** | LA County 653,243; San Bernardino County 299,958 |
| SD 27 | 55,802 | 5.38% | All in LA County: part of the City of Santa Clarita 33,002, Stevenson Ranch CDP 20,178, unincorporated land 2,622 |
| SD 23 (2011 plan) | 27,311 | 2.63% | All in San Bernardino County: Phelan 13,859, Piñon Hills 7,258, Wrightwood 4,720, Lytle Creek 725, other 749 |
| SD 25 | 210 | 0.02% | LA County, unincorporated |
| SD 16 | 118 | 0.01% | San Bernardino County |

The old-21 core of the new 23rd: Santa Clarita (195,671 of it), Lancaster (173,516), Palmdale (169,450), Victorville (134,810), Hesperia (99,818), Adelanto (38,046), Castaic, Lake Los Angeles, Sun Village, Quartz Hill, Spring Valley Lake, Oak Hills, Acton, Agua Dulce, Val Verde and the rest of the Antelope Valley.

## 2. Where the 2011-plan SD 21's population went

2020 Census population of the 2011-plan SD 21: **1,029,111**.

| 2021-plan district | Population | Share | Where |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **SD 23** | **953,201** | **92.62%** | as above |
| SD 19 | 75,812 | 7.37% | Town of Apple Valley (75,783) and 29 people outside it, San Bernardino County |
| SD 20 | 98 | 0.01% | LA County, unincorporated |
| SD 25 | 0 | 0% | uninhabited blocks only |

So the change was small: Apple Valley left, and the southwest corner of the Santa Clarita Valley (from the old SD 27) and the Wrightwood and Phelan area (from the old SD 23) came in.

**Check against the valley figures in `legislative-districts-2026-10-04.md`:** on these 2020 files the valley was 80.9% SD 21 and 19.1% SD 27 under the 2011 plan, and 100% SD 23 under the 2021 plan. That earlier report gave 79% and 21% on 2010 population. The method agrees, and the small difference comes from growth between 2010 and 2020.

## 3. Where the 2021-plan SD 21 is

The 2021-plan SD 21 covers all of **Santa Barbara County** (448,229), **western and coastal Ventura County** (520,958) and the **southern end of San Luis Obispo County** (55,503). Its 2020 population is 1,024,690. The largest places are Oxnard, Ventura, Santa Maria, Santa Barbara, Camarillo, Lompoc, Goleta, Orcutt, Santa Paula, Port Hueneme, Nipomo, Fillmore and Grover Beach.

- 94.4% of its people (967,791) came from the 2011-plan SD 19, 55,503 from the 2011-plan SD 17, and 1,396 from the 2011-plan SD 27.
- **It shares no population with Wilk's old 21st.** Every block of the old 21st lies in Los Angeles or San Bernardino County, and every block of the new 21st lies in Ventura, Santa Barbara or San Luis Obispo County.
- The CRC report (page 72) describes it as "Santa Barbara County and portions of San Luis Obispo and Ventura Counties ... coastal communities and smaller, rural, agricultural communities."
- S. Monique Limón (D) won the new SD 21 in November 2024 over Elijah Mack (R). The Secretary of State marks her with an asterisk as an incumbent (`sos/general-election-nov-5-2024/37-state-senator.pdf`). That she had held the 2011-plan SD 19 fits the overlap above but is NEEDS_VERIFICATION from the Senate Record.

## What the CRC 2021 final report says

File: `S/crc/Final-Maps-Report-with-Appendices-12.26.21-230-PM-1.pdf` (Citizens Redistricting Commission, Final Report, 26 December 2021). Page numbers below are the PDF's own.

The report never compares any new district with an old one by number, and it does not mention the old 21st. Three passages bear on the question.

**SD 23 (page 72):**
> SD 23 is based on nesting AD 39 and AD 40 and includes portions of Los Angeles and San Bernardino Counties, including the whole Cities of Adelanto, Hesperia, Lancaster, Palmdale, Santa Clarita, and Victorville. This district includes the High Desert communities of the Antelope Valley and parts of the Victor Valley. This district groups together communities with similar suburban backgrounds and commuter patterns. The region is home to clean energy and aerospace industries.

**How the numbers were chosen (Section V.H, "Numbering of Districts," pages 49 to 50):**
> In light of these issues, the Commission chose a numbering alternative for Senate districts that best maintained continuity in terms of the placement of voters in odd and even numbered districts. In other words, if a voter lived in an odd-numbered Senate district during the last decade, the Commission chose the numbering alternative that maximized the likelihood that this same voter would remain in an odd-numbered Senate district for the next decade, thereby minimizing accelerations and deferrals.

The Commission kept odd districts odd so that voters would not lose or gain a Senate election. It did not try to keep each number on its old territory. That is why the old 21st (odd, elected 2020) became the 23rd (odd, first elected 2024) instead of keeping its number. The report does not say this about SD 23 by name: it is my reading of the general rule together with the overlap figures.

**Appendix 5, Nesting Report (page 208):** SD 23 total population 1,033,749, of which 772,983 (74.77%) lies in AD 39 and AD 40. The CRC's totals are on its adjusted population, which moves incarcerated people to their last home address (Elections Code section 21003; report Section III). That is why its 1,033,749 differs from the 1,036,642 unadjusted Census figure used here. The difference does not change the shares by more than a fraction of a point. That last point is an inference, NEEDS_VERIFICATION only if Nathan wants the adjusted-population figures run as well. The Statewide Database publishes the adjusted block file.

## Method

1. **2011 plan on 2020 blocks:** Census Bureau 2020 Census Block Assignment File for California, SLDU table (`BlockAssign_ST06_CA_SLDU.txt`). The Census Bureau assigns every 2020 block to the Senate district in effect for the 2020 Census, which is the 2011 CRC plan. These are official Census Bureau files, so the 2010-block Statewide Database file did not have to be carried forward to 2020 blocks.
2. **2021 plan on 2020 blocks:** Census Bureau 2022 State Legislative District Block Equivalency File, California upper chamber (`06_CA_SLDU22.txt`). It is built from the plan the CRC delivered to the Secretary of State.
3. Both files list the same 519,723 California blocks with no mismatches.
4. **Population:** 2020 Census block population (POP100, the PL 94-171 total) from the Census TIGERweb 2020 block layer.
   - The county totals match the 2020 Census exactly (Los Angeles 10,014,009; San Bernardino 2,181,654), and every block in the two files has a population.
   - The Census Data API now refuses requests without a key, so TIGERweb was used instead.
5. Each block was assigned under both plans and the populations were summed (`analysis/sd21_sd23.py`). Place names come from the 2020 place and CDP block assignment file.
6. Every block of the old SD 21 and of the new SD 23 is in Los Angeles or San Bernardino County, so those two counties hold the whole of both districts.
