## Answers in brief

1. **Seats up in November 2024: District 1 and District 3 only**, as Ordinance 23-4 provides. District 1 was contested (Ayala, Jepsen, Burkhart). District 3 had one nominee, and its election was cancelled. Districts 2, 4 and 5 come up first in November 2026.
2. **Gibbs was the only qualified candidate for District 3.** The County lists the seat as cancelled, "Santa Clarita (Council District 3)", under "APPOINTMENT IN LIEU OF ELECTION DUE TO INSUFFICIENCY OF CANDIDATES". The City Council, not the County, appointed him, at a special meeting on 19 August 2024 under Elections Code 10229.
3. **Yes. The three members elected at large in 2022 stay at large until their terms end in December 2026.** At large now: Laurene Weste, Marsha McLean, Bill Miranda. By district: Patsy Ayala (District 1, elected 2024) and Jason Gibbs (District 3, appointed in lieu of election 2024).
4. **Weste was not elected in 2025.** No council election was held in 2025. Her current term is the one she won at large on 8 November 2022, her seventh. The "2025" comes from the City's council page, which lists 2025 among her "Years Elected". That matches the council naming her mayor on 9 December 2025 (mayor for 2026), not any vote of the public. No special election, resignation, reappointment or recall happened.
5. **The statute's term.** Section 10229 never says "in lieu of election". It says the council may "Appoint to the office the person who has been nominated", and that the appointee "shall qualify and take office and serve exactly as if elected at a municipal election for the office." "Appointment in lieu of election" is the County's wording, not the statute's. Section 10229 governs a city council seat, general-law city included, when the election is consolidated with the statewide one. Section 10403 governs the consolidation itself. Section 10515 is the Uniform District Election Law and does not apply to cities. School boards fall under Education Code 5326 and 5328.

## 5. The statutes

**Elections Code 10229** (Division 10, Part 2 "Municipal Elections", Chapter 2 "Regulations Governing Elections in Cities", Article 2 "Nomination of Candidates"). It governs the 2024 District 3 case. Operative words, current text:

> "(a) If, by the 88th day ... before the day fixed for a regularly scheduled municipal election ... (ii) no one or only one person is nominated to be elected from or by a legislative district, ... the city elections official shall submit a certificate of these facts to the governing body of the city and inform the governing body of the city that it may, at a regular or special meeting held before the municipal election, adopt one of the following courses of action:
> (1) Appoint to the office the person who has been nominated.
> (2) Appoint to the office an eligible elector if no one has been nominated.
> (3) Hold the election, if either no one or only one person has been nominated. ...
> The person appointed, if any, shall qualify and take office and serve exactly as if elected at a municipal election for the office.
> Notwithstanding Section 10403, if, by the 75th day before the municipal election, no person has been appointed to office pursuant to paragraph (1) or (2), the election shall be held."

The last paragraph of the section reads: "If an appointment to office is made in a particular legislative district pursuant to subdivision (a), that appointment shall not affect the conduct of the municipal election in other legislative districts of the city."

Subdivision (b) excludes the case where another office "to be elected on a citywide basis" is contested. District 1 is not citywide, so (b) did not apply.

The saved text is as amended by Stats. 2024, ch. 355 (AB 1784), effective 1 January 2025. NEEDS_VERIFICATION: the exact wording in force in August 2024. The City's notice of 9 August 2024 quotes the same three courses and the same "serve exactly as if elected" sentence, so the substance did not change.

**The term.** The statute calls it an appointment: "Appoint to the office the person who has been nominated", and the appointee serves "exactly as if elected". The words "in lieu of election" do not appear in 10229, 10515, or Education Code 5326 or 5328. "Appointment in lieu of election" is the County Registrar-Recorder's heading. The City's notice is headed "Notice That There Are Not More Candidates Than Offices To Be Elected".

**Which section governs.** Santa Clarita is a general-law city (Candidate Handbook 2024). Its council seats are municipal offices under Part 2, so a sole nominee is handled by Elections Code 10229, and the council makes the appointment. Elections Code 10403 (Part 3, "Consolidation of Elections") governs only the consolidation with the statewide election: the resolution requesting it, the ballot form, and filing candidate names with the county 81 days before. Section 10229 expressly overrides 10403 on the 75-day point.

**Elections Code 10515** is the Uniform District Election Law (Part 4). It applies to "all districts and agencies whose principal acts so provide" (section 10502). There, the officer conducting the election certifies the facts "to the supervising authority" (the Board of Supervisors), which shall "appoint to the office or offices the person or persons, if any, who have filed declarations of candidacy", and "The person appointed shall qualify and take office and serve exactly as if elected at a general district election for the office." It does not govern cities.

**School districts: Education Code 5326 and 5328.** Under 5326, if nominees do not exceed seats by the 83rd day and no petition is presented, "appointment will be made as prescribed by Section 5328". Section 5328 reads: "If pursuant to Section 5326 a district election is not held, the qualified person or persons nominated shall be seated at the organizational meeting of the board, ... and such appointee or appointees shall be seated at the organizational meeting of the board as if elected at a district election." NEEDS_VERIFICATION: whether the Los Angeles County Board of Supervisors adopts a formal appointing resolution for school board seats in practice, as the archive's footnotes say. The statute does not require one.
