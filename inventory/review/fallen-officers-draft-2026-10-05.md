## Constable McCoy Pyle

| | |
|---|---|
| Agency | Constable of Fillmore, Ventura County (the Foundation lists his department as "Ventura Constable"); no organization record exists |
| Rank | Constable |
| Assignment | Fillmore |
| End of watch | April 24, 1897 (`1897-04-24`) |
| Place | The Castac switch on the railroad, "four miles from Saugus" |
| What happened | Shot through the head before dawn while holding two men for a Fillmore robbery; Edward McCamish was convicted of his murder. |
| Tie to the valley | Killed in the valley |

McCoy Pyle was a constable at Fillmore, in Ventura County. [1, 2] He was shot dead at about four in the morning of April 24, 1897, at the Castac switch, "four miles from Saugus, in Los Angeles county." [2]

The first reports said robbers had beaten and chloroformed Ed McCamish in his room at Fillmore and robbed him, and that Pyle and his deputy set out after them. [1, 2] They traced two men to Castac, where the deputy handcuffed one while Pyle covered them with his pistol, and in the dark a shot was fired and Pyle fell with a bullet through his head. [2] The two men were reported to have escaped, and posses from both counties went out after them. [1, 2]

That September, Edward McCamish was sentenced to Folsom for life, "recently convicted of murdering Constable Pyle." [3] The Sentinel reported that the evidence showed he was infatuated with Pyle's wife and that, after going with the constable after "supposed robbers," he ambushed and shot him. [3] Pyle left a widow and three young children. [1, 2] His name is on the California Peace Officers' Memorial. [4]

**Memorials**

- California Peace Officers' Memorial, Sacramento. Listed on the Foundation's honor roll, plaque 31. [4]

**Editor's notes**

- *Two accounts of the shooting.* The April reports, written the day after, say robbers in Pyle's custody shot him and escaped (notes 1 and 2). The September report says Edward McCamish, the man who had reported the robbery, was convicted of the murder and that he ambushed Pyle after going with him after "supposed robbers" (note 3). The later report records the verdict; the earlier ones record what was first believed.
- *His age.* The Chronicle gives 29 and the Herald "about 35" (notes 2 and 1). Neither is preferred here.

## Deputy Constable Charles A. De Moranville

| | |
|---|---|
| Agency | Los Angeles County township constable, Newhall Township (the Foundation and the Sheriff's Memorial Wall count him with the Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department, organization record #29282); no record exists for the township constables |

| Assignment | Newhall Township, under Constable Ed Pardee |
| End of watch | January 4, 1909 (`1909-01-04`) |
| Place | Along the railroad tracks at the edge of Newhall |
| What happened | Shot while trying to arrest John W. "Arizona Jack" Allen for a disturbance at a Newhall saloon; Allen's trial ended in a hung jury and the charge was dismissed. |
| Tie to the valley | Killed in the valley |

Charles De Moranville was deputy constable of Newhall under Constable Ed Pardee. [1, 2] A Newhall justice testified that he made 71 arrests in December 1908. [2]

On the night of January 4, 1909, he went after John W. "Arizona Jack" Allen, a ranch hand who had drawn a pair of revolvers in a Newhall saloon. [1] He caught up with Allen on the railroad tracks at the edge of town, they exchanged shots, and Allen's single bullet killed him. [1, 3] Sheriff William Hammel and Deputy Martin Aguirre arrested Allen at the old Kellogg ranch the next evening. [3]

Allen said he had been held up by robbers minutes before and took the constable for one of them. [3, 4] The district attorney's investigator reported that De Moranville "was regarded as one of the most careful officers in the county." [5] The jury at Allen's trial in June could not agree, and the murder charge was dismissed on July 13, 1909. [1, 6] De Moranville was 39 and left a widow. [1]

His name was added to the Memorial Wall at the Sheriff's Training Academy in Whittier on May 29, 2013, and is on the California Peace Officers' Memorial. [1, 7]

**Memorials**

- Memorial Wall, Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department Training Academy, Whittier. Name and plaque added May 29, 2013. [1]

## Deputy Constable J. Edward "Ed" Brown

| | |
|---|---|
| Agency | Los Angeles County township constable, Newhall Township, stationed at Saugus (counted by the Foundation and the Sheriff with the Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department, #29282); no record exists for the township constables |

| What happened | Shot in the stomach by Gus Le Brun, who ambushed him and Constable Pilcher when they answered Darling's complaint; he died that night in a Los Angeles hospital. |
| Tie to the valley | Killed in the valley |
| Born | July 1878 (`1878-07`) |
| Buried | Grand View Memorial Park, Glendale |

Ed Brown was a deputy constable stationed at Saugus, the deputy and friend of Newhall's constable, Jack Pilcher. [1, 2] The Sheriff's Department says he was born in Missouri in July 1878 and came to Saugus from Nevada with his wife, a registered nurse. [3]

On Sunday, September 14, 1924, the actress Bonita Darling asked for help against Gus Le Brun, a well driller who had threatened her at her ranch in Tunnel Canyon after she refused to marry him. [1, 2] Pilcher picked up Brown at Saugus and drove out, and Le Brun opened fire on them from the brush with a rifle. [1, 2] When Le Brun called out that he was dying and the two men went to him, he fired a revolver he had hidden, and the bullet passed through Brown's stomach. [1] Pilcher shot Le Brun dead, and Brown died in a Los Angeles hospital that night. [1, 2, 4]

He is buried at Grand View Memorial Park in Glendale. [1, 5] The Santa Clarita Valley Sheriff's Station added his name to its memorial wall on June 28, 2012, and his name is on the California Peace Officers' Memorial. [1, 3, 6]

**Memorials**

- Memorial wall, Santa Clarita Valley Sheriff's Station. Name added at the rededication of the station's memorial garden, June 28, 2012. [3]
- Memorial Wall, Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department Training Academy, Whittier. Name added May 2012. [1]
- California Peace Officers' Memorial, Sacramento, and the national memorial. Recognized in May 2013. [1, 6]

## Constable John S. "Jack" Pilcher

| | |
|---|---|
| Agency | Los Angeles County township constable, Newhall Township (constable from April 1922); no record exists for the township constables |
| Rank | Constable |
| Assignment | Newhall Township |
| End of watch | June 4, 1925 (`1925-06-04`) |
| Place | The Gage Ranch, Bouquet Canyon |
| What happened | Killed when a rookie deputy constable's revolver fell from its holster to a cement floor and fired, during a burglary stakeout. |
| Tie to the valley | Killed in the valley |
| Born | 1880 (`1880`) |
| Buried | Grand View Memorial Park, Glendale |

Jack Pilcher became constable of Newhall Township in April 1922, after twelve years as constable at Chatsworth and Calabasas. [1] He was born at Port Townsend, Washington, in 1880, and lived on his ranch at Chatsworth. [1] With his deputy Ed Brown he served on the Sheriff's dry squad, and in September 1924 he shot Gus Le Brun dead in the gunfight in Tunnel Canyon in which Brown was killed. [1, 2, 3]

On June 4, 1925, after a night watching the burglarized Gage Ranch in Bouquet Canyon, Pilcher and two deputy constables were carrying furniture out of the house when a lizard ran under a bed. [1] The newest deputy, John Seltzer, three days on the job, bent to look, and the revolver Pilcher had given him slipped from an old holster, struck the cement floor and fired. [1] The bullet struck Pilcher between the eyes, and he died about an hour and a half later. [1]

He was president of Newhall's Masonic lodge, and his brother Masons led his funeral procession. [1] He is buried at Grand View Memorial Park. [1, 4] His name was enrolled on the state and national peace officers' memorials in May 2014. [1]

**Memorials**

- California Peace Officers' Memorial, Sacramento, and the national memorial. Name enrolled at the memorial ceremonies of May 2014. [1]

## Officer Walter C. Frago

| | |
|---|---|
| Agency | California Highway Patrol, Newhall Area office; no organization record exists |
| Rank | Officer |
| Assignment | Newhall office; partner of Officer Gore |
| Badge | 6573 (Caltrans); 6520 (CHP memorial page) |
| End of watch | April 5 or 6, 1970 (`[1970-04-05,1970-04-06]`) |
| Place | Restaurant parking lot at the Golden State Freeway (I-5) and Henry Mayo Drive, now The Old Road at Magic Mountain Parkway |
| What happened | Shot twice in the chest by the passenger of a car he and Officer Gore had stopped, in the Newhall Incident. |
| Tie to the valley | Killed in the valley |

Walter C. Frago was one of four California Highway Patrol officers of the Newhall office killed in the gun battle known as the Newhall Incident, just before midnight on April 5, 1970; the official memorials date it April 6. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] He was 23. [2, 5]

Officers Walter Frago and Roger Gore, partners, had been alerted by radio to a car whose occupant had brandished a gun at another motorist, and they stopped it in a restaurant parking lot at the Golden State Freeway and Henry Mayo Drive. [3, 4] As Gore approached the driver, the passenger sprang out and shot Frago twice in the chest, and the driver turned and shot Gore; both died at once. [4, 5] The gunmen were Jack Twinning and Bobby Davis; Twinning killed himself the next morning in a house on Pico Canyon Road, and Davis was convicted of four counts of murder. [2, 4]

A brick memorial wall to the four, first dedicated on June 5, 1970, stands at the CHP's Newhall Area office, rebuilt there from the former office. [4, 6] Interstate 5 from Magic Mountain Parkway to Rye Canyon Road is the California Highway Patrol Officers James E. Pence, Jr., Roger D. Gore, Walter C. Frago, and George M. Alleyn Memorial Highway, named in 2006. [7] A portion of Route 99 in Merced County is named for Officers Frago and Gore, who both came from Merced County. [8]

**Memorials**

- Newhall Incident memorial wall, CHP Newhall Area office. Dedicated June 5, 1970, at the former office; rebuilt at the present office, about a mile from the scene. [4, 6]
- Four cypress trees and a memorial plaque, Newhall CHP station. Planted in memory of the four officers. [2]
- California Highway Patrol Officers James E. Pence, Jr., Roger D. Gore, Walter C. Frago, and George M. Alleyn Memorial Highway. Interstate 5, Magic Mountain Parkway to Rye Canyon Road undercrossing; SCR 93, Ch. 92, 2006. Pollack dates a naming ceremony to April 2008. [3, 7]
- CHP Officers Walter Frago and Roger Gore Memorial Highway. Route 99, Merced County, Mission Avenue to Campus Parkway; ACR 180, Ch. 147, 2000. [8]
- California Peace Officers' Memorial, Sacramento. Listed on the Foundation's honor roll, plaque 2. [5]

Clarence Wayne Dean is listed apart from the officers killed serving the valley because he was not on duty here: he died in the Newhall Pass on his way to work, riding in after the Northridge earthquake. [1, 2, 3] His department counts his death in the line of duty, and the valley's freeway interchange bears his name. [1, 3]

Dean had served the Los Angeles Police Department for 26 years, ten of them as a motorcycle officer, and was assigned to the Valley Traffic Division. [1, 2] He left his home in Lancaster within minutes of the 4:31 a.m. earthquake on January 17, 1994, though his shift did not start for two hours. [2, 4] In the dark he could not see that the transition from the Antelope Valley Freeway to the southbound Golden State Freeway had fallen, and he went off the broken end and fell to Interstate 5 below. [2, 5, 6] He was 46. [2, 5]

He was born in Los Angeles in 1947 and served four years in the Marine Corps before joining the department in 1968. [2, 3] More than 1,000 people came to his graveside service at Rose Hills in Whittier. [2] The Legislature named the junction of Interstate 5 and Route 14 the Clarence Wayne Dean Memorial Interchange in 1994. [1, 3]
