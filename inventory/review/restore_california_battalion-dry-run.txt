BODY NEW: The California Battalion was the force John C. Frémont commanded in the American conquest of California. Dr. Alan Pollack writes that in the summer of 1846 Frémont's men, "now referred to as the California Battalion," met Commodore Robert Stockton at Monterey.[1] In January 1847 the battalion came south through this valley. Jerry Reynolds has Frémont's hundred-man "buckskin battalion" arriving at Castaic Junction on the evening of January 9, 1847, and quotes Edwin Bryant, "a member of the battalion," on the rancho where it camped.[2] Two environmental impact reports prepared for projects in the valley, NorthLake (2017) and Tesoro del Valle (2018), say that "Fremont's battalion marched through the Santa Clara River Valley and south to Mission San Fernando."[3][4]
  [1] Alan Pollack, "Kit Carson, Ned Beale Cross Paths and Alter California's Future," Heritage Junction Dispatch, November-December 2014, as carried on SCVHistory.com, /scvhistory/pollack1114kitcarson.htm: "The Mexican-American War began in April 1846.
