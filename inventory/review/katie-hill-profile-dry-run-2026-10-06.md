## Body as it will read

Katie Hill represented the Santa Clarita Valley in the United States House of Representatives, for the 25th District, from 3 January 2019 until she resigned on 3 November 2019.[1] She grew up in Saugus, graduated from Saugus High School in 2004, and in 2019 lived in Agua Dulce.[2][1] On 6 November 2018 she won the seat from the incumbent, Steve Knight, by 133,209 votes to 111,813, 54.4 per cent of the vote.[3] She was the first Democrat to hold the 25th District since the 1991 lines put the valley in it: Republicans held it from January 1993, Buck McKeon until 2015 and Steve Knight after him.[4]

Before Congress she was the executive director of PATH (People Assisting The Homeless), by her official biography.[2] In the House she sat on the Armed Services, Oversight and Reform, and Science, Space, and Technology committees, and by her official biography her colleagues elected her Freshman Co-Representative to Leadership in the 116th Congress.[5][2] On 6 February 2019 she introduced H.R. 1015, with Julia Brownley, to establish a national memorial and national monument to those killed by the collapse of the St. Francis Dam on 12 March 1928.[6] The memorial became law as section 1111 of S. 47, the Natural Resources Management Act, for which she voted when the House passed it on 26 February 2019, by 363 votes to 62; it was approved on 12 March 2019.[7]

On 23 October 2019 the House Committee on Ethics announced that it was investigating public allegations that she "may have engaged in a sexual relationship with an individual on her congressional staff," in violation of House Rule XXIII, clause 18(a), and stated that the investigation did not itself indicate that any violation had occurred.[8] She denied the allegation.[9] The same day, in an email to her supporters, she acknowledged "a relationship with someone on my campaign" and apologized for it.[9] The rule the Committee cited applies to a Member's relationship with an employee of the House who works under the Member's supervision or for one of the Member's committees.[10] Intimate photographs of her had been published without her consent; she said that she had notified the Capitol Police, and later that their publication was illegal.[9][11] On 27 October she announced that she would resign.[11] She gave her last speech on the House floor on 31 October, and on 1 November wrote to the Speaker and to Governor Gavin Newsom that her resignation would take effect on 3 November 2019.[12][13] The Governor called a special election for 12 May 2020 to fill the vacancy, and Mike Garcia won it.[14]

In December 2020 she sued over the publication of the photographs in the Los Angeles County Superior Court, under California's statute giving a civil claim against distributing sexually explicit material without consent, Civil Code section 1708.85, which excepts material that "constitutes a matter of public concern."[15] The court struck her claims against the media defendants; in a ruling of April 2021 it found that the images were a matter of public concern under that exception. Her claim against the remaining defendant was settled in December 2023, on terms that were not made public.[16]
