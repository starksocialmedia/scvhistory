## Summary

* **Living or deceased: DECEASED (1977).** Born 1891, Bennington, Vermont (ap0828, lw2488 '1891 – 1977', reynolds part61, worden lw092596, lat19770102perkins). Died 1977 (ap0828, lw3113, lw3550, sg6002, reynolds part61). The LA Times profile of **Jan 2, 1977** describes him as living, so he died after that. The exact day is not on the site. A Death Master File entry (via sortedbyname.com: b. 12 Oct 1891, card issued in California, d. April 1977) is a plausible match but not proven (NEEDS_VERIFICATION). AI search summaries giving 'April 8, 1977' and 'Oct 11, 1891' cite nothing and are disregarded. No Find a Grave memorial or Wikidata item was found.
* **Life, from site sources:**
  * **Early years.** Raised by aunts in Bennington; studied at Middlebury College and the University of Arizona.
  * **Nevada, about 1906–1918.** Followed his brother P.V. Perkins to Nevada. Worked at the Carrara marble camp, running its newspaper. Married Marguerite O'Brien in 1915: at Rhyolite (Desmond) or in 'Pioneer' (LAT).
  * **Newhall, from 1919.** Came to Newhall in 1919 to manage the Newhall Water Co. and bought it from H. Clay Needham in 1920. Also a realtor (Healey & Perkins, 1926) and justice of the peace of Soledad Township (1920s–30s; 'Judge Perkins' at the 1930 Oak of the Golden Dream dedication).
  * **Civic life.** Co-organized the first big July 4 parade (1932). Suggested the 'Santa Clarita' name for the 1945 school district. Named Outstanding Citizen in 1964. Built the Perkins Building (1956), The Signal's home until 1986.
  * **Historian.** The Santa Clarita Valley's first town historian. President Emeritus at the SCVHS incorporation (Boyer 2015, finding aid).
* **Reliability: first-ranked local source on documents, weak on oral tradition.** The ledger has 58 Perkins claims checked against lifetime primary sources and the five earlier dossiers.
  * **Totals:** 18 contradicted, 6 partly right, 1 superseded, 8 conflicting, 8 unresolved, 17 confirmed. That is **25 wrong or partly wrong**, 16 conflicting or unresolved, and 17 confirmed.
  * **Where the confirmed claims come from:** 8 of the 17 are his transcriptions of documents; the rest are offices, quotations and dates that match documents.
  * **Required items:**
    * **'Rags':** Hart was not in it (P13).
    * **The 1-mile toll radius at Beale's Cut:** wrong; the statute says two miles (P15).
    * **'Soledad Township':** a valid term in his era; its start date is disputed (P58, P26).
    * **Del Valle in the 1856 Assembly:** wrong; 1856 was the Common Council (P18).
* **Perkins vs Reynolds:** 6 Reynolds errors trace back to Perkins (3 copied, 3 elaborated). Reynolds introduced at least 4 errors on points Perkins had right. Reynolds' 1998 web text corrects Perkins on 7 points; some of those may be the editor's corrections (Reynolds dossier C5).
* **Trust rule for Claude Code:** Trust Perkins where he transcribes or cites a document (deeds, decrees, petitions, Board minutes, newspapers): none of his document-based claims in this ledger was contradicted. Treat his old-timer hearsay, film and celebrity anecdotes, 'first' superlatives, family-relationship labels, institutional labels (asistencia, Assembly vs Council) and all Indigenous ethnography as unverified until a primary source is found. Spot-check his minor-event dates (typos and year slips are common), and prefer the editor's note where one exists.
* **Counts:** 16 conflicts; 17 live errors; 4 archive-beats; 7 corrections to earlier dossiers; 204 pages fetched.

## 2. Lifetime primary vs later

* **Made in his lifetime (primary for his life; secondary for the history he wrote):**
  * His own writings, 1930–1968 (1.a).
  * Documents naming him: rodeo program 1926; Hart letter to his wife 1933; dedication papers 1930–34; Dobson letter 1940s; Hart High 1949 photo; the 1963–65 photos; the 1964 honor.
  * The LA Times profile of Jan 2, 1977, written months before his death.
* **After his death:**
  * Worden columns 1996–97; Reynolds' 1998 web book (part61, part57, preface).
  * Desmond and NCWD 2001; Worden 2017 and 2019; the 2005 obituary of his son.
  * All editor's and webmaster's notes on his texts (web edition ©2000, 2013).
* **His texts are mixed.** perkins-rsf-1957 and perkins-pico-1958 are footnoted to deeds, Board minutes, probate, contemporary newspapers and the Bancroft. The 1954–55 series and the 1960s columns are largely unsourced and lean on old-timers.

### 2.1 His own sources, as the site shows them

* **Documents:**
  * LA County deed books, probate (Case No. 2) and Board of Supervisors minutes.
  * Land Case 303 SD and depositions (Case 318); Board of Education records; Post Office Department archives.
  * Originals at the Bancroft Library (the 1842 petition and appointment).
* **Newspapers:** LA Star (1863), Ventura Signal 1876, Alta California 1876, LA Express 1877, Ventura Weekly Free Press 1878, Wilmington Journal 1866.
* **Printed works:**
  * Bancroft; Thompson & West 1880; the 1889 *Illustrated History of Los Angeles County*; Guinn 1901; Lindley & Widney 1896.
  * Horace Bell 1881; the Pacific Railroad Surveys (vol. 5); the Butterfield volume (1954); Ripley (toll road); Youle; Division of Oil and Gas 1934.
  * For ethnography: J.P. Harrington (letter) and a 'Story of Candelaria' ms (Ventura Pioneer Museum, 1934) (CARE).
* **Informants (old-timers), named in his notes:**
  * The Giffords, Addi Lyon (1873–1951), Mrs. Dunn's family, Mabel Gifford Taylor, H. Clay Needham, Albert Swall, John Saunders, Lou Ledger and others.
  * The rsf editor: 'In extremely rare instances where no written record was available, he relied upon the memories of old timers'.
  * The ledger shows these oral items are where he goes wrong (P24, P34, P35).
* **Collaborators:**
  * His wife Marguerite did 'much of the original research on the Soledad Mining Camp' (perkins-newhall-1958).
  * Translation help on the 1842 document came from Hart High staff (part03).

### 2.3 Pattern of error (counts are of the wrong or partly wrong items)

* **Dates of minor events (typos, year slips) (12):** P1, P2, P3, P5, P10, P11, P12, P16, P17, P19, P21, P22
* **Institutional / ethnographic labels and Indigenous content (CARE) (4):** P6, P7, P8, P18

* **Family-relationship labels (2):** P9, P23
* **Numbers and distances (2):** P15, P25
* **Superlatives copied from plaques (1):** P4
* **Oral history from old-timers (1):** P24
* **Omissions (1):** P20

**What the patterns mean:**
1. **Small dates slip.** The minor-event dates are typos or year slips: July 6 for Sept 5; 1883 for 1888; 1856 for 1857; 1893 for 1882; 1886/1887 for 1888. His major documented dates are right.
2. **Labels are his biggest systemic weakness.** He applies 'asistencia' to an estancia and merges Assembly with Council. He also uses the Indigenous terminology of his day ('Alliklik', 'Venturenos', 'stone-age'). All of it needs editorial framing (CARE).
3. **Hollywood and superlatives are unreliable.** The 'Rags' cast, the garbled film titles, and 'first steam rig' (copied from a plaque).
4. **Oral history is hedged but propagates.** He usually marks hearsay ('probably', 'apparently', 'may have'). Later writers, above all Reynolds, dropped the hedges.
5. **Positive record:** every document he transcribes or cites checks out (P42–P48, P50–P51). The site's editors repeatedly use him to correct Reynolds and others (del Valle L4–L6, L8, L11–L12).

**Trust rule for Claude Code:** Trust Perkins where he transcribes or cites a document (deeds, decrees, petitions, Board minutes, newspapers): none of his document-based claims in this ledger was contradicted. Treat his old-timer hearsay, film and celebrity anecdotes, 'first' superlatives, family-relationship labels, institutional labels (asistencia, Assembly vs Council) and all Indigenous ethnography as unverified until a primary source is found. Spot-check his minor-event dates (typos and year slips are common), and prefer the editor's note where one exists. Where Perkins and Reynolds disagree on a documented fact (sums, acreage, offices, dates of record), prefer Perkins. Where they agree on an anecdote, count it as one source, not two: Reynolds often took it from Perkins.
