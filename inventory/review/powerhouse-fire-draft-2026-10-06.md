## Body

The Powerhouse Fire broke out on the afternoon of May 30, 2013, near the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power's Power House No. 1 in San Francisquito Canyon, from which it took its name, and spread north.[1] Its apparent point of origin was a hillside in the Angeles National Forest just above the power plant; a power plant operator first reported it, at 3:28 p.m.[2] The U.S. Forest Service took command of the fire, with Los Angeles County firefighters and the Sheriff's Department, and in the days that followed 2,185 firefighters worked on it.[3] That first evening it had burned 1,000 acres, and the residents of Green Valley were evacuated; the fire did not reach Green Valley.[4]

On the evening of Saturday, June 1, the wind turned the fire north into Lake Hughes, where four or five homes burned to the ground, most of them on the south side of Newview Drive.[5][6] By Sunday afternoon it had crossed the California Aqueduct into the Antelope Valley, and its spread was halted on Monday, June 3.[6] The lake communities of Lake Hughes and Elizabeth Lake bore the worst of it.[1] Many buildings came through, among them the Lakes Community Center, the Hughes-Elizabeth Lakes school, Papas Country Store and the Rock Inn of 1929; at The Painted Turtle, a camp for chronically ill children, the buildings were saved but the grounds were destroyed, and the camp's 2013 season was called off.[7] Nine firefighters were injured.[8] Returning residents were told to boil their drinking water.[9]

The fire burned on for some days on Sawtooth Mountain, and on June 10 the Forest Service handed command to the Los Angeles County Fire Department for mop-up.[10] By Leon Worden's account it blackened 30,274 acres, and 30 homes were declared total losses and 28 outbuildings were destroyed.[1][5] Governor Edmund G. Brown Jr.'s proclamation of a state of emergency, issued on July 12, 2013, gives the fire's dates as May 30 through June 11, 2013.[11] The Federal Emergency Management Agency agreed to pay up to three quarters of the cost of fighting it.[11][12] State and federal assessors surveyed the damage on June 17.[13]

Leon Worden's gallery of the fire on SCVHistory.com has 105 views, from the point of origin to the burned homes and the survivors of Lake Hughes and Elizabeth Lake.[14]

## Editor notes (bottom)

- **The count of homes lost.** Leon Worden (2013) and the SCVHistory.com timeline give 30 homes declared total losses and 28 outbuildings destroyed (notes 1 and 5). The NASA caption carried with LW2400 gives "at least 24 homes" destroyed and "at least 29 other structures" destroyed or damaged (note 6). The record gives the larger, later count, Leon Worden's; the NASA figures are given here with their source.
- **When the fire ended.** The sources mark the end in three ways. The NASA caption says the fire's spread "was halted on Monday" (June 3) (note 6). Leon Worden writes that the Forest Service handed command to the County for mop-up on June 10, 2013, and that "the smoke cleared two weeks later" (notes 1 and 10). The Governor's proclamation gives the wildfire's dates as May 30 through June 11, 2013 (note 11). The record's end date, June 11, is the proclamation's.
- **Deaths.** No source in the archive reports a death in the fire. The pages of the LW2382 to LW2400 series, the proclamation and the timeline were searched; they report nine firefighters injured (note 8) and no one killed.
