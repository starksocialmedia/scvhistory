## Method

- **Inventory.** Craft elements updated since 2026-09-26 (6,933 rows scanned, 208 with a matching phrase), scripts/import/*.php created or changed since 26 Sept (293 files), scripts/import/removed-claims.json, inventory/review/*.md. Phrases: no source, not found, has not been (found|seen|read), no record, nothing in the archive, unsourced, not established, no text, does not appear, no legacy page, uncited, no other source, only source, only tie, alone, no evidence, none found, no mention, no reliable source, in no source. Hits were read one by one; legacy prose (Leon Worden's captions, Perkins, Reynolds) and code messages were set aside.

- **Mirror.** Python: every .htm/.html/.xml/.txt file under /Volumes/Reggie/SCVHistory/scvhistory.com (84,849 files, flipbook basic-html pages and search XML included) read as bytes and decoded cp1252 with errors='replace', tags stripped, case-insensitive regex. Never the shell's `grep` wrapper. Most searches are proximity searches: name A within N characters of term B, so that a page naming the person and, elsewhere, a cemetery does not count. Per-note terms and scope are in the table and the JSON.

- **Craft.** Read-only query of `elements_sites.content` for every live element (temporary PHP under storage/, deleted afterwards) for the key phrases, and a map of legacyKey/legacyUrl to record ids for the sources cited below.

- Field: body, end of first paragraph
- Text: "That letter is his only tie to this valley in the archive's sources, and it may not be one: "the Santa Clara" may as well be the valley around San Jose, where the drive ended, and no source here places Couts in the Santa Clarita Valley."

- Draft replacement: Jerry Reynolds quotes the letter in his history of this valley, but "the Santa Clara" may as well be the valley around San Jose, where the drive ended.[1] Leon Worden writes that Couts's presence "incidentally was felt in the Santa Clarita Valley at one time or another," without saying when or how; no source here gives a dated act of his in the valley.[n] His Rancho Guajome, in San Diego County, was built about the time the del Valles built at Camulos, and the two later contended to be the "Home of Ramona."[n] (Footnote: Leon Worden, captions to the Lummis "Home of Ramona" pages, HS3001 to HS3016, and to LW3758. In Craft, the unsourced WordPress bodies of Edwin Bryant (#313) and the recordDates of Carson (#315) and Pico (#317) also use the name; they are not sources.
- Draft replacement: Folded into John C. Frémont (#307). Dr. Alan Pollack writes that Frémont's men were by then "referred to as the California Battalion" when they met Commodore Stockton at Monterey in 1846, and two environmental impact reports in the archive (NorthLake, 2017; Tesoro del Valle, 2018) say the battalion, under Frémont, "marched through the Santa Clara River Valley and south to Mission San Fernando." The battalion's story here is Frémont's march, which his record tells. (Whether the fold stands is Nathan's call; only the stated reason was wrong.)
