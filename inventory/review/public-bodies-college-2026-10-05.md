### Body

The Santa Clarita Community College District is the public body that runs College of the Canyons, the valley's community college. Its main campus is in Valencia, at 26455 Rockwell Canyon Road, and a second campus, on 70 acres at 17200 Sierra Highway in Canyon Country, opened for classes on August 27, 2007.[1][2][3] The district lies wholly within Los Angeles County and serves an area of 367 square miles.[2] A board of five trustees elected by the voters governs it. Since 2016 each trustee has been elected by the voters of one of five trustee areas of roughly equal population, and must live in that area; elections fall in November of even-numbered years.[4][17]

Valley voters approved a junior college district on November 21, 1967, and elected its first board the same day: William G. Bonelli Jr., Bruce Fortine, Sheila Dyer, Peter Huntsinger and Edward Muhl.[5] The board began to meet in December.[6] On the evening of Wednesday, December 13, it named the district the Santa Clarita Valley Junior College District, over objections, at a time when the valley itself had no official name.[7] The trustees named the college College of the Canyons on May 15, 1969.[8] "Valley" was later dropped from the district's name, and it took its present name in 1972, when California's junior colleges became community colleges.[9]

The college opened in the fall of 1969 with about 800 students, in the evenings, at Hart High School in Newhall, under Dr. Robert C. Rockwell, the district's first superintendent-president.[10] Voters approved a $4 million building bond in January 1970; the district bought 153.4 acres in Valencia, and the first classes there met in portable buildings on October 5, 1970.[11][12] A second bond, of $8 million, passed on February 6, 1973, and Governor Ronald Reagan dedicated the first permanent building, the Instructional Resource Center named for Bonelli, on April 22, 1974.[12] Bonelli had died in office in February 1972, and his seat was filled at a special election that June.[13] Dr. Dianne G. Van Hook became superintendent-president on July 1, 1988, and the college's later histories call her chancellor.[18]

For most of its history the trustees held numbered seats and were elected at large by every voter in the district, in odd-numbered years.[14][19] In 2015, to settle a lawsuit under the California Voting Rights Act brought by Jim Soliz and Rosemarie Sanchez-Fraser, who argued that at-large voting was a barrier to Latino participation, the board agreed to elect its trustees by area.[15] It adopted five trustee areas on November 4, 2015, and sent them to the Board of Governors of the California Community Colleges for approval in January 2016.[16] The same change moved the district's elections to even-numbered years, and the first election by area was held on November 8, 2016.[14][17]

### Editor notes

- **Dates that disagree.** The college's own history gives its opening day as September 22, 1969; The Signal of Friday, October 3, 1969 says classes "began Monday," which was September 29. The 45th-anniversary book's year-by-year page dates the naming of the district to December 15, 1967, and the naming of the college to May 21, 1968; The Signal, writing at the time, puts the first on Wednesday, December 13, 1967, and the second on Thursday, May 15, 1969, which the book's own narrative also gives. The district's board history lists John Hackney on the board in 1968; the college's histories say he replaced Sheila Dyer in 1969.
- **Appointed or elected.** The college's histories say Francis T. Claffey was appointed to Bonelli's seat in 1972. The Signal of June 23, 1972 reports that he won a special election, held because "State law now requires that vacancies be filled by election, not appointment."
- **Name.** In 1967 and 1969 The Signal called the valley "Valencia Valley," its editor's name for it, which did not last.
