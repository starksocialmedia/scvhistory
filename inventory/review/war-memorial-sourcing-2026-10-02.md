# Sourcing the war memorial records, 2 October 2026

## Where the records stand

There are 54 records: 1 World War I, 21 World War II, 6 Korea, 14 Vietnam and 12 War on Terror. Not one has a footnote. Their facts (service number, unit, dates, burial, awards) came from the legacy pages, and the legacy pages rarely say where they got them. Of the 40 legacy pages in the mirror:
- 4 name NARA;
- 3 name ABMC;
- 3 name the census;
- 2 quote the Newhall Signal.

The memorial's home page has a bibliography link but no sources of its own.

What the records already hold:
- **The legacy page**, with its narrative. This is a source in its own right and is never rewritten.
- **Documents among their images.** 10 of the 54 records have them: newspaper clippings on 8 records, draft cards on 4, service records on 3, an interment paper on 1 and a death certificate on 1.
- **Grave and memorial photographs** on 25 records. A headstone is a primary source for its names and dates.
- **ABMC readings** for Wingfield and Smart, saved today (`inventory/sources/abmc-war-memorial-2026-10-02.json`).

## What a sourced memorial record looks like

1. **Two independent sources for the death in service.** One official federal record that he died in service, and one source that ties him to the valley (his home of record, a newspaper, the census or a draft card at a valley address). This is the same two-source rule as for living people.
2. **Every fact field traced to a source.** Service number, rank, unit, dates of birth and death, place of loss, burial or memorial, and awards each cite the footnote they came from. Where two sources disagree, both are shown and the disagreement is noted, as on the Perkins and del Valle profiles. Nothing is silently chosen.
3. **The legacy narrative kept as written** and cited as the SCVHistory.com page it came from. Corrections go in a "Correction, 2026" editor note, as with the live errors. The first one is already in hand:
   - Wingfield is listed by ABMC as Missing in Action, commemorated on Manila's Walls of the Missing, not buried there as the legacy page's "Remains" line says.
4. **Documents linked as sources, not only shown as pictures.** A draft card or a clipping already attached becomes a footnote that points to the image.

## Where to source them from, by conflict

| Conflict | Official record of the death | Tie to the valley |
|---|---|---|
| World War I | ABMC, for overseas burials; *Soldiers of the Great War* (1920), California volume | the census; the Signal from 1919 |
| World War II | War Department, *World War II Honor List of Dead and Missing, State of California* (1946, NARA), listed by county with serial number and status; Navy, *State Summary of War Casualties* (1946); ABMC for overseas burials and the Walls of the Missing; NARA WWII Army Enlistment Records (AAD) | 1940 draft cards (4 held); 1940 census (3 held); Signal clippings |
| Korea | NARA Korean War Casualty File (AAD); ABMC Honolulu Courts of the Missing (Thomas and Kelly); DPAA | Signal clippings; the census |
| Vietnam | NARA Combat Area Casualties Current File (AAD); the Wall's panel and line (`wmWallReference`) | Signal clippings; school yearbooks |
| War on Terror | Department of Defense casualty releases (defense.gov, public domain), which give each man's unit and hometown | the Signal |

All of these are free and public. Each one is a new external source, and reading it needs your approval, as the live site did.

## Proposal

- **A pilot of five records, one from each conflict.** Read the sources above for each, footnote every fact, show disagreements, and leave the narrative untouched. You review the five, then the rest go in batches of about ten, each pushed when clean.
- **Start with what needs no new approval:** the documents already attached to 10 records, the 25 headstones, and the ABMC records.
- **ABMC turned out to hold no portraits.** Its records carry facts, not faces.
- **The 16 portraits need a different source.** None ever existed on scvhistory.com: on Leon's pages the image tag is commented out, a placeholder he never filled. Possible sources are the Signal's casualty notices, high school yearbooks, and families. Find a Grave photographs belong to the people who uploaded them, so each one needs that person's permission.
