Research-only audit, 2026-09-25 (times Europe/Berlin). Scratch: /workspace/plm (not in repo). No Craft/production/DNS/Archive.org changes; no git commits.

## Answer

The 47 lw2320.htm links aren't all wrong. Only **16** of them use the anchor "Noah Beery". The other 31 are William S. Hart sidebar thumbnails, and their captions correctly name Wallace Beery. Across the whole inventory (4,289 pages with links, 3,549 person-name links, 625 unique anchor→target pairs) there are exactly **2 different-person clusters, 31 links on 31 pages**. Both come from repeated boilerplate bio paragraphs, and both are "relative-of" errors:

1. "Noah Beery" → lw2320.htm (Wallace Beery photo): 16 pages. Source: the "About Charley Mack" boilerplate. No Noah Beery page exists on the site.
2. "Herman The Hermit" → vs3801.htm (Cliffie Stone photo page): 15 pages. Source: the Cliffie Stone bio boilerplate, where Herman is described as Cliffie's father. No Herman page exists. Confidence is medium because it could be read as "photo of his son".

A random live sample of 300 of the 5,358 un-inventoried pages found 0 more. Estimate for the uncrawled half: about 0 to a few dozen more links, most likely copies of the same boilerplate. It's a boilerplate pattern, not a site-wide problem.

## Method

1. Extracted every internal link from `links_out` in 15 legacy inventories (snapshot taken 2026-09-25 ~13:06 CEST), for 71,418 links in total. The label is the anchor text. For image links, the label is the thumbnail caption parsed from the body_html (`<font class="caption">`).
2. Person-name heuristic: 2–4 capitalised tokens, honorifics, initials, and Jr/Sr/III suffixes. Tokens were checked against the SSA given-name list and census surnames, and common English words, places, organisations and film words were excluded. A relaxed pass covered text anchors, and the top ~400 rejected anchors were reviewed by hand. `people_mentioned` was not used.
3. Target subject: taken from the title subject segment, the obituary subject (private obituary inventory), the lead/caption, and name mentions in the body (given-name compatibility, a nickname table, initials, suffix handling). Targets missing from the inventory were fetched live, with meta-refresh redirects followed.
4. Auto-classified a–e. Every c/c? and d candidate was hand-read on the target page text. Final verdicts are in overrides.py.
5. Live verification: all 121 source pages of the c/d links were re-fetched (~1 req/s). A link counts as verified when the live page has a link to the target path whose anchor text (or, for image links, whose caption) contains the name.

## Heuristic precision

- (c) auto-flag: 30 pairs flagged, 1 real (Noah Beery), so about 3% precision. The false positives were typos (Barlett/Bartlett, Kathyrn Barger), nicknames, maiden names, dropped Jr./III suffixes, author links, and a column index. Herman The Hermit was missed by the name detector because it's a stage name, and was found through the manual review of rejected anchors.
- (a) random sample of 50: 0 false negatives (95% upper bound about 6%). 3 of the 50 labels weren't people (a film, a Monogram set, an adobe), so the name detector is about 94% precise.

## Caveats / coverage

- 9,647 known URLs. 4,289 have `links_out` and were fully analysed. 5,358 (~55%) are title-only in the inventories. A random live sample of 300 of those (298 OK, 2×404) gave 318 person links and 0 true (c). Upper bound: about 1% of the ~5,700 estimated person links on those pages. Because the errors live in boilerplate, the realistic extra is 0 to a few dozen.
- Flipbook `/files/` excluded. Image links without a caption (alt="thumbnail", a/b/c series navigation) have no name label and weren't checked. Links whose label doesn't look like a name (e.g. "his father") weren't checked either.
- Stage names and nicknames are the known recall gap.
- The inventory snapshot was taken while another process was rewriting the inventory files.
- Suggested targets are given only where a page about that person was confirmed to exist (by title and page text). None exists for Noah Beery or Herman the Hermit, so for those the options are to unlink, or to create a page.
