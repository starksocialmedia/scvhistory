**[2]** Archaeologists tend to agree that while the Lopez find was the first documented discovery of gold in California and touched off the first gold rush, it was likely not the first time gold was found in the state (see, for example, Newhall Ranch: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 1996). Historian Charles F. Outland relays the story (in Mines, Murders and Grizzlies, 1969) of a party of about twenty men, led by one Santiago Feliciano, that left Mission San Fernando in 1820 on an expedition into Hasley Canyon where, approximately ten miles back, near Piru (in the area now known as San Feliciano Canyon), they were shown "how the Mexicans washed out gold" (pp. 72 ff.). There are also reports of a Canadian named Baptiste Ruelle finding gold near Mission San Fernando in 1841 (see Pioneers of California by Donovan Lewis, 1993).
